## 2. The harness (`scratch/pairtail/`)

- `pt_lists.py`: `scratch/mixed/eng2_local.py` loaded from source with two lines patched (the score dictionary goes
  through a hook; the diagnostic loop also ranks `base` and `pair`); the cached engine-2 records, rankers and key cache
  of the regime are reused. The hook is the gate above, the fit is `fit_lambda_rankers` with V44's parameters (three
  threads instead of all; `allow_pickle=False`). Every call asserts that weight 0 returns the blend's order. `--np 1`
  recomputes the logits with goooman's NP model first (`ENG_EXTRA_FPNET`), as `scratch/np_eng2/np_lists.py`.
- `pt_replay.py`: `scratch/np_eng2/np_replay.py` for any set of engine-2 arms (`pub`, `pt`, `np`, `nppt`): rebuilds the
  `nb/07` candidate groups per arm, `fusion_core.build_submission` through `scratch/fwd/replay.py`, per-type paired
  differences on all molecules and on those with finite logits, wins / losses, changed lists, exposure as in
  `scratch/combo/cm_replay.py` (joined first, answer lost first, joined rows in the first 25, a joined row above the
  answer; also on the finite subset), engine 2's own list MRR, the pair model's own rank of the answer.
- `pt_fwd.py`: ICEBERG / GLACIER for candidates that only the new lists bring in (GPU, under a minute in the np study).
- `pt_null.py`: sign-flip null on the equal-weight sum of the four type differences, 20,000 draws, all molecules and
  the finite subset.

Check [V]: the `pub` arm of `pt_replay.py` gives `c1` 0.8223, `c2` 0.7339, `dA` 0.5016, `dB` 0.3096 and exposure 25 /
8 / 9.23 / 30 on `c2`, 6 / 1 / 1.77 / 4 on `c1`: the numbers of `fwd-replay`, `combo` and `np-eng2`.

What carries over to the real stage, and what does not:
- **Carries over (mechanism)** [V]: the gate re-orders most lists (282 of 380 `c2` lists change in the first 25 after
  the fusion) but, with engine 2's first candidate locked, almost never the first row (6 lists), and exposure did not
  move at all in this run. The gate cannot gain on a molecule where engine 2 already has the answer first; its room is
  the answers at positions 2 to 40 of engine 2.
- **Does not carry over**: the sign and size of every difference. The stand-in loses exactly where the public
  fingerprint features are alive (finite logits), because it is blind to them; V44's ranker has fingerprint features.
  Whether it then helps or hurts there is the open measurement.

## 4. What the leak of the 380 molecules weakens (for the measurement to come)

- The public FPNets have very likely seen every one of the 380 answers. With finite logits the blend already ranks a
  leaked answer first far more often than it will on the hidden test (answer first in engine 2: 67% of the `c2`
  molecules with finite logits, 52% overall). A stage that only acts below rank 1 is therefore measured on fewer
  molecules here than it would act on there, and on an easier blend to beat or to damage.
- The pair ranker's own input is inflated the same way (section 1, last paragraph): on leaked answers its fingerprint
  features are out of the range it was trained on. The local replay will show its behaviour on memorised structures;
  the hidden test asks for its behaviour on unseen ones. megayak's README gives 0.689 → 0.710 for his ranker on
  enveda-np-examples with these rows [C], which is the leak-free number and is not ours.
- 58% of the local molecules have NaN logits (no collision energy): there the pair ranker runs on the non-fingerprint
  part of its 39 features only. The hidden test (Enveda timsTOF) presumably has collision energies, so the finite subset
  is the relevant one, and it is 158 `c2` / 70 `c1` molecules, 40 of them enveda-180 where engine 2 is already at 0.94.
- `dA` / `dB`: ICEBERG and GLACIER favour the leaked answer; any change in which pool rows engine 2 supports against
  joined rows is measured against forward scores that are stronger than on the hidden test. Exposure counts are more
  likely understated, as in the combo review.
- None of the 380 is a timsTOF natural product.
