# Engine-2 join (2026-10-04): engine-2 scores the PubChem candidates that the v1 engine joins

Question: if engine-2 scores the same 100 PubChem candidates that the v1 engine takes in (gate `lib_max` < 0.7), does
a joined answer collect its engine-2 share in the fusion without the joined decoys collecting it too?

Scripts: `scratch/eng2_join/` (`eng2_join.py`, `diag_summary.py`, `eval_eng2_join.py`, `LOG.md`). Data:
`D:\casmi-data\eng2_join\`. Nothing was submitted, pushed or committed; no existing file was edited.

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Mechanically yes.** A candidate given as SMILES gets every engine-2 feature; only the library features are 0, as
   for any COCONUT row without reference spectra.
2. **Engine-2 ranks a joined answer well**: rank 2 in the median, first for 49%, in its top 40 for 95% (40% and 90%
   with its fingerprint-model features zeroed).
3. **It does not hold the joined decoys down.** In a truth-in-pool list 29 of engine-2's 40 places go to joined
   decoys, a joined decoy is first in 31% of the lists (44% zeroed), and the pool answer loses the first place of the
   engine-2 list in 44 of 263 lists.
4. **After the fusion, against the current best (v13a):**

   | Replay | answer only in PubChem (`dB`) | only among engine-2's candidates (`dA`) | in the pool, no spectra (`c2`) | class 1 (`c1`) |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | zero-forward (325 / 325 / 380 / 150) | +0.074 ± 0.010 | −0.052 ± 0.009 | −0.032 ± 0.008 | −0.002 ± 0.006 |
   | ... fingerprint features zeroed | +0.065 ± 0.010 | −0.056 ± 0.009 | −0.028 ± 0.007 | 0.000 ± 0.005 |
   | ICEBERG prefix (145 / 145 / 169 / 64) | +0.044 ± 0.013 | −0.068 ± 0.015 | −0.034 ± 0.012 | −0.011 ± 0.010 |
   | ... fingerprint features zeroed | +0.044 ± 0.012 | −0.065 ± 0.015 | −0.043 ± 0.013 | −0.009 ± 0.008 |

   This is the trade of the blanket credit (`FUSE_INJ_CREDIT` 1.0 on the same molecules: +0.049 / −0.036), with a
   somewhat larger gain and the same cost. Exposure (zero-forward): a joined decoy gains rank in 263 of 380 `c2`
   lists; 24 correct top-1s are lost and 4 won.
5. **No drop-in was written and no submission is recommended.** The leaderboard punished exactly this exposure
   (N 200: 0.406, wider gate: 0.416).

## 1. Mechanism (step 1: GO)

**Engine-2's candidate set per molecule** (`nb/embed/eng/casmi_engine.py`). The pool is COCONUT + ChEBI / LIPID MAPS +
the training structures, sorted by mass, with packed 6,930-bit fingerprints. Candidates are all pool rows within
10 ppm of the target mass (30 ppm if none). The library search and the analog search (200 best analogs within 200 Da)
depend on the query only. Per candidate:

| Feature family | Source | For a joined SMILES |
|---|---|---|
| library (`lib`, `lib_mean`, `libsh`, `lib_cnt`) | library rows with the candidate's pool id | 0: no reference spectrum, as for a COCONUT-only row |
| analog propagation (two blocks) | Tanimoto of the candidate fingerprint to the analogs' fingerprints | computed from the fingerprint |
| fingerprint model | fingerprint · logits of the two FPNet models | computed from the fingerprint |
| MetFrag-lite (`frag*`) | fragment masses of the SMILES against the query peaks | computed from the SMILES |
| mass error | pool mass against the target | `ExactMolWt` |
| list-relative columns (rank, value minus list maximum, log of the list size) | the whole candidate list | change for every row when 100 rows are added |

The fingerprint is Morgan radius 2 (4,096) + Morgan radius 3 (4,096) + RDKit path (2,048) + MACCS, cut to
prvsiyan's 6,930 bits (`fp_bits.npy` next to `coco_fp.npy`). It is not the v1 selection of 10,226 bits, so the v1
records of `pc_join.build_records` cannot be reused. Nothing is undefined for a structure outside the tables.

Two rankers score the list (prvsiyan's 31 features with the fingerprint model, ours without), blended by rank
0.88 / 0.12; the top 40 by metric key go to the fusion.

**The wrapper** (`scratch/eng2_join/eng2_join.py`). Because the searches do not depend on the candidates, the cached
records of the mixed-regime study are extended with the joined rows and scored again; the 5.6 GB library is not
loaded. The joined set is the one the v1 engine got: the injected rows of the `*_n100_first` runs for the molecules
with `lib_max` < 0.7. A joined candidate whose key is already among engine-2's own candidates is dropped (0.4 per
list; 1.0 in `dA`, where engine-2 holds the answer itself). The reference lists rebuilt by this code equal the stored
engine-2 lists for 100% of the molecules of every type.

**What would have to move in the notebook.** `nb/03_engine2.py` runs before the PubChem channel (`04`) and before the
gate is decided (`06`, from the v1 engine's `lib_max`). Two ways:

- Move cell 03 behind cell 06 and let 06 dump the joined SMILES per molecule. Engine-2 (about 8 GB) then runs while
  the v1 engine (10.2 GB resident at N 100) is still loaded.
- Keep cell 03 and split `casmi_engine.main` after `compute_channels`: the first half dumps the records with their
  fingerprints, a second subprocess after cell 06 appends the joined rows and runs MetFrag-lite, the rankers and the
  lists. This is what the local wrapper does and it needs no library in the second half.

Engine-2 prefers a joined answer to the joined decoys, but not a pool answer to them. The joined rows are the 100
best of several thousand isomers by a fingerprint score, so they are selected for the very evidence engine-2 has in a
list without library hits (analog similarity and fingerprint logits), and its rankers were fitted on lists of about
50 pool rows. The 21 class-1 lists below the gate are the weak ones (answer first in 38% before the join).

## 4. Why not

- The task's condition was: clearly positive on `dB` and `c2` / `c1` not hurt. `dB` is positive in all four replays;
  `c2` loses 0.03 to 0.04 in all four, three to four errors from zero, and `dA` loses 0.05 to 0.07.
- Engine-2 separates a joined answer from joined decoys, not a pool answer from joined decoys. The support it gives is
  therefore not selective where the exposure is.
- The regime's own bias goes against the idea. Engine-2's fingerprint models have seen these molecules, which helps
  the joined answer; with those features zeroed the gain is smaller and the list-level cost larger. A hidden answer
  will be ranked worse than here, a hidden decoy no worse.
- The leaderboard has punished more exposure twice (N 200, the gate at 0.8) and each time harder than the regime
  showed. This change exposes every list below the gate through a second channel.

## 5. Runtime (for completeness; nothing was built)

Per joined candidate on one thread: fingerprint 1.7 ms, fragment masses 3.6 ms (26,031 structures, no failure). On
Kaggle the MetFrag-lite stage costs 7.2 ms per candidate with 4 workers (355 s for 48,976 candidates in
`runs/diag-n100`), the two rankers under 0.3 s per list. 100 joined rows add about 0.8 to 1 s to engine-2's 7 s per
molecule, for the molecules below the gate only: 4 to 7 minutes per 400 molecules, well inside the 9 h limit next to
today's 3 h 40 min. Runtime is not the obstacle.

## 6. What this cannot tell

- The molecules are training structures; "only in PubChem" hides a pool row. Levels are upper bounds.
- The mixture of types on the hidden test is unknown, and the regime's net numbers have mispredicted every change in
  how many outside candidates enter. No leaderboard estimate is given.
- `dA` is a construction (an answer that only engine-2 holds); its share of the hidden test is probably under 1%.
- GLACIER is missing; ICEBERG covers the 169-molecule prefix and has very likely seen the molecules.
- Only N 100 and the gate at 0.7 were run. A smaller N for engine-2 than for v1 was not tried.
- `INDEX.md` was not regenerated (no existing file may be edited): run `python tools/gen_index.py` when this review
  is committed.
