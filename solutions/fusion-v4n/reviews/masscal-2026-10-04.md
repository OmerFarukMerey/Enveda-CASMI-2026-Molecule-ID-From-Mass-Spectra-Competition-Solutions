## 0. Bottom line

1. **The offset is real, stable, and not an electron.** On the Enveda timsTOF libraries the neutral mass the pipeline
   computes is high by +2.2 ppm for [M+H]+ and +0.8 ppm for [M−H]− (other adducts 0 to +1.5 ppm). Both adduct tables
   handle the electron correctly. The two libraries agree within 0.2 ppm on the two main adducts; split halves agree;
   across mass the offset is closer to constant in ppm than in Da [T].
2. **A ±5 ppm window on the corrected target loses 0.2% (enveda-180) to 0.9% (enveda-np-examples) of the answers;
   ±3 ppm loses 0.7% and 7%.** 5 ppm is supported, 3 ppm is not [T].
3. **Most of the decoy load is not mass-separable.** In the pool and in engine-2's set 55 to 75% of the window rows
   share the answer's formula; in PubChem 40 to 49%. A 5 ppm window removes 15 to 28% of the pool and engine-2 rows and
   33 to 35% of the PubChem rows [C]. In the lists we submit it matters less still: of the rows ranked above the
   answer, 87 to 95% share its formula and a 5 ppm window would remove 0.5 to 4% of them [C]. The ranker already
   puts other-formula rows low.
4. **The pool window and engine-2's window: no gain.** 5 ppm in the main pool on top of the PubChem change: `c2`
   −0.004 ± 0.005, `c1` −0.005 ± 0.003 (0 / 5), `dB` no better [R]; it also shifts `lib_max` for 13% of the molecules.
   Engine-2 at 5 ppm: 0.000 on every type in the zero-forward replay [R].
5. **The PubChem channel: more answers enter, the exposure does not fall.** Real 5 ppm run: answer among the first
   25 / 50 / 100 candidates 59.1 / 65.2 / 70.5% → 62.5 / 69.8 / 74.8% [C], because the 5,000-row shortlist binds less
   often (the same loss `PC_N1=20000` addresses; 11 of the 18 answers gained are the same molecules). End to end:
   `dB` +0.011 ± 0.010, `c2` +0.003 ± 0.003 after the fusion and −0.004 ± 0.004 (9 / 17) on the v1 side, `c1`
   −0.003 ± 0.002 (0 / 3) [R]. Joined rows among the first 25 of a truth-in-pool list: 14.1 → 14.4; a joined row on
   top of the v1-side list: 13.1% → 15.0% [C]. **The window swaps other-formula joined rows for same-formula
   isomers; it is not a decoy reduction.**
6. **Recommendation**: leave `PPM_WIN` and engine-2 at 10 ppm. `PC_PPM=5` with `MASS_OFFSET_BY_ADDUCT='enveda'` is a
   coverage lever of the same kind as `PC_N1=20000`, smaller, free in run time, with the same pool-side risk sign as
   every change that hands harder outside candidates to the engine. Not a submission of its own; section 6.

## 1. The error model [T]

`s1_error_model.py`, `s1b_testlike.py`. Error = `chem.neutral_mass(precursor_mz, adduct)` minus the exact mass of
`molecular_formula`. 2,539,608 spectra; 0.01% have an adduct outside the pipeline's table.

**Electron.** `casmi/chem.py` builds every adduct as formula shift − sign × z × electron mass; `nb/embed/eng/pv.py`
uses PROTON = H − e, Na − e, Cl + e. Both are right. A missing electron would be +0.549 mDa on every positive adduct and
−0.549 mDa on every negative one; the measured offsets are +0.72 ([M+H]+), +0.34 ([M+Na]+), +0.08 ([M+K]+) and +0.26
([M−H]−): same sign in both polarities, different per adduct. It is a calibration property of the measurement. The
data's own `precursor_error_ppm` column is something else (median +0.98, correlation −0.12 with this error). Engine-2's
ranker already carries a feature `abs(ppm + 1.5)`.

## 5. Run time

Local, one worker, shared CPU, per molecule: pass 1 (ECFP4 of every window row) 1.5 → 1.0 s; pass 2 (full
fingerprints of the shortlist) 8.2 → 7.4 s; key walk 6.1 → 5.9 s; total 15.8 → 14.3 s. Pass-2 rows fall from 3,529
to 3,006 on average (−15%), on the timsTOF stratum from 4,988 to 4,568 (−8%; 72% of its windows still exceed
5,000). **About −10%, not the halving the 3 October review expected**; on Kaggle a few minutes. The saving becomes
real with a larger shortlist: with `PC_N1=20000` pass 2 is the window itself, 7,585 → 4,962 rows (−35%; timsTOF
stratum 13,137 → 8,803), so 5 ppm takes back about a third of that flag's +35 minutes. The engine and engine-2 are
unchanged.

## 6. Recommendation

- **Do not narrow the pool or engine-2 windows.** Measured, no gain; the pool window also perturbs the join gate.
- **`PC_PPM=5` + `MASS_OFFSET_BY_ADDUCT='enveda'` is a coverage lever, not a decoy lever.** It brings 4 points more
  answers into the first 100 PubChem candidates (count), keeps N at 100, and loses under 1% of the answers on the
  test's instrument. It does not lower the number of joined rows that reach the top of truth-in-pool lists; it
  makes them slightly harder. Given that the regime has underestimated exactly that cost every time, I would not
  spend a submission on it alone.
- **Where it fits**: with `PC_N1=20000` (config `v15b`). The two overlap (11 of the 13 pass-1 answers that 20,000
  recovers within 100 are also recovered by 5 ppm alone), 5 ppm adds 7 answers 20,000 does not, and it pays back a
  third of the extra run time. If `v15b` scores at or above v13a, add the two flags to it; if `v15b` scores below,
  the pool-side cost of harder joined rows is real on the leaderboard and this change should not be submitted.
  The combination itself was not run here.
- **3 ppm: no.** It loses 6 to 7% of the natural-product library's answers.

## 7. Limits, what failed, reproduce

- **The regime is mostly not the test's instrument**: 40 of its 380 molecules are timsTOF. The correction was
  applied to those only; for the other 340 the run is a plain 5 ppm window, and 7.9% of them lose the answer to
  instrument error that does not exist on the test. Rows "answer within 5 ppm" are the closer reading; the
  timsTOF-only rows have 39 to 40 molecules.
- The local FPNet is the fold-safe single model; engine-2's models and ICEBERG have seen the molecules; GLACIER is
  absent. enveda-np-examples could not be used end to end (its structures are training structures of every local
  model); it enters through the error model and the counts.

  different runs carry that noise (unbiased; their errors are wider than they need to be).
