## 3. Reading [I]

- **Where the wins come from.** In every promoting rule (A2, B1, C1), almost all wins are dA lists, where engine 2 holds the answer
  in its library. The losses are pool answers (c1, c2) and PubChem-only answers (dB), where engine 1's first row was right. Each
  rule is therefore a re-weighting toward engine 2 on a sub-set: the dA / dB-c2 trade of E2R2, now at rank 1. The arm difference
  says the same thing. With real fame, the PubChem promotion already puts more dA answers first (fewer disagreements, fewer
  dA wins), while the c1 / c2 losses stay the same.
- **B1 vs B2.** The two forward-model rules pass in opposite arms, and their wins come from different types (B1: dA; B2: dB, from
  restoring the displaced row). That is two halves of one class bet, not one robust signal.
- **Leakage.** ICEBERG and GLACIER have very probably been trained on spectra of the local molecules. Agreement of two leaked
  forward models with the right structure is the optimistic case. On the hidden test the forward evidence is weaker, and the
  rules' gain shrinks, while their c1 / c2 losses (engine 1 right, forward models wrong) are not leak-driven.
- **Engine 2's own probability (A2)** is the one input the chain never passes on (engine 2 hands over only ranks). It finds dA
  answers well (76 / 2 in arm m), but no threshold keeps c1 / dB losses within 2 SE.
- **Analog support (D1)** does not separate the two first rows: no threshold gave 2 : 1 on any training fold.
- **The month's lesson applies.** Local replays matched the leaderboard's sign about half the time. The closest candidates here pass
  in one arm only, depend on one class whose hidden share is unknown, and rest on leaked forward models. Building a submission from
  them would be a coin flip spent on the leaderboard.

## 6. The v72b probe (built on request after section 4; NOT a validated candidate)

**What it is.** stack1's notebook plus one appended last cell, `solutions/fusion-v4n/nb/embed/arb_v72.py` verbatim. B1 runs first: it
swaps ranks 1 and 2 when ICEBERG and GLACIER both score engine 2's top above rank 1. B2 runs only where B1 did not fire: it restores
the row as it was before E2R2 when both models score engine 2's top below the row E2R2 displaced. It uses only the ICE_SCORES /
GL_SCORES the chain already has. Safety:
- Switch `V72 = dict(b1, b2)`. Both False = the cell writes nothing, so the submission is stack1's byte for byte.
- A self-test on synthetic rows runs first. If it fails, the patch is off and the cell prints `V72_SELFTEST_FAILED`.
- Any error on one molecule keeps stack1's row for it (`V72_FALLBACK`). A file-level error keeps stack1's file (`V72_FAILED`).
- If E2R2 did not apply, the patch stays off.
- Log line: `V72_PATCH {b1_swaps, b2_undos, changed, changed_top1, fallback, ...}`.

Build and checks:
- Builder: `scratch/arb/build_v72.py` → `scratch/arb/sub_v72b/` (notebook, `kernel-metadata.json` for the private kernel
  `huseyinemreaksoy/casmi26-sub-v72b` with stack1's 14 datasets, README.txt).
- The builder asserts that every stack1 cell is unchanged and that every code cell parses.
- Tests: `solutions/fusion-v4n/tests/test_arb_v72.py`, 10 of 10 pass. They cover the branches, switch-off writes nothing, no rewrite
  without a change, per-molecule fallback, file-level restore, self-test failure → off, and the builder.
- The cell's own function reproduces the post-hoc lists on 3,186 of 3,186 replay lists (`scratch/arb/v72_check.py`).
- Not run on Kaggle; run time is seconds of CPU.

**Why it is a probe, not a candidate.** Each point is stated in its README too:
- The rule was assembled after seeing B1 and B2 pass in opposite arms.
- ICEBERG / GLACIER have very probably seen the local molecules.
- Its gains are one class bet (dA).

**Expected worst case on the public part** (about 124 molecules) [I, arithmetic]:
- **Asked-for formula:** touched share × 124 × local loss rate = 0.122 × 124 × 0.099 ≈ **1.5 losing molecules**. At 0.5 RR each, that
  is 0.75 / 124 ≈ **−0.006** (0.75 of one answer at rank 1). This holds if every local win is a leakage artefact and disappears.
- **Class-mix worst case:** the hidden lists behave like local pool-answer lists (c1 / c2, where engine 1 is usually right). Net per
  list is c2 −0.0042 (51 of 606 touched, 10 / 9) and c1 −0.0031 (12 of 376, 0 / 3), so about **−0.003 to −0.004**.
- **Bound nobody should expect:** every touched list loses a rank-1 answer: 0.122 × 0.5 = −0.061.
- **Upside at the local rates:** +0.004 (real fame) to +0.010 (fame masked), i.e. about half an answer to one answer.

The probe's likely range is therefore within one answer either way. A leaderboard result at that size cannot be told apart from the
noise of this month's single-change pairs.
