# The popularity prior without the libraries' own deposits (2026-10-08)

**First line: the split is clean, allowed, and almost inert.** Taking the spectral libraries' own substance records out
of the count changes the prior on 59,225 of 710,701 pool rows, changes 26 of 904 (panel) and 67 of 1,180 (mixed) final
top-25 lists, and moves the answer's rank on one list in each set. It does not cost anything locally, where it was
expected to lose by construction, and the "answer is not a library compound" reading gains nothing either. What the
split does establish is the size of the circularity: **2% of the prior's signal above chance on all natural-product
answers, 8% on the less famous half, 32% in the bottom quartile of substance counts**, and for low-count answers that
have no library deposit the prior is below chance with or without the split. `POP_SPLIT` is wired default-off with a
preset (`v53a`); it is a harmless, principled form of the prior, not a candidate with an expected gain.

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Provenance and rules** [V, then my reading]. The table holds counts of public PubChem records per InChIKey first
   block (PubChem FTP, 2026-10-01, CC0). `spec` is **one sum over four depositors**: MoNA 1,318,129 substance records,
   **NIST Mass Spectrometry Data Center 263,148 (16.4%)**, MassBank Europe 20,576, RIKEN NPDepo 1. The table **cannot
   separate them per structure**, so no "without NIST" version of `spec` exists. Counting PubChem substance records is
   within the rules as written (they forbid NIST spectra and models trained on NIST; this is neither, and it is free
   public data). The form used, `sid − spec`, counts no record deposited by NIST at all, which today's prior does.

**Rules verdict.** `docs/competition/rules-and-pitfalls.md` forbids "NIST spectra, or any model trained on NIST" (not
freely available; the licence restricts derivatives including weights) and our `AGENTS.md` forbids "NIST or other
non-free data or weights derived from it". The host added on 10-07 that a public dataset whose *annotations come from
matching against NIST* "would be a derivative of NIST" (745832; `docs/landscape/update-2026-10-08.md`).
- A count of PubChem substance records per depositor is **within these rules as written**: PubChem records are public
  domain and freely downloadable, the table contains no spectrum, peak or annotation, and nothing was trained. This is
  my reading of the text, not a host ruling.
- **Today's prior already counts NIST's records**: `pool_lsid.npy` is log1p of all substance records, the 263,148 of
  the NIST Mass Spectrometry Data Center and the 53,511 of the NIST Chemistry WebBook included.
- **Two versions as asked: not possible from this table.** `spec` is one number per structure; "with all library
  depositors" and "without NIST" cannot be built side by side. Rebuilding it would need `Substance/Extras/SID-Map.gz`
  (3.3 GB; not approved, not fetched).
- What was done instead. (a) The only quantity that enters the notebook is `sid − spec`: the count of records from
  depositors that are *not* one of the four. No record deposited by NIST is inside it; if NIST's count were wrong,
  only the subtraction would be affected. It is the more conservative of the two priors on this axis. (b) Quantities
  that use `spec` positively ("has a library deposit", `spec` alone) carry "is in the NIST library" for about a sixth
  of their mass. One could argue that this is the NIST library's compound list reaching the model, even though it is
  NIST's own public deposit. They are reported in section 3 as measurements and **are not shipped**. If such a flag is
  ever wanted, ask the host first or rebuild `spec` from MoNA and MassBank alone.

Reading [M, then H].
- **The circularity, measured.** The popmodel review bounded the deposit's share by taking one or two records from
  every train structure: 2% / 4% overall, 12% / 20% in the less famous half, 45% / 78% in the bottom quartile. With
  the depositors actually separated: **2%, 8%, 32%**. The bound was the right order and too pessimistic.
- **Answers without a deposit exist, and for them nothing is constructed.** 7,521 of the 23,328 (32%) have `spec = 0`
  (they are in GNPS, RIKEN, MS-DIAL and the other libraries that do not deposit in PubChem). For them the split can
  only help, and does: +0.0015 ± 0.0002 (282 groups up, 0 down), +0.0033 ± 0.0006 in the bottom quartile. That is
  the real size of "isomers were being lifted by their library deposits": three thousandths of a reciprocal rank.
- **The level is the news.** Bottom-quartile answers without a deposit: −0.019 ± 0.003 against chance (2,754 answers,
  median 10 records). Bottom-quartile answers with a deposit, deposit removed: +0.063 ± 0.004; under today's prior
  with only the answer's deposit removed: +0.037 ± 0.004. The difference between the two kinds of answer is not the
  deposit record; a compound that MoNA or NIST holds has more of every other kind of documentation too.
- **What this says about the hidden test** [H]. If a hidden answer is famous, the prior is intact and the split does
  not matter. If it is little documented and has no public spectrum (the data page's class 2), the nearest local
  stand-in is the bottom-quartile, no-deposit row: the prior ranks it slightly below chance, and the split moves it
  by +0.003. This is consistent with a stronger prior losing on the leaderboard (v44a, v46c; not yet confirmed after
  the rescoring, `docs/landscape/update-2026-10-08.md`) and it does not make the split a remedy.

## 5. What the local replay can decide, and what it cannot

- **It decides harm.** Library hits (`c1`, and `c1` with `lib_max` ≥ 0.9: 57 and 89 lists, difference 0.0000), pool
  answers that start first (0.0000), exposure (unchanged), the synthetic part of the mixed regime (0.0000): the split
  breaks nothing that the hidden class 1 also contains.
- **It was expected to show a loss by construction and shows none.** Every panel answer and two thirds of the mixed
  natural products lose part of their count while unlisted isomers keep theirs. The loss is one list of 2,084.
  This is informative in an unexpected direction: the lists are not sensitive to the deposit records at all.
- **It cannot decide a gain.** A gain needs an answer that has no deposit, is little documented, and sits next to
  isomers whose count is mostly deposits. Locally such answers exist only in the in-group sample of section 3.2
  (+0.0015 ± 0.0002, +0.0033 in the bottom quartile); in the replay sets the non-library construction gives 0.000.
  Whether the hidden test contains many such lists cannot be measured here. One hidden molecule is 0.0025 MRR; the
  local evidence puts the effect of this change below one molecule.
- **It says nothing about the leaderboard sign of the popularity weight.** That question (v44a, v46c, confounded with
  the scoring change of 10-07) is untouched by the split.
