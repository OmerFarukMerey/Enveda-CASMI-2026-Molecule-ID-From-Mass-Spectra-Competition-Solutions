# Where a joined answer loses its rank, and whether a targeted rule can lift it (2026-10-05)

Offline only. Nothing was submitted, pushed or committed, no Kaggle command was run, no existing file was edited, no
model was trained, the GPU was not used; one worker process at below-normal priority, every script under two minutes.
Scripts: `scratch/joinrank/` (git-ignored). Outputs: `D:\casmi-data\joinrank\` (under 30 MB). No prototype was added to
`prototypes/`: no rule survived (section 4).

**Every number below was computed in this study** from the cached engine rows of the join runs
(`D:\casmi-data\pcjoin\rows`, `D:\casmi-data\hardneg\join_ft_hn\rows`), unless a file is cited. MRR = MRR@25 on the
metric key; "±" is one standard error of a paired per-molecule difference; (wins / losses) in brackets. Setting: mixed
regime, 380 hold-out molecules, N 100, join gate `lib_max` < 0.7, public ranker, fusion config v4b; "new" = selection by
the fine-tune `ft_hn`, "cur" = selection by the fold-safe model. `dB` / `dA` are reported for the 325 molecules with a
PubChem record. Replays: zero-forward (all molecules) and the ICEBERG prefix (169 molecules, 118 with a record and an

## 0. Bottom line

1. **The better position inside the PubChem list is discarded at the join.** The ranker orders the joined rows with
   its own features and never sees the selection model's score. For the 157 answers that both selections list, the
   PubChem-list MRR rises 0.389 → 0.546 (+0.157 ± 0.024) and the joint list moves +0.015 ± 0.011, the final list
   +0.006 ± 0.010. The whole end-to-end gain of the fine-tune (`dB` final 0.256 → 0.280, +0.024 ± 0.008, 58 / 21)
   is the 20 newly listed answers (+0.018 of it); they sit deep in the list and end at 0.29.
2. **That is not a defect that can be repaired by passing the rank on.** Among the joined rows the ranker's order
   is much better than the selection model's (MRR of the answer among joined rows 0.710 against 0.506), and no
   combination of the two beats the ranker alone (best mixture −0.009 ± 0.012; cross-validated logistic +0.000 ± 0.004).
3. **Where the rank goes, joined answers of `dB` (177):** PubChem list 0.501 → among joined rows 0.708 → joint list
   0.566 → after the slot merge 0.559 → **after the engine-2 fusion 0.465**. Two losses of similar size: pool rows and
   joined decoys of the same formula above the answer in the joint list (each alone would leave about 0.70), and the
   fusion, which costs −0.094 per joined answer (6 better / 80 worse) because engine-2 cannot list the row. On `dA`
   (engine-2 lists the answer) the same step is +0.150. Before any of that, 76 of 253 answers in the first 100 are
   never joined (list at or above the gate): final MRR 0.098 where the PubChem list alone has 0.550.
4. **No row feature separates a joined answer from the joined decoy that tops a pool-answer list better than the
   ranker's own score gap** (AUC 0.883). Selection rank adds 0.01 to 0.02, agreement of the two selection models
   nothing (standardised coefficient 0.03). At equal exposure the selection rank puts 75 to 79 answers on top
   instead of 74 (of 177).
5. **Two rules, neither passes.** A selection-rank term never gains on `dB` (−0.054 to −0.005 after the fusion); as a pure penalty it
   lowers the exposure and costs `dB` 0.014 to 0.054. A bonus for rows both models place in their first k gains
   +0.001 to +0.018 on `dB` and raises the exposure at every setting (pool-answer lists with a joined row on top 43 →
   45 to 86 on the base list; first places lost 19 → 21 to 53 with engine-2 off).
6. **Recommendation: no.** Nothing here should be submitted. The ceiling of any exposure-neutral re-ordering among
   joined rows is +0.084 ± 0.012 on `dB` (oracle); the only signal that reaches a part of it is the best-analog
   similarity (+0.007 ± 0.003), a feature the regime inflates and the ranker already has.

## 4. Two rules

`s5_rules.py`, `s5_report.py`. Only joined rows change score; pool and generated rows keep the ranker's.

- **rank(w, r0)**: s' = s + w (ln r0 − ln rank in the fine-tune's list). r0 = 1 is a pure penalty: no joined row
  gains, so the exposure cannot rise. r0 > 1 lifts the first r0 − 1 rows and lowers the deeper ones.
- **agree(k, b)**: s' = s + b for joined rows that both selection models place in their first k.

- **rank**: no setting gains on `dB`. The pure penalty (r0 = 1) is the only family that lowers the exposure (joined
  row on top 43 → 36 / 30 / 22, first places lost with engine-2 off 19 → 14 / 11 / 11) and it pays for that with
  −0.014 / −0.026 / −0.054 on `dB`: it moves along the same gain-for-exposure line as a shallower join, it does not
  leave it. With r0 > 1 the exposure rises and `dB` still does not gain, because the penalty re-orders the joined
  rows by a signal that is worse than the ranker's (section 3).
- **agree**: every setting raises the exposure; the smallest one (k 1, b 0.25) adds two exposed lists and two lost
  first places for +0.001 ± 0.002 on `dB`. With engine-2 off, what `dB` gains `c2` loses (k 10, b 0.5: +0.021 against

- The best "rule" by the letter of the conditions is the cross-validated logistic re-ordering among joined rows of
  section 3 (`dB` +0.007 ± 0.003, `dA` +0.005 ± 0.002, `c2` and `c1` 0, exposure unchanged). It is not proposed: its

  one eleventh of the ceiling.

## 5. What this says about the open question

- A joined answer does not lose its rank at one stage that a rule can reach. The ranker places it first among the
  joined rows for 59% (the same structure as a pool row scores 0.9 higher, through the order-coded ties and the
  list-relative features: `pcjoin-2026-10-02.md` section 3.1, not re-measured here); it then loses to same-formula
  pool rows and same-formula joined decoys in equal parts, and again in the fusion when engine-2 does not know it.
- The fine-tune's value is coverage, not rank: it brings 20 more answers into the 100 here. Its better order
  inside the list is unused and, tested directly, unusable next to the ranker.
- The one stage with a large, one-sided loss is the engine-2 fusion on `dB` (−0.094 per joined answer; `dA` +0.150).
  The row-level answer to it (credit for joined rows) is closed. A list-level answer would need a signal that says
  "engine-2 does not have this molecule's answer"; none of the features here is that signal, and
  `eng2-join-2026-10-04.md` closed engine-2 scoring of joined rows.
- ICEBERG's gap to the best scored pool row is the strongest separator against harmful decoys in the table (0.87 /
  0.85), on 60 answers and 57 decoys with cached scores from a model that has seen the molecules. The pipeline
  already applies ICEBERG twice, and this sample cannot say whether a third, gap-shaped use would hold on molecules
  ICEBERG has not seen. It is recorded, not proposed.

## 6. Limits, and what failed or could not be verified

- **ICEBERG / GLACIER stage.** Only cached ICEBERG scores were used: 139 molecules, 27% of the fine-tune's joined
  rows, 59% of the answers' same-formula competitors. No GLACIER scores and no Kaggle stage dumps exist locally. The
  ICEBERG columns are therefore a partial replay, not the notebook's stage. The GPU was not used to fill the gap.
- **"Public A+B list".** A and B were trained on the regime's molecules; the agreement feature uses the fold-safe
  model's list as the second selection model (`ft_hn` was fine-tuned from it, as the production checkpoints would be
  from A and B). The two local models are therefore more alike than `prod_hn` and A+B need be.
- **The engine's own fingerprint model is the fold-safe one** here and A+B on Kaggle; in both cases it is not the
  selection model, which is the property that matters for section 0, item 1.
- Hold-out answers are masked training structures with library relatives; engine-2 and ICEBERG have seen them.
  `best_tan` and everything engine-2 repairs are inflated by that.
- Rule settings were read on the lists they are evaluated on (no rule was selected, so nothing was tuned in the end).
  The thresholds of `s4_toprow.py` are set on the same c2 / c1 lists whose exposure they match.
- 177 joined answers, 49 exposed pool lists, 66 joined answers on the ICEBERG prefix: differences below about 0.01 on
  `dB` and below three lists of exposure are not resolved.
- `s4_toprow.py` prints 0 answers for the rank-only scorers at matched exposure: ties at rank 1 make the threshold
  degenerate; those rows are not reported above.
- The hardneg review's local numbers (0.307 → 0.335 before the fusion) are reproduced here as 0.308 → 0.334 after
  stage A; after the whole fusion the same comparison is 0.256 → 0.280.
- `INDEX.md` was not regenerated (an existing file): run `python tools/gen_index.py` when this review is committed.
