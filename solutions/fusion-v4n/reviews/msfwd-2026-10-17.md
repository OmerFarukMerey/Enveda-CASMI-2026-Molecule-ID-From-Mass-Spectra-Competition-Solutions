## 0. Bottom line

- **stack1 already scores each [M+H]+ spectrum separately at its own collision energy and averages the similarities.** "Per-CE
  prediction matched to each measured spectrum, aggregated" and "every [M+H]+ spectrum instead of one merged one" are what the
  runners do today. What stack1 does **not** use: [M+Na]+ for GLACIER, ICEBERG on the final list (except as gate / fallback), and
  every [M+NH4]+, water-loss, [M+K]+ and negative spectrum (only the MetFrag-lite gap branch sees them, and only where no
  simulator scores the molecule).
- **All three pre-registered rules fail** (`scratch/msfwd/PREREG.md`, written before any outcome; addendum on the pool-answer split
  added before any outcome). None reaches +0.0133 composite in either arm; the best is +0.0013.
- R2 (geometric mean) is clearly harmful on answer-removed lists (dA / dB −0.013 ± 0.003, 15 / 88). R1 and R3 are within noise;
  R3 gains slightly on the timsTOF panel's pool answers (+0.003 ± 0.002, 10 / 2), far below one public answer.
- **No patch.** Runtime would not have been the obstacle (R3 about +2 to +3 min, R1 about +1 to +2 min on a T4 for ~430 molecules).

## 1. What stack1 does with a molecule's spectra [V code]

Sources: `scratch/comply/stack1_src/cell22.py`, `cell26.py`, `cell30.py`, `cell33.py`, `cell34.py`, `cell36.py`; runners
`casmi26-iceberg/ice_runner.py`, `fuse.py`, `casmi26-glacier/gl_runner.py`, `gl_fuse.py` (local copies under `D:\casmi-data\coverage_study`).

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Runner input (cell 22, `fuse.build_ice_input(te, cands)`, `covered_only=True`) | Per molecule: every **positive-mode spectrum labelled [M+H]+ or [M+Na]+**, unmerged, with its own peaks, precursor, instrument and `collision_energy_ev`. All other adducts and all negative spectra are dropped here (and again inside both runners, `is_covered`). |
| ICEBERG (cell 22, `run_ice`) | One prediction per spectrum: adduct as labelled, instrument token Orbitrap / QTOF, energy = mean of `collision_energy_ev` rounded to 5 eV (min 5; NCE units: mean of the stated numbers); no energy → predictions at 20, 40, 60 eV merged. Score per candidate = **unweighted mean of the entropy similarity over the covered spectra** (`ice_runner.score_molecule`). |
| GLACIER (cell 22, `run_gl`, v29) | The same items **filtered to [M+H]+ spectra** (`gl_items`); same per-spectrum prediction and mean. Molecules without an [M+H]+ spectrum get no GLACIER score. |
| Candidates scored | `BASE_ICE = False`, so engine-1 base groups are not sent by themselves; `ICE_UNION` sends members of same-formula groups over engine 1's top 60 + engine 2's top 40, plus the PubChem channel's 25 when lib_max < 0.9 (`ICE_PC`). |
| Base list (cell 22) | Not re-ranked (`BASE_ICE = False`). The PubChem channel's list is re-ranked by z(f.z) + z(ICE) + z(GL) inside formula groups (lambda 1); the promotion gate keeps the original top f.z. |
| Cell 26 (`fuse2`) | GLACIER only (`CELL19_ICE = False`); its output is later rebuilt by cells 30 / 34 and only survives as the E004 control tail. |
| Final list (cell 34, window 40 = metric fusion of PREFUSION list and engine 2) | If the molecule has an ICE dict **and** GLACIER scores: **GLACIER only** (`rerank_multi([{}, gl])`, ICE ignored). Else if no finite ICE score: frag-gap (MetFrag-lite over the first 16 spectra of **any** adduct and polarity, lambda 0.5). Else (only [M+Na]+ covered): ICEBERG only. Then cell 36's E2R2. |
| Molecules with no simulator score | No positive [M+H]+ / [M+Na]+ spectrum: locally 66-74 of ~325-380 per list type in the mixed set (~20%) and 28 of 226 on the panel (12%); ICE-only: 9-13 and 4 [V `scratch/msfwd/inventory.py`]. |

Spectra per molecule [V inventory]: molecules with [M+H]+ have 2.7 [M+H]+ spectra on average (67% of mixed, 51% of panel
molecules have two or more); stepped [M+H]+ spectra 114 / 789 (mixed), 175 / 569 (panel); mixed [M+H]+ spectra without energy 271.
So "4+ spectra score no better" is not because the forward stage looks at one spectrum: it already averages all [M+H]+ spectra; the
extra spectra of such molecules are mostly negative / other adducts, which no simulator sees. Earlier work on other chains showed
the obvious aggregates do not help (`fwdmech-2026-10-06.md`: best spectrum, peak-count mean, [M+H]+ only, single-energy only),
negative mode against the 2 H-shifted [M+H]+ prediction is at chance and a frag term on every molecule cost 0.014 on the LB
(`coverage-2026-10-02.md`, `EXPERIMENTS.md`), so those were not re-registered here.

## 2. Rules and harness [V]

- **R1 SUR-GAP**: molecules with no [M+H]+ but an [M+NH4]+ / [M-H2O+H]+ / [M-2H2O+H]+ spectrum (frag-gap or ICE-fallback branch)
  get a GLACIER column from those spectra rewritten by `nb/embed/surrogate.rewrite` and take the GLACIER branch. Base B*.
- **R2 GEO**: GLACIER-branch molecules with ≥ 2 [M+H]+ spectra: column = mean of ln(sim + 0.02) instead of the mean of sim. Base B'.
- **R3 STEP**: GLACIER-branch molecules with a stepped [M+H]+ spectrum: that spectrum is compared with the merge of predictions at
  each of its own 5-eV buckets (the runner's `merge`) instead of one prediction at the mean energy. Base B'.
- Harness: the arb capture of the stack1-like replay (606 molecules, 2,084 lists per arm) with stack1err's B* (frag-gap
  reproduced). GLACIER re-run with the package runner unchanged, wrapped (`msf_runner.py`) to write each spectrum's similarity to
  the runner's own prediction and to the stepped merge: 483 molecules, 19,051 candidates, 0 failed predictions, plus 37
  surrogate molecules. **Checks**: cell 34's GLACIER branch rebuilt from the captured scores reproduces stack1's list in 2,534 /
  2,534 lists, the ICE-fallback branch in 42 / 42, the frag-gap base in 154 / 154; no candidate lacked a new score. **B'** (same
  construction with this run's means) vs B*: 426 lists re-ordered (2 first rows) by float-level batch differences (new mean vs
  cached differs < 0.01), composite −0.0002 / −0.0001 (1 / 6 lists): R2 and R3 are therefore compared with B', R1 with B*.
  B* composite 0.5664 (m), 0.6408 (u).

Readings [I]:
- **R1**: the surrogate GLACIER column is about as good as the MetFrag-lite / ICE evidence it replaces: it moves the first row in
  46 lists and wins about as often as it loses; the pool-answer lists lose slightly (8 / 10). Same picture as the v24a surrogate
  study (`anatomy-2026-10-06.md`). The segment is 6% of molecules, so even a strong effect would be under one public answer.
- **R2**: requiring all spectra to agree punishes the answer whenever one of its spectra is poorly predicted (weak or partly
  unfragmented spectra, `fwdmech` section 3); the arithmetic mean is the more robust aggregate. Its slight pool gain in the mixed set
  (+0.0056 ± 0.0034) does not hold on the panel (−0.0028).
- **R3**: physically the right model of a stepped spectrum, and the only rule positive on the timsTOF panel's pool answers
  (+0.0031 ± 0.0023, 10 / 2, about 1.3 SE), where the forward models are least leaked; on everything else it is zero. Size
  arithmetic: even if the whole panel effect transferred, 0.6 × 0.003 ≈ +0.002 public, a quarter of one answer.
- Forward models have probably seen most local molecules (MassSpecGym), so every local isomer gain here is optimistic; none was
  large enough for that to matter.

## 4. Runtime [V `msf_runtime.py`, `runtime.txt`; stack1's Kaggle log `scratch/nb1016/log_omer_text.txt`]

stack1's GLACIER on Kaggle: 1,524 predictions for 11 visible molecules at 83.9 predictions/s on the T4 (ICEBERG 38.3 /s). Extra
predictions on the replay, relative to stack1's GLACIER jobs for the same candidates (87 per molecule): R3 +21% (18 per molecule),

molecule): R3 ≈ +12,500 predictions ≈ **+2.5 min**, R1 ≈ +1 to +2 min, R2 0. All far inside the 9 h limit; runtime is not the
constraint.

## 5. Recommendation

Do not build a patch from this study. The simulator stage already uses every [M+H]+ spectrum at its own energy; the spectra it
does not use cannot be served by the two positive-mode checkpoints in a way that helps (surrogate here and on v24a; negative mode at
chance earlier). The remaining isomer gain would need a forward model that covers negative mode and [M+NH4]+ natively (a model
trained by us on permitted data, `coverage-2026-10-02.md` section 2.4), not a re-aggregation. If R3 is wanted for physical
correctness it is cheap and neutral locally, but it is not a leaderboard candidate on this evidence.
