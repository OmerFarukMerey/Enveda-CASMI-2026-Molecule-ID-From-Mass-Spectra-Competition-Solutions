**Two facts about the code that change how the brief's question has to be put.**
- **The prior does not handicap joined rows directly.** `popularity_reorder` re-sorts pool rows among the pool slots;
  joined rows (pool id −2) keep their slots and get a `|gen` formula tag, so in the stage-A forward re-rank they form
  their own group and are never compared with pool isomers. A joined row can pass a pool row only later (PubChem merge,
  engine fusion, post-fusion re-rank). There is therefore no existing flag that "removes the prior where it handicaps
  joined rows": the nearest form is "no prior on molecules that have joined rows", i.e. below the join gate. That needs
  one line (`pop_on = ... and not (c['POP_MIN_LIB'] is not None and lib_max < c['POP_MIN_LIB'])`, plus passing weight 0
  instead of skipping the call if the tags are to stay). Not made; replayed as g07 / g07n in a scratch copy.
- **`POP_MU` 0 is not "prior off".** At exactly 0 `popularity_reorder` is skipped, so the joined rows also lose their
  tag and compete with pool isomers in stage A. a000 against a000t isolates that: joined answers that start at base
  rank 2 to 10 gain +0.055 ± 0.020 (15 / 2, panel) and +0.050 ± 0.021 (23 / 11, mixed NP) with the tag dropped, against
  +0.007 ± 0.008 and +0.004 ± 0.004 with the tag kept; the exposure rises (mixed 28 / 6 → 33 / 11) and mixed `c2`
  falls by a further 0.012. The "`POP_MU` 0" rows of the popmu and fame reviews contain this second change.

## 5. The two readings

- **(a) "The optimum on today's chain is below 0.25; lowering gains."** For: the leaderboard is monotone downwards
  over 0.25 → 0.35 → 0.35 + in-group; the hidden answers are less famous than any local set (fame review, level
  argument); in the masked emulation lowering gains what raising loses. Against: every real local cut loses at every
  lower value, including the least famous terciles, the lists below the join gate and the joined answers; the chain
  comparison shows no downward shift; and the emulation's premise contradicts the earlier +0.005 for 0.15 → 0.25.
- **(b) "The response is not symmetric: 0.35 hurts, and 0.15 would also lose."** For: the local dose response (0.15:
  panel −0.0043 ± 0.0017, mixed NP −0.0039 ± 0.0018; 0.20: −0.0029 ± 0.0012, −0.0011 ± 0.0010) and the earlier
  leaderboard step, which have the same sign; an interior optimum near 0.25 is what two losses on either side look
  like. Against: the local replay has the wrong sign or none for 0.35 itself (+0.0035 / +0.0008 against −0.010), so its
  verdict on 0.15 has no demonstrated validity on this axis; the earlier leaderboard step was on another chain and is
  1.3 answers.

**The local evidence favours (b) and cannot decide.** It favours (b) only in the weak sense that nothing local
supports (a) apart from a construction. It cannot decide because the one thing it would have to get right, the sign at
0.35, it got wrong. What the leaderboard itself says about 0.15 on today's chain is nothing: there is one point above
0.25 (two, not independent: v46c contains v44a) and none below. The fame review put the standard deviation of one such
leaderboard difference at about 0.007 (`reviews/fame-2026-10-08.md` section 4), so −0.010 is about one and a half
standard deviations from zero: a real signal, not a precise slope.

## 6. Preset

**None written.** The rule required a lower value with no cell beyond −1 SE locally and a gain on the stratum that
reproduces the leaderboard's sign. No lower value is neutral (0.20, the mildest, is −0.0029 ± 0.0012 on the panel with
`c2` 1 gained / 12 lost), and the only stratum where lowering gains is the emulation. `tests/test_config_keys.py` and
`build_notebook.py` were not run. A submission of 0.20 or 0.15 would be a probe of reading (a) against the local
evidence, not a locally supported candidate; under the standing rule it is not proposed.
