# A better fingerprint bank for the main engine? (2026-10-06)

Offline only. Nothing was submitted, pushed, committed, uploaded or downloaded; the only `kaggle` commands were four
read-only file listings; no model was trained; the visible `test.parquet` was not read; no NIST data. No file of the
solution tree was changed except this review (`INDEX.md` not regenerated: two other agents were adding files).
GPU: inference only (FPNet logits under 1 GB, GLACIER 0.42 GB, ICEBERG 0.7 GB, one at a time, started only while the
other processes held less than 8 GB). CPU: three v1-engine workers plus one other process, below-normal priority.
Scripts: `scratch/fpbank/` (git-ignored). Outputs: `D:\casmi-data\fpbank\` (all new; other caches were read or copied).

**Every number below was computed in this study** unless a file is named. MRR = MRR@25 on the metric key; "±" = one
standard error of a paired per-molecule difference; (wins / losses) in brackets. **No leaderboard estimate is given.**
"Seen" = the molecule's structure was in the model's training data; a comparison on seen molecules favours the
memorised public models and says nothing about the hidden test.

## 0. Bottom line

1. **No bank passes the rule** (better beyond two standard errors on one type for fold-safe-evaluated molecules, no
   type lost beyond one, exposure not up by 5 lists). Four banks went through the full v24a replay; no type gains
   beyond 1.6 standard errors anywhere, and every bank loses `c1` beyond one standard error wherever it was run.
   **No `FP_BANK` value, no `PC_MODELS` value, no preset and no dataset folder were prepared.**
2. **`fpnet_full1` is not on this machine** (198.8 MB on Kaggle, one file). It was not downloaded: this task had no
   user approval for a download. Everything said about "the production bank" uses public model A as its stand-in, as
   the panel study did. This is the largest gap of the study.
3. **The fine-tune `ft_hn` is the best unseen model we have, on the Enveda panel only**: fingerprint score alone,
   answer in the pool window, MRR 0.541 (fold-safe) → 0.588 (+0.047 ± 0.014); on the mixed regime −0.004 ± 0.009.
   Inside the engine that gain does not arrive: `dA` +0.006 to +0.008 (about one standard error), `c1` and `c2` down.
4. **Logit scales differ by a factor of 20**: std 19.0 (A), 20.3 (B), 1.55 (fold-safe), 0.86 (`ft_hn`), 0.14 / 0.12
   (the destroyed production fine-tunes). A plain average of a public model and `ft_hn` is the public model.
5. **Combination rule**: temperature matching (one constant per model) is the only logit-level rule that does not
   hurt; z-scoring and quantile matching lose 0.02 to 0.19 MRR because they remove the logits' mean, which carries
   the bit prior. Rank averaging of the candidates' scores is the best rule (+0.019 and +0.032 for A+B against A) but
   works on scores, not logits, so it cannot be a bank without changing the engine's feature code.
6. **Temperature matching does not make the ranker's inputs comparable when the reference is a public model**:
   with A + `ft_hn` at equal weight the `fz_gap` features keep 0.71 of their spread and `fp_ll` moves by 2.7 standard
   deviations; that bank loses `dA` by 0.022 ± 0.008 on the panel. Feature drift is the mechanism, as feared.
7. **PubChem channel**: A+B with `ft_hn` at weight 0.3 (temperature-matched) holds the panel answer in the first 25
   for 83.2% instead of 80.0% (+0.032 ± 0.014) and in the first 100 for 92.4% instead of 92.0% (+0.004 ± 0.007); on
   the seen regime molecules it costs 0.009 to 0.012. Its exposure was not replayed, so it is not a candidate.
