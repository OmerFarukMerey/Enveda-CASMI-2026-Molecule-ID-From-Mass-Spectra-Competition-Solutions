1. **The benchmark exists and is fixed**: 5,350 molecules that no fold-safe model has seen, **DEV 2,675 / FINAL 2,675**,
   no generic Murcko scaffold in both halves. One function, `bench.evaluate(scores_parquet, baseline, half,
   as_feature)`, returns the table with paired differences; `as_feature=True` refits the ranker out of fold by scaffold
   with the score as four extra inputs, alone and inside the rank average with the public ranker (v33b's base list).
2. **Leak-safety is asserted in code and holds**: 220,443 training structures, 55,367 held out, **0 identifiers shared**
   (InChIKey first block, metric key, raw key) between any training positive and any held-out structure or benchmark
   answer; 0 of the 19,258 benchmark spectra belong to a training structure.
3. **Negatives**: 98.998% of the training structures have at least one same-formula negative (93.2% in the pool, median
   30 pool isomers; the PubChem cache of `mine_pc_negatives.py` adds 16 hard + 32 random rows per structure, median 26
   of them same-formula). 99.5% have at least 15 negatives available, median 78.
4. **Throughput** (RTX 4070 Ti SUPER, mixed precision, batch 256 molecules × 15 negatives, 5.1 M-parameter spectrum
   encoder): 5,650 spectra/s = 2,460 molecules/s in the model, **7.2 steps/s at duty 0.8** in a real run; one pass over
   the 220,443 training molecules takes 2.0 min, all 1.98 M spectra 7.7 min. The loader is not the limit for the

6. **The loop works end to end** (10-minute smoke run → scores for 435,268 pairs in 3 s → `evaluate`). Its score is
   not a result.
7. **Not built**: a cross-encoder; a graph cache; anything on Kaggle; FINAL-half fingerprints. torch-geometric and DGL

A batch: B molecules, 1 to 4 of each molecule's spectra (all are available through `JointData.spectra_of`), tokens =
m/z and intensity of the 96 most intense peaks, plus precursor m/z, adduct index, polarity, mean collision energy with
a known flag, instrument family; the true structure (pool row, packed 10,226-bit fingerprint; SMILES and graph for the
GNN); K negatives with their kind. Deterministic in (seed, batch number), so a resumed run continues the same stream.

Decisions that matter later:
- **Held-out structures can be drawn as negatives** (default). On the hidden test a class-2 answer is a pool row and
  is drawn as a negative of other spectra; excluding the benchmark's answers from the negatives would give them an
  advantage the hidden answers do not have. `neg_hold=False` exists. Which one matches the leaderboard better is open.
- The instrument "type" is the six families of `casmi.build.instr_family`; collision energy is `ce_mean` of the cache.
  The string-to-index maps for a Kaggle notebook (adduct, instrument) live in the `casmi` package, not in this folder.

Missing or not reused: nothing is missing for the training structures. `hardneg/w10_hold` (negatives of the held-out
structures) is not needed. The cache holds 48 rows per structure, not the window: more or different PubChem negatives
mean a new 2.6-hour mining run.

## 3. Reference model and throughput (`train/joint/model.py`, `train_joint.py`)

Spectrum encoder: 6-layer transformer, width 256, 8 heads, one token per peak (Fourier features of m/z and of the
neutral loss) + a metadata token; 5,075,712 parameters. Structure encoders: MLP 10,226 → 1,024 → 512 → 256 over the
pool's fingerprint (11,128,576 parameters) or a 5-layer message-passing network (1,533,696). InfoNCE over the batch's
positives + the molecule's own K negatives (271 candidates per row at batch 256), plus the reverse direction; float16
autocast. Engine 2's 6,930-bit fingerprint was not used: the pool's 10,226 bits exist on disk for every pool row and
every cached PubChem negative.

The 10-minute run confirms the fingerprint row: 4,325 steps in 603 s = **7.2 steps/s, 4,300 spectra/s, 1,840
molecules/s** at duty 0.8. Throughput does not grow with the batch; memory does (about 8 MB per spectrum), so a larger
contrastive batch needs fewer spectra per molecule, fewer tokens or gradient caching, not a bigger card setting.

**Time per epoch** (fingerprint variant, batch 256): one pass over the 220,443 training molecules = 861 steps = 2.0 min
at duty 0.8 (1.5 min unthrottled); all 1,982,405 training spectra once = 7.7 min at duty 0.8 (5.9 min unthrottled).

**Data loading** (4 workers, CPU only):
- Fingerprint variant: 78 batches of 256 per second (20,000 molecules/s), ten times what the GPU takes. Condition: the
  two fingerprint tables (0.85 + 4.0 GB) are in the OS file cache. `data.warm()` reads them once (1.4 s). Cold, a random
  row costs 0.3 ms and a batch of 256 × 16 structures 0.42 s per worker; when the machine has no spare RAM for 4.9 GB
  of standby pages the run becomes loader-bound.
- GNN variant: 4.7 batches of 128 per second = **600 molecules/s** (0.28 ms of RDKit per graph on one core, 26 atoms on
  average, 2,048 graphs per batch). The model alone would take 940 molecules/s at duty 0.8. A graph cache for the
  710,701 pool rows and 3,377,070 PubChem rows was not built (estimated 3 GB; D: cannot hold it today).
- 54% of the token slots are padding (44.5 real peaks per spectrum under the cap of 96). Length bucketing was not
  implemented; it would cut the transformer's work, by how much was not measured.

**Inference** (GPU only, autocast): spectrum encoder 24,000 spectra/s; fingerprint MLP 1.84 M candidates/s; GNN 66,000
candidates/s. Scoring DEV end to end with the smoke checkpoint: 2,515 molecules, 9,307 spectra, 371,698 distinct
candidates, 435,268 pairs in 2.0 s of encoder time (3.2 s of GPU wall time, 6 s for the process), fingerprints given.
The fingerprints are the cost: 2.55 ms per candidate on one core (206,601 non-pool DEV candidates took about 4 min with
3 workers). Graphs: 0.28 ms per candidate.

**Estimate for the Kaggle notebook** (assumption: a T4 runs at one third of this card; not measured):

| case | candidates | GPU, fingerprint MLP | GPU, GNN | CPU featurisation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| the ranker's lists (about 170 per molecule, the stored lists' median) | 68,000 | < 1 s | 3 s | fingerprints 3 min on one core; graphs 20 s |
| 400 molecules × 3,000 candidates | 1.2 M | 2 s | 55 s | fingerprints 51 min on one core (13 min on 4, if Kaggle's cores match ours: not known); graphs 5.6 min on one core |

The 1,500 spectra take under a second. The GPU is never the constraint; with a few thousand candidates per molecule
the fingerprint variant is outside "a few minutes" unless it reuses fingerprints the pipeline already computes for the
PubChem channel (not checked), and the GNN variant is inside.

## 5. Time and feasibility (fingerprint variant, batch 256, K 15; from the measured 7.2 steps/s at duty 0.8 and 9.6 unthrottled)

Assumptions, not measurements: a full run = 100 passes over the training molecules (no convergence study was made);
a T4 = one third of this card (3.2 steps/s); game mode = duty 0.25 (2.4 steps/s, by the duty arithmetic). The P100 is
retired for this competition (`docs/competition/overview.md`) and is not considered.

| run | size | local, duty 0.8 | local, unthrottled | local while a game runs | Kaggle, one T4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ablation, 30 min | local 12,900 steps = 15 passes over all 220,443 molecules (3.8× all spectra) | 30 min | 22 min | 4,300 steps in 30 min at duty 0.25 = 5 passes | 30 min = 5,760 steps = 6.7 passes |
| ablation, 45 min | 19,400 steps = 22.5 passes | 45 min | 34 min | | 45 min = 10 passes |
| + scoring and the as-feature refit | 6 s GPU, 5 min CPU | | | | |
| full hold-out-safe run | 86,100 steps | 3.3 h | 2.5 h | 10 h | 7.5 h, one 12-hour session |
| full-data run (275,810 structures) | 107,700 steps | 4.2 h | 3.1 h | 12.5 h | 9.3 h, one session |
| five-fold cross-fitting (each on 4/5 of all structures) | 5 × 86,200 steps | 16.7 h | 12.5 h | | 37 h on one T4: more than one account's 30 h per week; two folds per T4 ×2 session = 3 sessions of 7.5 h |
| GNN variant, full hold-out-safe run | batch 128, loader-bound at 600 molecules/s | 10.3 h (6.5 h with a graph cache) | | | not estimated: Kaggle's 4 CPU cores are the limit |

Whether a T4 ×2 session is charged as one or two GPU-hours per hour was not checked. Kaggle needs the spectrum cache
(1.1 GB), the pool fingerprints (0.9 GB), the PubChem negatives (4.3 GB) and the index as private datasets; nothing was
uploaded.

**Recommendation.**
- **Ablations: local.** A 30-minute run already sees all data 15 times, the feedback loop (train, score, refit) is 36
  minutes, and the run yields to games. No data fraction is needed; `--frac` exists for cheaper checks.
- **Full hold-out-safe run: local**, 3.3 h in one go or resumed across sessions (checkpoint every 500 steps).
- **Full-data production run: Kaggle or local**, 9.3 h against 4.2 h; local unless the PC is needed.
- **Five-fold cross-fitting: probably not needed.** The ranker's training molecules all sit in fold 0 and the panel, so
  one hold-out-safe model gives them leak-free scores, as the fold-safe FPNet does today. It is only needed if ranker
  training molecules are taken from other folds; then Kaggle (T4 ×2, two folds per session) so the PC stays free.

## 6. What could not be built or verified

- torch-geometric, DGL and torch-scatter are not installed (nothing was installed). The GNN is plain torch; it was timed
  for 40 steps and never trained beyond that; its scoring path is written and has not been run.
- No cross-encoder re-ranker. No graph cache. No length bucketing. No `torch.compile`.
- FINAL was not evaluated, and its 200,553 non-pool candidate fingerprints are not computed (`cand_fp.py --half final`,
  about 4 minutes of CPU, 260 MB).
- The benchmark is at ranker level. The fused list exists for 306 / 235 DEV lists as stored ranks only.
- Benchmark spectra are the cleaned cache spectra of one library per molecule, not raw `train.parquet` rows.
- Game mode and the yield path (exit code 75) were not exercised: no game ran. `guard_run.py` was not tried with this trainer. Batch 256 sits at 7.9 GB under the 8 GB
  cap; an out-of-memory would end the process (`--resume` continues).
- The T4 factor, the number of passes of a full run, Kaggle's CPU speed and quota accounting are assumptions.
- The loader's speed depends on 4.9 GB of file cache; under memory pressure it was measured 100 times slower per row.
- The first loader measurement of the GNN (476 molecules/s) ran while three fingerprint workers were still busy; the
  repeat on an otherwise idle allowance gave 597. The loader figures include up to 12 prefetched batches (≤ 12% high).
- The two test files pass when run directly with the venv python (`pytest` is not installed); the other tests of the
  solution were not run, no tracked file besides the new ones was touched.
