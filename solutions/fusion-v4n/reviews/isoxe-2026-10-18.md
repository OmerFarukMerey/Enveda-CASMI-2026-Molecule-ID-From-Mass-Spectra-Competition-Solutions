# Isomer cross-encoder (isoxe) pilot on stack1 (2026-10-18)

Marks: **[V]** computed here (file named); **[C]** cited; **[I]** inference. Isomer RR = 1 / (1 + #same-formula decoys above the
truth + 0.5 #ties). Replay numbers as in `stack1err-2026-10-17.md` / `tims-2026-10-17.md` (composite, arms m / u, 10,000 bootstrap
resamples of molecules; one public answer = +0.008, +0.0133 composite after the 0.6 scaling). **No leaderboard estimate.** Nothing
submitted, pushed, downloaded or committed; no NIST data; the visible `test.parquet` was not read. Pre-registration
`scratch/isoxe/PREREG.md`, written before any training or model output.

## 0. Bottom line

- **KILL by the pre-registered gate.** On the 193 held-out Enveda panel molecules (stack1's GLACIER-scored window groups, about 37
  same-formula candidates each) the model (XE) reaches isomer MRR **0.306**, against MetFrag-lite 0.176 and **GLACIER 0.559**.
  GLACIER + XE (z-sum, lambda 1) gives 0.479, i.e. **−0.081 [−0.127, −0.035]** against GLACIER alone. The KILL condition
  "(GL+XE) − GL <= 0" is met. XE − MF = +0.130 [+0.091, +0.170] passes its own half.
- **What the model does learn is real and transfers across sets.** XE beats MetFrag-lite by +0.12 to +0.13 everywhere: panel
  (P and all 225 panel replay molecules), enveda-180 timsTOF (35 replay molecules +0.27; T 1,992 held-out molecules +0.120 ± 0.006),
  other libraries (V +0.13). With the annotation removed ("blind" diagnostic) it falls to 0.116 on P (random 0.070): the gain comes
  from the fragment evidence, not a structure prior.
- **But GLACIER is far ahead on natural products, and level on drug-like timsTOF.** On the 35 enveda-180 molecules XE = GLACIER
  (0.782 vs 0.781; GL+XE +0.046 ± 0.047, n.s.). On the NP panel GLACIER leads by 0.25. GLACIER's panel advantage may partly be
  training-set overlap (its MassSpecGym training structures were never checked against the panel) [I].
- **Replay (secondary, pre-registered rule X1): fails.** timsTOF composite +0.010 [+0.000, +0.020] (m) / +0.007 [−0.004, +0.018]
  (u). Panel pool-answer lists (c1 + c2) **−0.006 ± 0.008** (17 better / 25 worse); the gain sits in the answer-removed types
  (dA / dB +0.02), the pattern finalrank warned about. Expected public effect on pool answers −0.7 answers. None of five variants
  (lambda 0.5, GLACIER branch only, frag-gap only, XE replacing GLACIER, post-hoc XE replacing MetFrag-lite in frag-gap) passes.
- **No candidate for stack1.** The bi-encoder (joint1) and this cross-encoder agree: a model trained on train.parquet alone does not
  out-rank GLACIER's isomer order on Enveda NP spectra.

## 1. Design [V code]

| Part | What |
|---|---|
| Featurizer `clean/isoxe_feat.py` | per candidate: heavy-atom graph, all pieces after removing 1-3 bonds (3 if <= 50 bonds, 2 if <= 150), intact molecule k = 0; cheapest piece per unique mass with heavy / N / O / ring / aromatic counts and the hashed types (4,096 buckets: both atom types, order, ring) of the broken bonds. Each of the 32 most intense peaks of every spectrum gets the best explanation over the adduct's carriers and H shifts −2..+3 within max(0.01 Da, 20 ppm): k, dh, carrier, error / tol, fragment fractions, bond types, number of matching pieces. numba; 10-15 ms per candidate. |
| Model `train/isoxe/model.py` | one sequence per (candidate, spectrum): CLS (candidate-global + adduct / polarity / instrument / CE) + peak tokens (spectrum side + annotation, embeddings for k, dh, carrier, summed bond types); 3-layer pre-norm transformer, d 128, 4 heads, 0.95 M parameters; candidate score = mean logit over the molecule's spectra (all adducts, both polarities). |
| Training `train/isoxe/train_isoxe.py` | listwise softmax over a group (positive first; <= 15 same-formula negatives, distinct InChIKey14: up to 8 w10 PubChem window rows, most similar first, then pool isomers); enveda-180 groups weight 2; AdamW 3e-4, cosine, batch 32 groups, 12 epochs, 3 sampled spectra per molecule; best epoch by V; two seeds averaged. |
| Data `train/isoxe/build_groups.py` | train 39,526 groups (20k with enveda-180 timsTOF spectra, 20k other libraries) from the identity-disjoint split's training part, minus 10,284 structures sharing a Murcko scaffold with any panel / mixed / V / T molecule; V 2,955 and T 1,992 from the `hold` fold (mixed replay molecules excluded); spectra from the library cache (`chemcore.clean_peaks`). |
| Tests | `tests/test_isoxe.py` (8, no competition data): intact mass, benzoyl / precursor / Na / negative-mode annotation, bond-type ids, peak selection, cache cleaning parity, model shapes, group scoring, chunk packing and row addressing. |

Provenance: train.parquet, the public pool tables, the PubChem tier (w10 negatives); all code ours; no third-party weights.

## 5. Runtime on Kaggle (T4, about 430 hidden molecules) [V timings here, I for Kaggle]

- Featurisation: the 226 panel molecules (13,835 candidates, all spectra) took 55 s on one core plus 16 s MetFrag-lite; scaled to 430
  molecules and stack1's window + PubChem rows, about 2-4 CPU minutes on one core.
- Inference: 0.24 ms per (candidate, spectrum) sequence on 4 CPU threads, about 25 s per model for 100k sequences; seconds on the T4.
- Total well under 10 minutes next to stack1's ~4 h. Runtime is not the obstacle.

## 6. Next step

- Do not wire XE into stack1. The decisive measurement for any isomer model is GLACIER on the Enveda NP panel (0.559 here); an
  annotation model at 0.31 cannot add to it.
- If the team continues on isomer order, the two questions this pilot leaves are: (1) how much of GLACIER's panel lead is training
  overlap (check panel InChIKeys against MassSpecGym's training split, a free download that needs the main session's approval);
  if it is large, GLACIER's local numbers overstate its hidden-test value and XE's +0.13 over MetFrag-lite becomes relevant again;
  (2) a forward model of our own trained on enveda-180 timsTOF spectra (XE matches GLACIER there) evaluated against GLACIER on NP
  isomers before anything else.
