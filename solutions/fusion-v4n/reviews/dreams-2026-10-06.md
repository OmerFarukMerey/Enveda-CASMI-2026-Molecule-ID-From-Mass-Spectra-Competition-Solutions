## 0. Bottom line

1. **Compliance: DreaMS is clean; the heads are clean per their card only.** `ssl_model.ckpt` is the official
   self-supervised checkpoint (md5 equal to Zenodo 10997887), trained on GeMS (unannotated MassIVE / GNPS spectra),
   CC BY 4.0, code MIT; commercial use and redistribution are allowed with attribution; the host allows it. The heads'
   licence field is "Other" with no grant text, their training pairs are described by counts whose names match
   `train.parquet` sources, and no training code or pair list is published: no NIST per the card, not verifiable.
   That is a residual prize-eligibility risk and is stated as such (section 1).
2. **The component is weak.** Alone, on the pool rows within 10 ppm, panel molecules with an [M+H]+ spectrum (209):
   MRR 0.376, top-1 23.0%; the fold-safe FPNet, which has not seen these molecules either, has 0.573 and 38.3% on the
   same molecules (−0.198 ± 0.028, 35 / 126); public model A 0.673. The author's card number (0.367 on "NP209") is
   reproduced. Nitrogen-free molecules: 0.272 against 0.439.
3. **It does not add to our list.** Nine variants fixed in advance, full v24a replay, three sets: no type gains
   beyond two standard errors anywhere (largest: `dB` +0.011 ± 0.006 on the panel, +0.009 ± 0.005 on the mixed
   regime; sign-flip p 0.54 and 0.52), `c1` loses beyond one standard error in every panel row, and the third-list
   variants lose `dA` / `dB` by up to 6.7 standard errors. The stated test fails. **Nothing was wired, no flag, no
   preset, no dataset entry.**
4. **Complementarity is near a coin flip**: where our list has the answer at ranks 2 to 25, the component scores the
   answer above our first row in 50 to 71% of the scored cases; where the answer is first, it prefers our second row
   in 30 to 38%.
5. **DreaMS is already in our chain**: `fe_D.pt` of `casmi26-v4b-models`, one of the v1 engine's `model_views`, is a
   DreaMS fine-tune (its `LICENSE-NOTICE.txt`). The premise "an encoder nobody in the pipeline uses" does not hold.
6. **Kaggle cost would be negligible** (about one to two minutes, 1.2 GB of GPU memory, no wheel to install), so cost
   is not the reason for the no.

## 2. Step 1: what the component is, and running it

- **Encoder**: DreaMS transformer, 116.1 M parameters; input = the 100 most intense peaks plus a precursor token;
  output used = the precursor token of the last layer, 1,024-d, L2-normalised (`raw_ssl_adapter.py`).
- **Heads**: a spectrum MLP (1,024 → 256) and a molecule MLP (Morgan radius 2, 4,096 bits → 256), contrastive; score =
  cosine, averaged over the molecule's spectra and the two seeds. **Not a fingerprint and not class probabilities: a
  joint embedding for retrieval.**
- **The author's intended use** (`joint_retrieval_worker.py`, `joint_fusion.py`; no notebook of his uses it): only
  molecules with an **[M+H]+** spectrum (at most 3, chosen by a hash); candidates = pool rows within 10 ppm of the
  measured neutral mass plus any `candidate_smiles` passed in; the best 32 metric keys become a third list in the
  reciprocal-rank fusion with weight 0.35 (K 3); library hits are left alone.
- **Local run**: the runtime file is a self-contained re-packing of the DreaMS model code that needs only `torch` and
  `numpy` (plus RDKit for the Morgan bits). It ran in the repo's `.venv` as it is (torch 2.8.0+cu126, numpy 2.3.2,
  rdkit 2026.3.3): **no package was installed, `D:\casmi-data\dreams_env` was not created.** `scratch/dreams/dr_lib.py`
  loads the author's modules with their `/kaggle/working` asserts removed in memory and opens the checkpoints with
  `weights_only=True` and his class allow-list (on Windows `PosixPath` is mapped to `PurePosixPath`). Checks: batch
  size 1 against 16 differs by 9e-8; the heads' checkpoint headers match the worker's asserts (seed, step 3,000).

## 4. Step 3: Kaggle feasibility
