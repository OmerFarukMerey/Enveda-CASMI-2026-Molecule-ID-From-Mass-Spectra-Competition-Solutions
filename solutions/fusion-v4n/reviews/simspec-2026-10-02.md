# Simulated spectra for FPNet (2026-10-02): does a simulated spectrum of the true structure help retrieval?

Local experiment only. Nothing was submitted, pushed to Kaggle or committed. Edited: `train/train_fpnet.py` (new
options, defaults unchanged) and `train/README.md` (Question 2). New: `train/sim_iceberg.py`, this report, one-off
scripts and the running log in `scratch/simspec/` (git-ignored), data in `D:\casmi-data\simspec\` (73 MB) and
checkpoints / rank tables in the run directory `D:\casmi-data\directions\run`. `INDEX.md` was not regenerated
(`python tools/gen_index.py` when this is committed).

Metric everywhere: FPNet alone, rank of the true structure among the pool entries within 10 ppm (`window_eval` of
`train_fpnet.py`), on REAL spectra of structures the model never saw a real spectrum of. It is the first stage of
the pipeline, not the pipeline and not the leaderboard.

## 0. Bottom line

1. **The simulation is cheap and usable.** ICEBERG gave a spectrum for 11,523 of 11,525 structures (99.98%; the two
   failures contain Mg and V) at 37 predictions/s on the GPU (28/s wall at duty 0.8, 1.7 GB). Its intensities are on
   the **square-root scale** (ms-pred trains on sqrt intensities); squared, the peak-count profile matches measured
   timsTOF spectra. A real [M+H]+ spectrum has entropy similarity 0.35 to the simulated spectrum of its structure
   (0.52 on timsTOF), against 0.06 to the simulated spectrum of another structure of nearly the same mass and 0.69
   to a real replicate. The fold-safe FPNet, which never saw a simulated spectrum, finds a structure from its
   simulated spectrum as often as from a real one (top-1 0.385 against 0.39).
2. **The pilot shows a large gain exactly where the structure was simulated and nowhere else.** Fine-tune with a
   quarter of every batch simulated, against the control fine-tune, paired top-1 on real spectra: simulated
   structures T **+0.113 ± 0.008** (0.407 → 0.520), their [M+H]+ queries +0.161, negative-mode queries +0.060, the
   structures ICEBERG cannot have seen +0.131 ± 0.018, the timsTOF natural-product panel half that was simulated
   +0.206 ± 0.033. Structures that were not simulated: C +0.014 ± 0.007, panel half Cp −0.016 ± 0.024, all other
   hold-out rows within ±0.005.
3. **Most of that gain is not what a full run would give.** In the pilot the truth is one of 11,523 structures the
   model was trained to output and its window competitors are not. Measured per pair of truth and competitor: a
   simulated truth beats a never-seen competitor more often (+0.035), but a competitor that was simulated too only
   slightly more often (**+0.010 ± 0.006**), and a truth that was not simulated loses to simulated competitors
   (−0.027 ± 0.005). The model learned "structures I was trained on are likelier", which also explains the gain in
   negative mode from positive-mode simulations. In a full run every class-2 competitor is simulated, and that part
   cancels. What remains is the small spectrum-specific part and the gain against competitors that have real
   training spectra (+0.020 ± 0.002 per pair), which class-2 structures lack today.
4. **Projected to a full run: +0.03 to +0.08 top-1 on class-2-like structures instead of +0.11** (two model-based
   projections from the measured pairs, section 6). This is not measured, and a loss for structures that do have
   real spectra is possible and not measured either.
5. **Next step: the window arm, not the full simulation.** Simulate every never-seen competitor in the windows of
   431 targets (21,014 structures, 38 minutes), fine-tune once more, evaluate. One command, section 7. Only if that
   shows a gain is the full run (436,515 structures, about 13 hours of simulation and 5 to 10 hours of training)
   worth starting. No leaderboard number can be attached to the pilot.

Run note: a game started on the machine at 17:59. The fine-tune ran at a quarter duty from step 11,000 and yielded
the GPU nine times (the game held more than 9 GB); it finished its 20,000 steps at 19:55 (0.50 h of training time). All numbers are from the final
checkpoint `ft_sim25_final.pt`. The checkpoint at step 15,077 was evaluated in between and differs by less than 0.01
in every row (`compare_sim25s15.csv`).

## 1. Design, and what was changed from the brief

| Set | Structures | What | Simulated |
|---|---|---|---|
| T | 1,400 pool entries (1,094 inside, 306 outside MassSpecGym sources) | fold `hold`, natural-product catalogue, real [M+H]+ spectra | yes |
| C | 1,401 (1,094 / 307) | same population, disjoint | no (control) |
| Tp / Cp | 125 / 125 | random halves of the 265 np-panel structures (fold `np`) | Tp yes, Cp no |
| B | 10,000 | random class-2 entries (catalogue, no spectrum in train) | yes (filler) |

"Inside MassSpecGym sources" = the structure has a spectrum from GNPS, MassBank or MoNA in `train.parquet`
(`libmask`), so ICEBERG's training set probably contained it. The structure list of MassSpecGym itself was not
downloaded; the flag is a proxy.

Changes to the brief:
- **T and C have 1,400 structures each, not 1,500.** Counted per pool entry (several train structures can share one
  metric key) the eligible population is 4,103, with only 613 outside the MassSpecGym sources.
- **The np panel was split into Tp / Cp.** The real spectra of T and C are Orbitrap and QTOF (74,313 positive
  [M+H]+ spectra, 25 of them timsTOF). The panel is the only timsTOF natural-product hold-out, so half of it was
  simulated to have one test on the instrument of the hidden test. The "np panel" rows of the standard table are
  therefore reported per half.
- **Simulated intensities are squared** before training (section 2), decided from peak statistics before any
  training result.
- **C and Cp were simulated as well, but never trained on** (`simc/`), to measure how the fine-tuned model reads
  simulated spectra of structures it was not trained on.
- **One mixing ratio only** (a quarter of every batch). The result was not ambiguous in size; what it needed was an
  explanation, so the remaining effort went into the pairwise diagnostic (section 5) instead of a second ratio.

Leak control: a simulated structure gets a new training id that maps to its pool entry (`add_sim` in
`train_fpnet.py`), so the real spectra of T keep their id and their fold and never enter training. Evaluation groups
are built before the simulated rows are appended, and the standard sets draw the same 3,000 groups as the
`base_fold0` table (reproduced: hold all 0.675, hold NP-catalogue 0.389, np panel 0.347).

## 2. Simulation

`train/sim_iceberg.py`: the packaged ICEBERG checkpoint (ms-pred, open MassSpecGym weights, MIT; no NIST), adduct
[M+H]+, instrument token QTOF (the runner maps timsTOF to it), 20 / 40 / 60 eV. The timsTOF rows of train use
exactly these three energies (40 eV 55%, 60 eV 26%, 20 eV 19%, or the three merged); the runner rounds to 5 eV, so
no bucket is lost. Output in the layout of the spectrum cache (peaks, [M+H]+ precursor m/z, adduct index, collision
energy, mode). Chunks of 256 structures, resumable, GPU duty cycle through `train/gpushare.py`.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Yield | 11,523 of 11,525 structures (T 1,400 / 1,400, Tp 125 / 125, B 9,998 / 10,000; failures: one Mg, one V compound) |
| Speed | 37.3 predictions/s on the GPU (batch 32), 28/s wall at duty 0.8; 34,569 predictions in 20.5 min |
| GPU memory | 1.7 GB peak |
| Peaks per spectrum | 100 (the runner's cap) in 99.7% of the spectra; after squaring and the cache floor of 0.1%: median 69 |
| Disk | 0.8 kB per spectrum in the cache layout |

**ICEBERG intensities are on the sqrt scale.** `ms_pred/common/misc_utils.py` takes the square root of every
training spectrum ("Sqrt intensities here"), and the model is trained against that. Peaks per spectrum above a
relative intensity (medians):

| Floor | 0.1% | 0.5% | 1% | 2% | 5% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| simulated, as returned (T, Tp) | 100 | 100 | 100 | 70 | 31 |
| simulated, squared (T, Tp) | 48 | 22 | 16 | 11 | 6 |
| real timsTOF [M+H]+ (20,000 spectra) | 58 | 25 | 18 | 13 | 8 |
| real np panel [M+H]+ | 113 | 55 | 37 | 24 | 13 |

Training therefore used `--sim_pow 2 --sim_floor 0.001`. For ranking candidates by entropy similarity the scale does
not matter (below), so this is not a finding about the notebook's ICEBERG re-scoring.

**Realism** (`realism.py`; entropy similarity with the runner's cleaning, 0.01 Da / 20 ppm; at most 6 real [M+H]+
spectra per structure and instrument; simulated spectrum at the nearest of 20 / 40 / 60 eV, or the three merged when
the energy is unknown or in NCE; mean over structures):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| all T and Tp | 1,521 | 0.349 | 0.313 | 0.062 | 0.691 |
| timsTOF (np panel) | 110 | 0.517 | 0.493 | 0.105 | 0.683 |
| Orbitrap | 1,076 | 0.371 | 0.325 | 0.063 | 0.695 |

The simulation is about half as similar to a real spectrum as a replicate is, clearly better on structures ICEBERG
probably trained on (0.38 against 0.25), useless for ion-trap and triple-quadrupole spectra, and best on timsTOF.

Two further readings of the same spectra:
- **FPNet on simulated queries** (`fpnet_on_sim.py`, window rank, three energies merged): the fold-safe checkpoint
  ranks the structure first for 0.385 of T, 0.383 of C and 0.271 of B (0.362 for T with unsquared intensities). On
  real spectra of the same population it is 0.39. To this model a simulated spectrum is as readable as a real one.
- **Isomer pick** (`isomer_test.py`): the true structure among the simulated structures of the same formula (mean
  4.1 candidates, chance 0.33) by entropy similarity of real to simulated: top-1 0.770 (outside MassSpecGym
  sources 0.746, inside 0.775). Unsquared: 0.766; paired MRR difference +0.003 ± 0.004.

## 5. Where the gain comes from

The pilot answers "does the model find a structure more often when that structure, and almost no other structure of
its window, was a training target?". A full run makes every class-2 structure of the window a training target. The
difference matters if the model learns a preference for structures it was trained on, independent of the spectrum.

`pairwise.py` scores every pool entry in the window of every T, Tp, C and Cp query with the control and with the
fine-tuned model and sorts the competitors by what the fine-tuned model saw of them: `sim` (simulated spectra: other
members of T, Tp, B), `real` (real spectra in the training folds), `none` (class 2 not simulated, or held out).
Share of (query, competitor) pairs in which the truth scores higher (`pairwise_sim25.parquet`):

| Truth | Competitor | pairs | control | fine-tuned | difference (se by query) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| simulated (T) | never seen | 137,745 | 0.903 | 0.938 | +0.035 ± 0.005 |
| simulated (T) | real training spectra | 46,917 | 0.934 | 0.954 | +0.020 ± 0.002 |
| simulated (T) | **simulated too** | 3,522 | 0.882 | 0.896 | **+0.014 ± 0.007** |
| simulated (T and Tp) | simulated too, class 2 (set B) only | 3,229 | 0.899 | 0.909 | +0.010 ± 0.006 |
| simulated (Tp) | simulated too | 413 | 0.925 | 0.908 | −0.017 ± 0.013 |
| **not simulated (C)** | **simulated** | 3,180 | 0.892 | 0.865 | **−0.027 ± 0.005** |
| not simulated (Cp) | simulated | 446 | 0.962 | 0.915 | −0.047 ± 0.011 |
| not simulated (C) | never seen | 128,427 | 0.921 | 0.923 | +0.002 ± 0.002 |
| not simulated (C) | real training spectra | 45,160 | 0.938 | 0.940 | +0.003 ± 0.001 |

| Truth | Window cut to | queries | competitors | control | fine-tuned | difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T | never-seen competitors | 2,133 | 64.6 | 0.534 | 0.637 | +0.104 ± 0.008 |
| T | real-trained competitors | 1,925 | 24.4 | 0.493 | 0.610 | +0.117 ± 0.009 |
| T | simulated competitors | 1,216 | 2.9 | 0.799 | 0.820 | +0.021 ± 0.009 |
| Tp | simulated competitors | 140 | 3.0 | 0.850 | 0.814 | −0.036 ± 0.025 |
| C | simulated competitors | 1,184 | 2.7 | 0.820 | 0.773 | −0.047 ± 0.009 |
| Cp | simulated competitors | 147 | 3.0 | 0.891 | 0.789 | −0.102 ± 0.025 |

Reading:
- Being a training target is worth about 3 points of pairwise win rate, whatever the query: the simulated truth
  gains them (+0.035), and a simulated competitor gains them against a truth that was not simulated (−0.027; −0.047
  on the panel half). When both were simulated they cancel and +0.010 to +0.014 is left (two standard errors at
  best; on the timsTOF panel the sign is negative, n small).
- This is why negative-mode queries gain from positive-mode simulations, why the gain does not depend on whether
  ICEBERG knew the structure, and why C hardly moves: none of that needs a realistic simulation.
- The same preference exists in every FPNet today for the structures with real spectra. A class-2 truth competes
  against them with a handicap. Simulating it removes the handicap: +0.020 per pair against real-trained
  competitors, +0.12 top-1 in a window of real-trained competitors only. This part would survive a full run, and it
  is specific to the population the hidden test is said to consist of. It has a mirror image that the pilot cannot
  measure: a truth that has real spectra would face 436,515 competitors that are now training targets too.

## 6. What a full run would give, and what can inflate the numbers

Two projections from the measured pairs (`project2.py`): every never-seen competitor in the windows of T and Tp is
treated as if it had been simulated.
- **A, no net gain against class 2**: a never-seen competitor is beaten exactly when the control beats it; real-trained
  and simulated competitors as measured.
- **B, calibrated lift**: every never-seen competitor is raised by a constant (2.75 score units; a typical margin is
  37) chosen so that the win rate against class-2 competitors changes by the +0.010 measured against set B.

| Queries | control top-1 | pilot | projection A | projection B (range for ±1 se of the calibration) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T all | 0.399 | 0.513 (+0.114 ± 0.008) | 0.435 (+0.036 ± 0.005) | 0.475 (+0.076; +0.067 to +0.084) |
| T positive | 0.418 | 0.558 (+0.140) | 0.463 (+0.045) | 0.520 (+0.102) |
| T negative | 0.358 | 0.419 (+0.061) | 0.377 (+0.019) | 0.382 (+0.023) |
| T outside MassSpecGym sources | 0.328 | 0.460 (+0.132) | 0.360 (+0.032) | 0.430 (+0.102) |
| Tp all | 0.325 | 0.451 (+0.126) | 0.370 (+0.045) | 0.390 (+0.065) |

(The pairwise script counts ties against the truth and rebuilds the groups itself, hence 0.399 instead of 0.407.)
So the honest expectation for a full run is **+0.03 to +0.08 top-1 of the first stage on class-2-like structures**,
not +0.11, and it is a projection: a constant lift is a crude model of what a simulated isomer does to a query.

What can still inflate it, and what is unknown:
- **Dose.** The pilot gave every simulated structure 28 passes. The same passes over 436,515 structures need 38
  times more simulated samples (section 7). At 7 passes two thirds of the pilot gain were there.
- **Capacity.** 11,523 extra targets are easy to absorb for a 50 M parameter model; 436,515 are 1.6 times the number
  of structures it was trained on. Not tested.
- **ICEBERG has seen the target.** Not an issue for the pilot gain (outside +0.131, inside +0.109), because the gain
  hardly depends on the simulation being right. It matters for the spectrum-specific part, which is the small one.
- **T is easier than the hidden test.** T structures have spectra in public libraries, a median of 26 heavy atoms and
  45 competitors per window; class-2 pool entries have 30 heavy atoms and 51 competitors, and the hidden test is
  timsTOF with many adducts. The timsTOF panel half agrees in direction (+0.21 on 189 groups) and has the same
  problem as T: its competitors were not simulated.
- **Structures with real spectra.** Not simulated truths lose against simulated competitors (−0.027 per pair). With
  2.3% of class 2 simulated this is invisible in the standard table (hold not NP-catalogue −0.002 ± 0.006); with
  100% it need not be. In the pipeline the spectral library handles most of those molecules before FPNet matters.
- **The pipeline after FPNet.** The ranker and the fusion were fitted on the scores of the current FPNet
  (`REL_TH` is an f·z difference), and the ICEBERG re-scoring of the top 60 already compares the query with
  simulated spectra of the candidates. How much of a first-stage gain survives both is not known.
- **Our validation record.** Local gains failed to predict the leaderboard several times today. Nothing here is a
  leaderboard estimate.

## 7. Next step and full version

### 7.1 Window arm (run this first)

The fair local copy of a full run: for 431 targets (Tp, and the 306 T structures outside the MassSpecGym sources)
every pool entry of their windows that the fold-safe model has no training spectrum for is simulated too: 21,014
structures (18,952 class 2 and 2,062 held-out ones; C and Cp are left out so they stay controls), 63,042
predictions, about 38 minutes at duty 0.8. Then one fine-tune with the schedule of `ft_ctrl`, the simulated quarter
drawn from T, Tp and the window structures (the filler B at a tenth of the rate: about 14 passes per structure),
and the same evaluation. About 1.2 hours in total without a game on the machine.

```bash
bash scratch/simspec/run_chain5.sh > D:/casmi-data/simspec/chain5.log 2>&1     # select, simulate, fine-tune, evaluate
# read: rows "T all | outside" and "Tp all" of D:\casmi-data\simspec\compare_simw.csv (x_simw against x_ctrl and x_sim25)
```

Decision rule fixed now: the full run is worth starting if these two rows together gain at least +0.03 top-1 over the

## 8. The other use of the same spectra: an in-silico library

Instead of teaching FPNet, the simulated spectra can be searched directly: for every class-2 candidate of the 10 ppm
window, the entropy similarity of the query spectra to its simulated spectrum (nearest collision energy, the
runner's kernel) becomes a first-stage score.
- It is what `nb/07_rescore.py` does today with ICEBERG, but only for the top 60 of the base list and computed on
  Kaggle inside the 9 hours. Precomputed for all 436,515 structures (a 1.1 GB dataset keyed to the pool, not to the
  test, so it is not the dead-artefact trap) it would cover the whole window, decide who enters the top 60, and
  free the GPU time ICEBERG takes in the notebook.
- It must be a separate channel. `lib_max` and the library gate (`LIB_TAU` 0.9) mean "a real spectrum of this
  structure matched"; a simulated match is worth far less (0.35 to 0.52 against 0.69 for a replicate) and would
  need its own ranker feature or fusion weight.
- It only covers positive [M+H]+ queries (plus [M+Na]+ and the [M+NH4]+ / water-loss surrogates of
  `coverage-2026-10-02.md`); negative mode gets nothing. It has no trained-target prior, which after section 5 is
  an argument for it: what it adds is spectrum-specific by construction.
- Evidence so far: among simulated same-formula structures (4.1 candidates) the truth is first for 0.77 of the
  structures (chance 0.33, section 2); the coverage study measured in-group top-1 0.37 among 46 candidates on the
  val1 molecules.
- Test needed: the simulated spectra of the window arm (7.1) are exactly the input. For the 431 targets, rank the
  window by (a) f·z of the control, (b) simulated-spectrum similarity for the candidates that have one, (c) both
  z-scored and added, and compare top-1 and MRR on the same real queries, with the timsTOF panel as the row to
  read. No training, CPU only once the spectra exist.
