# Does a learned final combiner, fitted in the unseen regime, beat v33b's hand-set chain? (2026-10-08)

**First line: locally yes, by the pre-registered rule no, and the fit is not trustworthy.** Every fitted family beats v33b's
chain on the cross-fitted composite (+0.015 to +0.068, 4 to 13 standard errors), and none meets the acceptance rule. The
linear and tree combiners gain by lifting joined PubChem and generated rows (pool-answer lists topped by a joined row
24 → 33 to 49 of 606; the limit was +5 per 380), which is the move that has lost on the leaderboard every time. The hand
chain with refitted constants keeps exposure flat and chooses forward weights 0.5 and `POP_MU` 0.35 in all 15 folds: both
lost on the leaderboard (−0.004, −0.010). With the exposure levers taken out, the linear combiner is level with the hand
chain (+0.002 ± 0.005, post hoc). **Nothing was exported: no dataset, no flag, no test, no preset, no notebook build.**

## 6. What this can and cannot tell

- **Can:** on local library-compound lists whose v1 side has seen nothing, a fitted combiner orders the candidates better
  than the hand chain by 0.03 to 0.07 composite, and that gain is one lever: more trust in rows that entered through the
  join, the generator and the PubChem list (and, for the tree model, our ranker in place of the public one above the
  library gate: +0.090 at `lib_max` ≥ 0.7). Without that lever a fitted linear re-weighting of rankers, forward models,
  engine 2 and popularity is level with the hand-set constants (+0.002 ± 0.005).
- **Cannot:** say anything about the sign on the hidden test. Local replays matched the leaderboard's sign in 8 and 5 of
  14 contrasts (`obscure` review); the lists used here are the unseen chain's (the 5), with v33b's average as base, and
  the three join contrasts that chain gets wrong are exactly the lever the combiner pulls. "Fitted in the regime of the hidden test" worked for the ranker because its
  inputs (fingerprint features) change between seen and unseen molecules. The final stage's inputs do not change that
  way: what differs on the hidden test is the molecules (answers not first in the base list, stronger joined
  competitors, less famous answers), and no cross-fitting of local library compounds supplies those.
- The question of the brief therefore gets a narrow answer: the hand-set constants downstream of the ranker are not
  measurably wrong in the unseen regime, apart from directions the leaderboard has already tested and refused.
