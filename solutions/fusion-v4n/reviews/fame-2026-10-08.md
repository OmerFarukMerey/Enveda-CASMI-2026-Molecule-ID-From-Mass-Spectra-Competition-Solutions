# Can the past submissions tell us how famous the hidden answers are? (2026-10-08)

**First line: no, the data cannot tell.** The three past contrasts that touch the popularity prior leave the share of
"panel-like" (famous) hidden answers anywhere between 0.15 and 1.00 (90% set; the likelihood changes by a factor of 2
over the whole range 0 to 1, and the posterior standard deviation is 0.285 against 0.289 for no information at all).
Everything below about v44a and v46c is therefore the local replay averaged over an almost flat fame profile, not an
inference from the leaderboard.

Continues `reviews/popmu-2026-10-07.md` and `reviews/popgroup-2026-10-08.md`. Offline only: nothing submitted, pushed,
committed, uploaded or downloaded; no `kaggle` command; no GPU (coverage gap 0 in every new variant: the score caches
of `D:\casmi-data\fpens\` were read, not written); the visible `test.parquet` was not read; no NIST data. CPU: two

**Every number below was computed in this study** unless a file is named. MRR = MRR@25 on the metric key; "±" = one

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Of the 35 submissions, eleven contrasts could carry information about fame; none discriminates.** Three touch the
   popularity prior and are replayable on v33b's chain (`POP_MU` 0 → 0.15 with the fragment term, 0.15 → 0.25, v40a).
   Their local effect differs between the famous and the obscure stratum by 0.009, 0.001 and 0.002, against a noise of
   0.007 to 0.013 per contrast (0.2 to 0.7 standard deviations). The join contrasts are flat in fame locally (+0.063 to
   +0.081 on every tercile) and three of them have the wrong local sign, so they say nothing about fame either.
2. **Inferred profile: none.** Share p of panel-like answers (rest like the mixed regime's least famous natural-product
   tercile, median 13 substance records): most likely 1.00, 68% set 0.40 to 1.00, 90% set 0.15 to 1.00 with the full
   noise; with the leaderboard noise of 0.004 alone, most likely 0.38, 90% set 0.08 to 0.95. As a centre of
   log(1 + records): 90% set 2.25 to 7.5, i.e. 8 to 1,800 records, which is the whole grid.
3. **Level argument (qualitative).** 0.44 on the leaderboard against 0.70 / 0.58 locally needs a composition that is
   mostly not "pool answer": between 9% and 59% of the answers behaving like pool answers, depending on how many score
   zero. The join's gain (+0.022) puts answers that are in PubChem but outside our pool at roughly a quarter to a half
   of the public set if the local gain per such answer transfers. Those answers are obscure by construction and get no
   prior at all. This favours "a large part of the hidden set is not famous", but it is arithmetic on levels, not a
   measurement of fame.
4. **v44a against v33b:** expected +0.003 (standard deviation 0.007); beats by more than one answer 0.45, within ±0.004
   0.41, loses by more than one answer 0.14. Insensitive to fame (+0.0029 to +0.0035 over the whole range).
5. **v46c against v33b:** expected +0.009 (standard deviation 0.011; the expectation runs from −0.000 at p 0 to +0.016
   at p 1); wins 0.68, ties 0.19, loses 0.13. With pool answers only: 0.58 / 0.17 / 0.24. With the transfer error that
   all nine replayed contrasts show (0.022): 0.58 / 0.12 / 0.30. Against v44a: +0.006, 0.58 / 0.26 / 0.16.
   **These are model-based numbers from local replays and a handful of noisy contrasts; local replays had the

6. **Most informative single probe:** not `POP_MU` 0 (it measures the prior's worth, which depends on the share of pool
   answers and hardly on fame: 0.65 standard deviations between the two ends), and not v46c (1.6). It is the ungated
   in-group form, for which the preset `configs/v46x-v33b-popgroup-raw.json` exists: predicted −0.038 if the answers
   are obscure, +0.021 if they are panel-like, 4.3 standard deviations apart. It is expected to lose slightly (−0.001
   to −0.005) and can lose 0.04.

## 3. Inference (task 3)

`scratch/fame/fm_infer.py`, tables in `D:\casmi-data\fame\infer.md`.

**Model.** The public leaderboard set is 130 molecules drawn from a profile with one parameter.
- Family A: a share p is "panel-like" (drawn from the 226 panel molecules), the rest "less-famous-like" (the 89 mixed
  natural products of the bottom tercile). A2: the rest is the less famous half used in the earlier reviews (129).
- Family B: the pooled 486 natural products re-weighted by a Gaussian kernel (width 1.0) around a centre m of `lsid`.
- Type mix: equal (the composite) unless said; three other mixes as sensitivity.
- Likelihood: each observed leaderboard difference is normal around the local prediction. Standard deviation "lb" =
  0.004 alone (as asked); "full" = 0.004 plus the standard error of the local stratum mean plus the sampling spread of
  a 130-molecule draw (from the per-molecule spread of the difference). The full value is 0.007 to 0.013 for the
  popularity contrasts: the finite public set, not the rerun noise, is the main term. Flat prior on a grid.

- **The interval is the prior.** A flat prior on 0 to 1 has a 90% central interval 0.05 to 0.95 and standard deviation
  0.289; the posterior has 0.15 to 1.00 and 0.285. The most likely value moves from 0.38 to 1.00 with the noise model,
  which is what a flat likelihood does.
- **The last row is the warning.** With 0.004 alone and all nine contrasts the model becomes certain (p above 0.9).
  That certainty comes from the join contrasts, which the local replay mispredicts by 0.01 to 0.05 at every p; the
  likelihood rewards the end that mispredicts slightly less. It is model failure, not information. Residuals at p 0.5:
  K1 −0.002, K2 +0.001, K3 +0.002, K4 −0.053, J1 −0.008, J2 −0.001, J3 −0.034, J4 −0.018, J5 −0.011 (root mean square
  0.022 over the nine, 0.002 over the three popularity contrasts).
- The three popularity contrasts are reproduced within 0.002 at every p. That is three points whose predicted and
  observed values are all within 0.005 of zero; it does not show that popularity replays transfer.

**Level argument (qualitative, separate from the likelihood).** v33b's local composite is 0.705 on the panel and 0.582
on the mixed natural products; the leaderboard is 0.442. No fame profile inside the local sets gives 0.44 with equal
type weights (the bottom tercile is 0.572). The fully unseen base chain is not much lower either (0.886 / 0.793 /
0.559 / 0.345, `reviews/uregime-2026-10-07.md`), so memorisation does not close the gap; the composition must.
- Let x = share behaving like pool answers (mean of `c1` and `c2`), y = like `dB` (in PubChem, outside our pool and
  engine 2), z = scoring zero (class 3, or the answer in no source). With panel levels (0.882, 0.399): z 0 → x 0.09;
  z 0.2 → x 0.25; y 0 → x 0.50, z 0.50. With the bottom tercile's levels (0.749, 0.310): z 0 → x 0.30; z 0.2 → x 0.44;
  y 0 → x 0.59, z 0.41. **So at most about half to six tenths of the hidden answers can be pool answers at local
  quality, and possibly far fewer.**
- The join gained +0.022 at N 100 (v13a − v4b; two submissions, so about ± 0.006). On the chain it was submitted on,
  the local gain per answer outside the pool is +0.041 (panel `dB`) to +0.091 (mixed natural products `dB`), and about
  zero on pool answers (−0.008 to +0.007). Taken at face value: 0.022 / 0.091 = 24% to 0.022 / 0.041 = 54% of the
  public answers are in PubChem and outside our pool; with the noise, 18% to 68%. This is soft: the same local replay
  gets N 200 and the gate at 0.8 wrong in sign.
- **What this implies for fame.** Answers outside the pool are obscure by construction (PubChem outside the pool:
  median 2 records; with the COCONUT flag 16) and receive no prior, because joined rows carry none. For them a
  popularity variant acts only through the competitors (`dA` / `dB` columns). The famous-or-not question concerns the
  remaining pool answers, at most half of the set, and the level argument cannot say which of them are famous: lower
  local levels go with lower fame (panel tercile 1 0.61 against 0.77 for tercile 3), but also with everything else that
  makes a compound hard.
