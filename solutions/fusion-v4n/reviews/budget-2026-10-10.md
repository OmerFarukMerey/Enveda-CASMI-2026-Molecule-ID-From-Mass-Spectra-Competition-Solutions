## 0. Bottom line

1. **The three stated mixes do not reproduce 0.442; they give 0.62 to 0.93.** With local within-class accuracy, the
   leaderboard level needs at most about half of the hidden answers to behave like local library or pool answers
   (0.17 to 0.52 of them, section 1.3). The rest is PubChem-only (class C) and no-source (class N) in a proportion the
   level alone does not fix. The two pending probes (first row only, first two rows) fix it further:
   `python scratch/budget/b6_mix.py S1 S2 S`.
2. **Analytic ceiling of our architecture** (answer among our candidates, perfect ranking): 0.99 for library and pool
   answers on timsTOF, **0.65 to 0.80 for PubChem-only answers**, about 0.05 to 0.18 for no-source answers. Weighted:
   **0.52 to 0.83** over the leaderboard-consistent mixes; 0.62 to 0.69 in the mix that makes the leader's 0.76 an
   "answer is in some database" share (class N 0.24). A "realistic" number under one stated assumption (half of the
   rank 2-5 loss and a third of the deeper loss recovered, coverage unchanged): **0.47 to 0.60**, 0.52 to 0.55 in that
   mix. The leader's pair is 0.76 / 0.55.
3. **Where the 0.558 lost points are** (mix "class N 0.24", mixed-regime class profiles; the alternative mix in
   brackets): no source at all 0.23 (0.37); PubChem-only answer never becomes a candidate 0.13 (0.05); PubChem-only
   answer listed but not first 0.12 (0.04); pool and library answers listed but not first 0.05 (0.06); pool and
   library answers not listed 0.03 (0.03; the mass window of other instruments, about 0 on timsTOF).
4. **Ranking loss is isomer order and nothing else.** The top row has the answer's formula in 98.7% (`c2J`) and 99.2%
   (`dropJ`) of the lists; a perfect formula choice is worth +0.006 / +0.003, a perfect order inside the formula group
   +0.190 / +0.336 (0.772 → 0.962, 0.589 → 0.926). Above an answer at rank 2 to 5 stands a same-formula isomer in 98%
   of the cases: same ring scaffold 32 to 54%, another scaffold 40 to 56%, a tautomer-like variant under 1%. No signal
   we have prefers the answer in such a pair (FPNet f.z 36 to 46%, ICEBERG 52%, GLACIER 48%).
5. **The join's losses, of 1,215 DEV answers with a PubChem record:** 79.8% are in the first 100 (64.3% in the first
   25), 4.4% at 101 to 200, 7.3% beyond 200, 8.5% lost by the ECFP4 shortlist of 5,000. The gate at `lib_max` 0.7
   closes the join for 27.8% of them: class-C MRR at ranker level 0.476 ungated, 0.359 gated. Deep rows are placed
   badly: a joined answer selected at rank 1 to 5 scores 0.73, at 51 to 100 0.33.
6. **Widening cannot pay; the specification for the joint model is recall inside N ≤ 100 at unchanged exposure**
   (section 4.1): R@25 ≥ 0.75 and R@100 ≥ 0.88 of record answers (now 0.64 / 0.80), pool answers on `c2J` not below
   top-1 0.711 / MRR 0.794 with its 100 rows, and its score handed to the ranker.
7. **List composition is not a loss.** Sorting by calibrated marginal probability is the expected-MRR optimum by
   construction; cluster features add −0.0002 (DEV) and +0.00003 (fused) to a calibrated re-ordering, and every
   diversified order loses (−0.005 to −0.022). A near-duplicate of a wrong top row is more likely the answer, not less.
8. **Engine disagreement** costs 0.03 (pool answers) to 0.10 (answers only one engine holds) per molecule against an
   oracle that puts the right engine's top row first.
9. **Five design changes** (section 5): isomer-level joint scorer; the joint model as join selector; candidate-level
   library protection instead of the list-level gate; engine arbitration with an independent score; a generative
   fallback only if the probes show a large zero share. Not on the list: formula or mass work, popularity, list
   diversification.

### 1.3 What the leaderboard level says

One equation: `S = Σ w_c · MRR_c`. With L : P as in DEV (0.607 : 0.393) and the class profiles above, the mixes that
give exactly 0.442 lie on a line between "no class C" and "no class N":

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| no class C | `mx` | 0.318 / 0.206 / 0 / 0.475 | 0.396 | 0.422 | 0.535 | 0.535 | 0.480 |
| C = 0.13 (join equation, below) | `mx` | 0.289 / 0.187 / 0.130 / 0.394 | 0.388 | 0.417 | 0.565 | 0.611 | 0.493 |
| **N = 0.24** | `mx` | 0.233 / 0.151 / 0.377 / 0.240 | 0.372 | 0.406 | 0.623 | 0.757 | 0.518 |
| | `p2` | 0.185 / 0.120 / 0.455 / 0.240 | 0.342 | 0.398 | 0.689 | 0.782 | 0.546 |
| no class N | `mx` | 0.145 / 0.094 / 0.761 / 0 | 0.347 | 0.390 | 0.714 | 0.983 | 0.556 |
| | `p2` | 0.103 / 0.067 / 0.830 / 0 | 0.293 | 0.376 | 0.830 | 0.999 | 0.605 |

Readings, each an inference:
- L + P is 0.17 to 0.52 of the hidden answers if local class accuracy transfers; lower accuracy inside L and P (less
  famous answers, fewer library neighbours) raises that share. The level cannot tell the two apart.
- "N = 0.24" is the mix in which the leader's 0.76 equals the share of answers present in some database (L + P + C
  with the whole window = 0.757 / 0.782), and his 0.55 is close to our "realistic" 0.52 to 0.55. That reading of his
  two numbers is a guess; he did not say how they were built.
- Join equation: the join gained 0.022 on the leaderboard (0.409 → 0.431). With a local gain of about 0.22 per
  class-C answer (0.095 → 0.280 and 0.08 → 0.33 in `reviews/join-rank-2026-10-05.md` and this replay) and a pool cost
  of about 0.02 on a pool share of 0.3: `w_C ≈ (0.022 + 0.3 · 0.02) / 0.22 = 0.13`. `reviews/lbcal-2026-10-04.md`
  fitted 0.065 (0.045 to 0.085) for the same weight.

**Rank-1 share from scores.** With `S1` = score of the first-row-only submission, `S2` = first two rows, `S` = full
list (same pipeline, same scoring regime):
- share at rank 1: `p1 = S1`; share at rank 2: `p2 = 2 (S2 − S1)`; ranks 3 to 25 carry `R = S − S2`;
- share with the answer at ranks 3 to 25: between `3R` and `25R`; at the local mean reciprocal rank of such answers
  (0.17 to 0.26 by class, 0.20 used): `R / 0.20`;
- share with no answer in the 25: `1 − p1 − p2 − R/0.20` (bounds: `1 − p1 − p2 − 25R` to `1 − p1 − p2 − 3R`);
- class shares: non-negative solution of the four equations `Σ w_c p1_c = S1`, `Σ w_c (p1_c + p2_c / 2) = S2`,
  `Σ w_c MRR_c = S`, `Σ w_c = 1` with the profiles of section 2.2 (four unknowns, four equations; it assumes the
  profiles transfer). `python scratch/budget/b6_mix.py S1 S2 S` prints all of it.
- Without the probes: `p1 ≤ 0.442`, and under the mixes above `p1` = 0.29 to 0.40. A class-C-heavy test gives a low
  `S1 / S` (class C: 0.52 to 0.68), a class-N-heavy one a high ratio (L and P: 0.89 to 0.96).

### 3.6 (f) List composition

Under mutually exclusive candidates the expected reciprocal rank of an order is `Σ p_i / rank_i`: linear in the
marginal probabilities, so the optimum is the descending sort by calibrated `p_i` (rearrangement inequality).
Diversity enters only if the current order is not monotone in the calibrated probability once cluster membership is
known. Tested two ways.

*Calibrated re-ordering.* P(candidate is the answer) fitted out of fold (LightGBM, scaffold folds), first 30 rows of
the base list (DEV, 2,571 `c2J` + `dropJ` lists) and the final 25 (fused chain, 2,076 lists); calibration is good
(bins 0.03 / 0.07 / 0.14 / 0.28 / 0.50 / 0.70 / 0.92 observed as 0.03 / 0.07 / 0.15 / 0.27 / 0.46 / 0.70 / 0.91).

| Inputs of the calibration | DEV: MRR (current 0.697) | Δ | fused: MRR (current 0.628) | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| positions and scores of the existing rankers / signals | 0.731 | +0.034 ± 0.004 | 0.700 | +0.072 ± 0.005 |
| the same + cluster features (similarity to the rows above, counts of near-duplicates above, same formula as the top) | 0.731 | +0.034 ± 0.004 | 0.700 | +0.072 ± 0.005 |
| **cluster features, net** | | **−0.0002** | | **+0.00003** |

The +0.034 and +0.072 are not composition effects and are not candidates: on DEV it is "our ranker alone beats the
average" (leaderboard: 0.434 against 0.442, row 34); on the fused chain it is `metafit`'s learned combiner (gain on
`dA` +0.19 and `dB` +0.08, pool types −0.002 / −0.002: it lifts rows of the constructed types). With the popularity
count added the DEV gain is +0.127, the fame artefact once more.

*Diversified orders on DEV* (greedy, value of a position × (1 − λ · highest similarity to the rows already placed);
"skip" = rows above a similarity threshold to a placed row go to the back):

| Order | MRR | Δ ± SE | wins / losses |
|---|---|---|---|
| λ 0.25 | 0.697 | +0.0001 ± 0.0001 | 155 / 111 |
| λ 0.5 | 0.696 | −0.0004 ± 0.0002 | 157 / 147 |
| λ 1.0 | 0.688 | −0.009 ± 0.001 | 144 / 347 |
| skip ≥ 0.9 / 0.8 / 0.7 | 0.692 / 0.686 / 0.675 | −0.005 / −0.011 / −0.022 | 62 / 79, 126 / 154, 175 / 288 |

Why: when the first row is wrong, the second row is the answer in 24% of the lists if its similarity to the first is
below 0.3, 31% at 0.3 to 0.5, 34% at 0.5 to 0.7, 40% at 0.7 to 0.85 and 32% above. The answer usually sits inside the
cluster of the top row. In the final fused lists the same bins give 13% / 22% / 35% / 36% / 26%. 8.5% of rows 2 to 25 of the base list are within 0.9 of a row above them (fused: 4.9%); 28% of rows 2 to 5 are within 0.7 (fused: 22%). **Result: the current order loses
nothing to clustering; spending ranks on near-identical structures is the right bet.**

### 4.1 Specification of the joint model as join selector

Net effect of a join of size N with a selector of recall `R(N)` among record answers:
`Δ = w_C · [R(N) · m_C(N) − R_0 · m_C0] − w_PL · [c(N) − c_0]`, with `m_C` the placement of a joined answer, `c` the
cost to pool and library answers below the gate, and index 0 the current join (R@100 0.798, class-C MRR 0.476
ungated / 0.359 gated at ranker level; `c_0` 0.029 locally).

- **N = 200.** Rows 101 to 200 of the current selection hold 4.4% of the record answers at a placement of about 0.33:
  at most `w_C · 0.015` = 0.002 to 0.006. The leaderboard paid 0.025 for them. A selector at N 200 breaks even only
  if `w_C · ΔRM ≥ 0.025`: with **perfect** recall inside 200 (all 20.2 missing points, placed at 0.33 to 0.48) that
  is `w_C ≥ 0.26 to 0.38`; at `w_C` 0.13 it is impossible. In terms of precision: one row in N is right, so today's
  0.80% (0.798 / 100) would have to become 0.5% at N 200 with R = 1.0, i.e. a selection that never misses.
- **N = 500.** No leaderboard point exists; the local curve says rows beyond 25 are free, and for rows 101 to
  200 it was wrong by more than an order of magnitude (about 0.002 expected, 0.025 paid). Not to be tried before the N 200 condition holds.
- **So the specification is at N ≤ 100, three numbers on DEV** (all measurable with `bench` and the deep lists):
  1. *Recall.* R@25 ≥ 0.75 and R@100 ≥ 0.88 of record answers (now 0.643 and 0.798; timsTOF 0.743 and 0.814). The
     ECFP4 shortlist caps any re-ranking of it at 0.915: scoring the whole window (median 6,121 rows) lifts the cap.
     First test without new fingerprints: re-rank the stored first 200 (and the 1,000 of `next_levers/pc_k1000`, 105
     DEV molecules); R@100 can reach 0.842 there.
  2. *Exposure.* With its 100 rows in place of the current ones, pool answers on gate-open `c2J` lists stay at
     rank 1 ≥ 0.711 and MRR ≥ 0.794 (average ranker), per stratum (panel 0.778, timsTOF 0.884). A selector that finds
     harder decoys fails here even with better recall; `PC_MODELS='hn'` gained +0.028 locally on answer-removed lists
     and scored 0.423 against 0.431 (row 26).
  3. *Placement.* Its score enters the ranker (`as_feature`): class-C MRR over all record answers ≥ 0.55 ungated
     (now 0.476), and a joined answer at selection rank 26 to 100 above 0.45 (now 0.33 to 0.39).
- A hard precision target follows only for a smaller join: R@25 ≥ 0.80 (3.2% of the rows right) would allow N 25 to
  50 at today's coverage. Locally that saves nothing (the cost sits in the first rows); on the leaderboard N 50 was
  0.011 below N 100, so it is a probe, not a plan.

## 6. Not verified

- The hidden mix. Every leaderboard-scale number is `share × local per-class loss` with the share assumed.
- The class N profile (MRR 0.05, reach 0.10) is an assumption inside a measured range (0.03 to 0.16).
- Class E (engine-2-only candidates): its hidden share and the overlap of engine 2's files with the v1 pool were not
  counted; the mixes fold it into C, which understates what the engine fusion is worth on the hidden test.
- The reading of the leader's 0.76 / 0.55 is a guess from one sentence.
- The probes: no score existed (three submissions pending); the formulas and `b6_mix.py` are untested on our own data.
- The fused chain is v33b on 606 local molecules whose forward models have very probably seen them; engine 2's
  rankers are cross-fitted on the panel only. The stage attribution is cumulative ablation, not the internal order of
  `fusion_core` (popularity and forward terms are applied together in stage A).
- Join-size curves use stored ranker scores on sub-sampled lists (checked at N 0: 0.799 against 0.809).
- Panel molecules have no `truth_in_n1` flag: 17 panel answers beyond rank 200 are counted with the shortlist losses.
- The paired error of "cluster features, net" was not computed; it is the difference of two gains with SE 0.004 to
  0.005 each, on the same lists.
- The 2 to 5 analysis compares the answer with the first row only; relations use RDKit Murcko scaffolds and the
  `HetAtomTautomer` hash.
- Natural classes use library membership bits of `train_structs.parquet`, the catalogue flag and the PubChem tier
  window; 480 of the 2,675 DEV molecules have no deep list and no natural class.
- No change was implemented, replayed or submitted; the realistic shares in section 5 are stated assumptions.
