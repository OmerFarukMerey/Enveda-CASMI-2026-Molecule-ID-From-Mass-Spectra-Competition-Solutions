## 6. Conclusion

**Where the −0.03 most plausibly comes from.**
- **Proven by logs** [V]: (1) at full size the e006 stage on our base lists completes healthy (6,658 s, every
  simulator ok) and `submission.csv` is its table; (2) a stage killed in mid-run leaves a full own table with all
  forward models; (3) the literal stand-in can only be submitted if the last cell raises; (4) above `lib_max` 0.7
  v67a is e006 (133 of 133 first rows at full size; 400 of 400 rows in v67e's commit run); (5) below 0.7 the
  handed-over lists hold on average 21.6 joined PubChem rows among their first 25, and e006 cuts the base list to
  25 rows before its slots and fusion; (6) our handed-over base list has the answer first less often than e006's
  own (222 against 258 of 267 on leaked validation data).
- **Inference** [I]: the leaderboard loss is a **real loss of e006's chain on our base lists below the gate**, not
  a fallback. A fallback would have submitted our own table (v59a 0.434 for v67a), and nothing at full size makes
  the stage fail; the one remaining failure candidate is the deadline, whose margin at hidden scale is about 6%.
  For v67a and v67d the crowding of (5) is a concrete mechanism: a pool answer that the ranker does not put among
  the first few rows is cut with the 60 → 25 step, where e006's own list would still hold it; our own chain
  re-ranks 60 rows (`TOPN`; read from the code, not traced on lists). For **v67e (no joined rows) no mechanism is shown**: its equal loss (0.411) points at the
  rank-averaged order itself, and that is exactly what the unread run tests on validation data.
- **What validation cannot say**: v67a − e006 is +0.045 ± 0.011 there (54 / 24), because e006's own engine fusion
  costs it 54 first places on these molecules and joined rows shield our lists from that step. With answers the
