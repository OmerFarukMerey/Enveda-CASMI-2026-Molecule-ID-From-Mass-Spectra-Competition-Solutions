# Confidence gate for the PubChem join (2026-10-04)

Offline only. Nothing was submitted, pushed or committed, no Kaggle notebook was run, no existing file was edited, the
GPU was not used, no engine run was needed beyond a 2-minute rehearsal. Scripts: `scratch/confgate/` (git-ignored, log
in `LOG.md`). Prototype with a test: `solutions/fusion-v4n/prototypes/conf_gate.py`, `test_conf_gate.py` (8 tests, no
data). Outputs: `D:\casmi-data\confgate\` (4 MB). MRR = MRR@25 on the metric key; "±" is one standard error;
differences are paired per molecule; (wins / losses) in brackets. Reproduce: section 6.

**Leaderboard this builds on** (v4b settings, public ranker): no join 0.409; join N 50 / 100 / 200 where `lib_max` < 0.7:
0.420 / **0.431** / 0.406; N 100 where `lib_max` < 0.8: 0.416. **Reference** below = N 100 where `lib_max` < 0.7.

**How to read the numbers.** The molecules are the 380 hold-out molecules of the mixed regime: training structures
with library relatives whose truth is hidden by masking. Types: `dB` = truth in neither candidate set (reported for
the 325 with a PubChem record: the gain side), `dA` = truth only among engine-2's candidates, `c2` = truth a pool row
without spectra (380 lists), `c1` = class-1-like (150 lists). Replays: zero-forward (380 molecules), v1 side
(zero-forward with the engine-2 fusion off in both arms: the honest cost side, next-levers review), ICEBERG prefix
(169 molecules). The regime's net MRR has mispredicted the leaderboard for every change in how many outside candidates
enter and understated the damage to `c2`-like lists about fivefold. **This report gives no leaderboard number.** The
recommendation rests on two things that do not pass through that net: the share of the `dB` gain kept, and exposure
(which truth-in-pool lists the join touches, and how many injected rows reach their first 25).

## 0. Bottom line

1. **The detector.** A logistic regression on 9 list-shape features of the base list from one engine pass without
   injection. On the regime's molecules (never in the fit): AUC **0.82 ± 0.015** for "truth in the pool" (`c2`, truth
   placed as a COCONUT-only row) against "truth removed"; **0.73 ± 0.02 on natural-product catalogue structures**,
   0.95 on the others; 0.85 (natural products 0.77) among lists below `lib_max` 0.7, where the gate acts. The
   LightGBM on all 48 features, fitted on the same lists, reaches 0.83 (0.73): the small model is not worse and is the
   one packaged. End to end the two give the same rows.
2. **The question: yes for `c2`, half for `c1`.** With `lib_max` < 0.7 kept on top and N 100:

   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 0.3 | 77 / 66 / 68 / 80% | 8% (0.12 of the reference) | 1.0 | 4% | 5% (0.32) |
   | **0.5** | **88 / 79 / 81 / 91%** | **16% (0.23)** | **1.8** | **10%** | **9% (0.56)** |
   | **0.7** | **94 / 88 / 89 / 100%** | **29% (0.40)** | **3.1** | **19%** | **11% (0.68)** |

   Lowering the `lib_max` gate instead trades one for one (0.6: 85% kept at 0.87 of the exposure). The same gate on
   timsTOF natural products with the public models (enveda-np-examples, class-2 emulation) touches 20% of the lists at
   0.5 against 67% for `lib_max` < 0.7 alone (0.29).
3. **Recommended.** Conservative: `PC_JOIN_CONF=0.5` (N 100, `lib_max` < 0.7). Bolder: `PC_JOIN_CONF=0.7`, optionally
   with N 200 for the lists below 0.3 (`PC_JOIN_CONF_DEEP=0.3`, `PC_JOIN_N_DEEP=200`), which is the only place where the
   regime shows depth paying (+0.010 ± 0.005 on the ICEBERG prefix, +0.002 ± 0.004 zero-forward) and which puts 8% of
   the `c2` lists at N 200 instead of 72%. Section 3.
4. **Do not drop the `lib_max` condition.** The detector alone flags class-1-like lists with `lib_max` 0.7 to 0.9 (half
   of them at 0.5), class-1 exposure rises to 1.5 times the reference and the v1 side points the same way
   (−0.015 ± 0.010, 3 / 6; four right lists lose their first place instead of none). That band is where the
   leaderboard punished the gate at 0.8.
5. **What the detector depends on that may differ on the hidden test.** The public ranker's top score carries it, and
   that score contains the tie-order code: with the truth first among ties (every train-derived list as run) the same
   lists look more confident (AUC 0.87 instead of 0.82; enveda-np-examples lists flagged 9% instead of 20%). The fit
   and every number above use the emulation, so they are the pessimistic side of this. Not testable here: truth-absent
   lists of timsTOF natural products (no such runs exist; section 5).
6. **Runtime.** A second engine pass for flagged molecules only: about +5 to +7 s each on Kaggle, and −1.5 s for
   every unflagged molecule below the `lib_max` gate (no records, no injected rows). About **+10 to +20 minutes** on
   the 3 h 40 min run, +37 minutes if every list were flagged, +25 with the deep step. The literal notebook edit
   reproduces the study's lists for 24 of 24 molecules.

### 1.3 What could behave differently on the hidden test

- **Tie order (quantified).** The public ranker favours training structures among ties, so a train-derived list with
  the truth in the pool is more confident as run than a hidden class-2 list would be. Regime `c2` lists as run against
  the emulation `rk_c2`: first candidate right 80% against 62%; median `s1` 3.23 against 2.72 (truth-removed lists
  1.32); detector AUC 0.868 against 0.823; `c2` lists flagged at the threshold that flags 70% of the `dB` lists 19.8%
  against 26.3%. On enveda-np-examples: lists below `lib_max` 0.7 and flagged at 0.5, 9.2% as run against 19.6% under
  the emulation. A detector judged on lists as run would understate `c2` exposure by a quarter to a half. The fit and
  all tables use the emulation. A model fitted on lists as run is not worse when tested on emulated lists (0.842),
  so the leak is in the evaluation, not in the coefficients.
- **Class 1 is the other way round.** For a hidden class-1 molecule the truth is a training structure and the tie
  order helps it; class-1 lists are as run here and on the hidden test.
- **Fingerprint models.** The fit uses the fold-safe FPNet; the notebook's bank has seen every training structure.
  Only `t1_fp_cos` (small weight) reads a fingerprint quantity directly; `s1` contains them through the ranker. The
  one check available: enveda-np-examples with the public models (bank A never saw them) gives 20% / 29% / 47% of the
  `lib_max` gate's exposure at 0.3 / 0.5 / 0.7, against 12% / 23% / 40% in the regime. So on timsTOF natural products
  the same thresholds touch somewhat more truth-in-pool lists than the regime says, and still well under half.
- **Library relatives.** `t1_best_tan`, `t1_lib_max` and `s1` read analog evidence, which is easier on these hold-out
  molecules than on new natural products. Less analog evidence lowers `p_present` for every type: more lists flagged
  on both sides. The direction is known, the size is not.
- **`t1_lib_max` and class 1 below the gate.** A class-1 list with `lib_max` under 0.7 has a first candidate with a
  partial library match, which the model reads as "analog". Dropping the feature or weighting the 85 such fit lists
  up to 20 times does not lower class-1 exposure at a fixed `dB` share (`cg_explore3.py`): the `c1` lists that stay
  flagged are lists whose answer is not first (19% of those, 2.5% of the right ones at 0.5).
- **Not testable**: truth-absent lists on the test's instrument and chemistry. Every truth-removed run is a library
  compound on Orbitrap / QTOF (11% timsTOF, synthetic).

−0.006 ± 0.004 (0 / 6). N 200 runs exist only for lists below `lib_max` 0.7.

Reading.
- **What the gate removes** is the join on lists that were right and confident: 76% of the right `c2` lists are
  touched today, 10% at 0.5 and 19% at 0.7; the right lists that lose their first place on the v1 side go from 17 to
  4 and 7. The gross v1-side loss falls to a quarter and a half. On timsTOF natural products 13% of the right lists
  below the `lib_max` gate stay flagged at 0.5.
- **What it does not remove**: lists in which an injected row ends first. That share falls only from 4.2% to 3.4%
  (0.5) and not at all at 0.7, because those lists are the unconfident ones, where the pool answer was mostly not
  first anyway (43% of the `c2` lists with a wrong first candidate are flagged at 0.5). If the hidden damage is
  mainly "a correct first place is taken", the gate removes most of it; if it is "a pool answer at rank 3 to 10 is
  pushed down by a dozen injected rows", it removes less than the list count suggests. The regime cannot tell which.
- **N 200** touches the same lists as the reference and does more in them: injected rows in the final 25 +3%, an
  injected row first 4.2 → 6.3%, right lists losing the first place 4 → 12 (final) and 17 → 24 (v1 side), gross
  v1-side loss +46%. The direction of the leaderboard, not its size. Under the depth rule only 8% of the
  `c2` lists get N 200 and the counts stay at the level of the plain 0.7 gate, except "an injected row is first"
  (5.3% against 4.2%).
- **Class 1**: exposure is small in absolute terms once `lib_max` < 0.7 is on top (17% of the lists here, 10% on
  timsTOF natural products) and the gate halves it; it cannot be cut to a third without going down to 0.3.

Why 0.5 as the conservative point and not 0.3: from 0.5 to 0.3 the `dB` gain falls by 0.020 ± 0.006 (16 losses,

deep step first: its gain rests on 10 wins and 4 losses on the ICEBERG prefix, and depth is the one direction the
leaderboard has punished. A sensible order is 0.5 first; its result against 0.431 says how much damage N 100 is
paying today, which is the number nobody has.

## 5. What failed, what is weaker than it looks

- **Natural products.** AUC 0.73 against 0.95 for the other strata. The gate still cuts `c2` exposure on
  natural-product lists to 0.27 of the reference at 0.5 (20% against 72%) while keeping 79% of their `dB` gain, but
  the gain it gives up is mostly natural-product gain, the stratum closest to the hidden chemistry.
- **In distribution.** The regime's molecules come from the same sampling as the fit lists (other molecules, same
  libraries and instruments). The only out-of-distribution check is the flagged share of truth-in-pool lists on
  enveda-np-examples and valF. There is no truth-absent list of a timsTOF natural product anywhere in the local data,
  so "how many of the hidden answers outside the pool are flagged" is not measured. Row-deleted lists would say 87%
  of those below the `lib_max` gate at 0.5; they are an optimistic stand-in (section 1.1).
- **The first model comparison was wrong** (row-deleted lists in the fit made the LightGBM look clearly better). Found
  by checking the stand-in against real runs; an hour lost, conclusions reversed.
- **Injected rows on top** are hardly reduced (section 3). The gate is a tool against the join disturbing confident
  lists; it is not a ranker for joined lists.
- **N 200 only below `lib_max` 0.7**, the only depth with runs; N 150 and the `PC_N1=20000` lists were not combined
  with the gate. The gate is independent of both (it reads the list before any injection).
- **Not run**: the gate with `RANKER='blend'`; GLACIER; the gate on `dA` with ICEBERG beyond the prefix. `c1` has 150
  lists, 25 of them below the `lib_max` gate: every class-1 number of the gated rows is two to fourteen lists.
- **No leaderboard number** is given on purpose. What the leaderboard would test: whether N 100 at 0.431 is paying
  for exposure. If it is, the conservative point should score at or above 0.431 with 12% less `dB` gain; if it is
  not, it should score slightly below.
