# Is the hidden test a low-observation regime that our chain was not fitted for? (2026-10-09)

Scripts `scratch/lowobs/` (pre-registration `PREREG.md`, written before the first result on a subset), outputs
`D:\casmi-data\lowobs\`. CPU only: 2 engine workers and 1 engine-2 process below normal priority; **no GPU, no
training, no download, nothing uploaded, pushed or submitted, no solution code edited, nothing committed**; `INDEX.md`
not regenerated (other agents have uncommitted work in the tree). After the coordinator's correction the study stops
after step 3 unless the stated condition for step 4 holds; it does not hold.

## 0. Bottom line

1. **The host's sentence does not say what the hypothesis needs.** Topic 745715 (re-read 2026-10-09, signed out), Kaggle
   staff "inversion", in reply to a report that a notebook hard-coding the visible ids moved from 0.402 to 0.417:
   "There is no overlap in IDs between train and test, or between dummy test and rerun test. It's a relatively
   low-observation test set, so participants should be careful that they don't interpret noise with causation."
   In context "low-observation" = few scored molecules, a noisy leaderboard. It is not a statement about spectra per
   molecule, about how often a compound was seen in Enveda's samples, or about PubChem. It defines nothing further.
2. **What is documented about the hidden test's observation count** is on the data page: about 1,500 spectra for
   about 400 molecules (mean about 3.75), 1 to 16 per molecule, median 3. Its shape beyond that (share with one
   spectrum, adducts, polarities, energies per molecule) is not published and cannot be learnt from the visible file.
3. **There is no second regime mismatch of the kind proposed.** Every local set and every list our ranker was fitted
   on was built with ONE query library, the ten test adducts and at most 6 spectra per molecule
   (`scratch/ranker/sample_holdout.py`): mean 3.4 to 4.7, median 3 to 4, 21 to 30% of the hold-fold molecules with a
   single spectrum. The Enveda panel (enveda-np-examples, the test's instrument and pipeline) has mean 4.74, median 4,
   12% with one spectrum. The fingerprint nets are per-spectrum models (our training script feeds single spectra 70%
   of the time, `merge_p` 0.3). So the fitted components and the local evaluation are at the documented count of the
   test, not above it.
4. **Observation count does matter, by this much (causal: 204 mixed-regime molecules that have 4 to 6 query spectra,
   cut to one):** fingerprint score alone −23%; base list on joined lists: public ranker −0.065 / −0.076, ours −0.055 /
   −0.092, rank average −0.062 / −0.079 (class-2 / answer-removed lists; two seeds, each 2.9 to 4.0 SE); engine 2
   −0.084 ± 0.023; and the largest effect is on **class-1 lists: `lib_max` falls from a median of 1.00 to 0.90, the
   share below the 0.7 gate rises from 15% to 38%, the public ranker loses 0.121 ± 0.033**. With two spectra most of
   it is back for everything except the class-1 path (rank average −0.027 ± 0.016 / −0.032 ± 0.019, engine 2
   −0.012 ± 0.018, class-1 lists still −0.084 ± 0.026 and 29% below the gate). One adduct, one polarity or one
   collision energy: rank average −0.001 to −0.027, none beyond 1.3 SE.
5. **The order of the design choices does not change at low observation.** Rank average against public ranker
   (contrast 10): +0.032 → +0.039 / +0.030 and +0.073 → +0.067 / +0.071 at one spectrum; our ranker alone against the
   average (14): same sign and size. On the hold-fold molecules contrast 10 is positive in every observation stratum
   and list kind (+0.02 to +0.12), and contrast 14 has the same (leaderboard-wrong) sign for molecules with one
   spectrum as for those with three or more.
6. **The low-observation regime does not predict the leaderboard's signs better.** Molecules with at most 2 query
   spectra (LO, 216 / 211 molecules): 8 of 14 signs in the leaked chain against 8 on the full sets, 7 against 5 in
   the unseen chain; bootstrap of the difference −3 to +4 and −2 to +4 signs; Spearman +0.25 (−0.04 to +0.46) and
   −0.01 (−0.15 to +0.31). The pre-stated rule (two more signs in both chains and a Spearman interval that excludes
   zero in both) fails. One-spectrum molecules: 6 and 5 of 14. Plain statement: **no**.
7. **Step 4 was not run; nothing was exported.** Condition (a) fails (point 3) and the pre-stated steepness
   threshold of (b) fails as well (observational N1 − N3p of the rank average −0.041 and −0.017 against a threshold
   of −0.10; the causal loss at one spectrum, −0.06 to −0.08, is below it too). No dataset folder, no preset v58a,
   no notebook.
8. **What the study does leave** is a mechanism, not a candidate: for a molecule with one spectrum the library
   evidence is weaker for a physical reason (fewer chances that a query spectrum meets a library spectrum of a
   matching energy and adduct), so the fixed gates (0.7 join / rank average, 0.9 library tier) treat one in four
   class-1 molecules as "no library evidence". Whether an observation-dependent gate helps is a CPU-only question
   (section 6, item 1); it was not replayed here.
