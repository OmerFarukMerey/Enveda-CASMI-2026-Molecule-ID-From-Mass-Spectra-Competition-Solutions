# FRIGID as a source of structures that are in no database (2026-10-05)

Offline, inference only. Nothing was submitted, pushed or committed, no Kaggle notebook was run, no existing file was
edited, no spectrum or structure model was trained or fine-tuned. GPU work went through `train/gpushare.py`
(below-normal priority, `Share(duty=0.8, game_duty=0.25)`, a tick after every batch, exit on `must_yield()`), at most
two processes of 1.2 GB allocated each. Scripts and log: `scratch/frigid/` (git-ignored; `LOG.md`). Data and outputs:
`D:\casmi-data\frigid\` (about 6.5 GB). "±" is one standard error of a proportion. A local recall is not a leaderboard
gain; no leaderboard estimate is given.

**One thing in this study is fitted, and one pass was thrown away.** FRIGID starts each sample from a masked sequence
whose length comes from a small formula-to-length model that the authors did not publish. I replaced it by a
least-squares line on ten element counts (`fit_length.py`, CPU, seconds). My first version counted tokens with the
wrong converter setting and made every sequence about a fifth too short; a check on the authors' own benchmark exposed
it, the rule was corrected, and all generation runs were repeated. Only the second pass is reported (section 6).

## 0. The answer

**No. FRIGID as published does not reach our hidden natural products, and the reason is the one the MIST study
predicted.** With the **true** formula handed to it, 96 samples per molecule:

| Molecules (structure not in MassSpecGym) | n | answer among the generated | first place | our rule-based generator, same molecules |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A: no PubChem record** (natural-product libraries; the closest we have to "in no database") | 50 | **0 (0.0%; 95% upper bound 6%)** | 0 | **11 of 49 (22.4 ± 6.0%)** |
| B: PubChem record, timsTOF (enveda-180) | 36 | 0 | 0 | 0 of 36 |
| C: PubChem record, other instruments | 34 | 2 (5.9 ± 4.1%) | 1 | 3 of 34 |
| A + B + C | 120 | 2 (1.7 ± 1.2%) | 1 (0.8%) | 14 of 119 (11.8 ± 3.0%) |
| E: enveda-np-examples (timsTOF natural products) outside MassSpecGym | 10 | 1 (see 3.3) | 0 | not run |

With the formula guessed from our own list (the realistic case) the 119 honest regime molecules yield the same 2
answers, both molecules with a PubChem record; one is already in our final 25 at rank 6. **Answers gained for a final
list: 1 of 119, none of the 49 without a record.**

Where it does work, it has seen the structure: 8 of 12 enveda-np-examples structures that are in MassSpecGym (first
place for 7), 4 of 11 regime structures in MassSpecGym, and 6 of 18 spectra of its own benchmark's test fold (first
place for 5: 27.8 ± 10.6%; published 16%). The decoder is not the weak part: given the **true** fingerprint
it rebuilds 7 of 9 no-record natural products (6 in first place; 8 of 13 with four of E). The MIST encoder is: its predicted fingerprint has a
median Tanimoto of 0.30 to the truth on our honest molecules, 0.66 on its own test fold, 0.95 on structures it was
trained on.

Go / no-go per step: 1 set-up **go** (runs, provenance acceptable with one caveat); 2 formula **go** (not the
obstacle); 3 recall **no-go**; 4 list effect **no-go** (nothing to gain, decoys in every list); 5 Kaggle **possible
only cut down**, and pointless given 3.

## 3. Recall (step 3: NO-GO)

### 3.1 Molecules and adduct coverage

FRIGID's encoder accepts positive ions only (`[M+H]+`, `[M+Na]+`, `[M+K]+`, `[M+NH4]+`, `[M-H2O+H]+`,
`[M-2H2O+H]+`, `[M]+`); MassSpecGym holds the first two. Share of molecules with a usable spectrum:

| Set | molecules | a supported positive adduct | an `[M+H]+` spectrum |
|---|---|---|---|
| visible `test.parquet` (placeholder, drawn from train) | 400 | 93.3% | 91.5% |
| mixed regime | 380 | 87.1% | 77.9% |
| enveda-np-examples (timsTOF) | 250 | 92.8% | 84.0% |

One spectrum per molecule is built from all spectra of its preferred adduct (`[M+H]+` first), merged as upstream MIST
does. Test molecules (`prep_tasks.py`; "in MassSpecGym" = the structure's key is one of the 28,923 MassSpecGym
structures, any fold, as in the MIST study):

| | Subset | drawn | run |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | regime, no PubChem record, not in MassSpecGym | all 50 with a supported adduct | 50 (for one of them, a 520 Da bromine compound, the subformula table exceeded the size guard: no output, counted as a miss; the rule-based generator has a record for 49) |
| B | regime, record, not in MassSpecGym, timsTOF (enveda-180, synthetic) | all 36 | 36 |
| C | regime, record, not in MassSpecGym, other instruments | 34 of 89, random | 34 |
| D | regime, in MassSpecGym | 11 of 156, random | 11 |
| E | enveda-np-examples, not in MassSpecGym | all 10 | 10 |
| F | enveda-np-examples, in MassSpecGym | 12 of 222, random | 12 |

Settings: 96 samples per (molecule, formula) in batches of 32, stopping early at 25 distinct formula-matched
structures (the repository's benchmark default is at most 100 samples and 10 structures). Hit = the metric's key
(`tools/metric.ik14`, RDKit 2026.03.3) of a generated structure equals the truth's; rank = position among the
key-distinct structures in the generator's own order.

### 3.2 Is the pipeline right? The authors' benchmark first

18 random spectra of the MassSpecGym **test** fold (structures the encoder has not seen, Orbitrap / QTOF), true
formula, same code path (`bench_msg.py`): answer in first place **27.8 ± 10.6%** (5 of 18), among the first five 33.3%;
published top-1 16.1%. Fingerprint Tanimoto to the truth 0.66 in the median. So loader, features and length rule
behave like the published system, on a small sample. (With the first, wrong length rule the same spectra gave 0 of 11.)

### 3.5 Which half fails: the decoder with the true fingerprint

`run_gen.py --mist oracle` gives the decoder the true Morgan fingerprint and the true formula (not available on the
test): **7 of 9 no-record molecules of A regenerated, 6 in first place**; E: 1 of 4 (the other three are above 700 Da, where
96 samples give 0 to 4 structures with the right formula). The decoder can write these
natural products; the predicted fingerprint is what fails (0.30 against the 0.66 of the benchmark test fold and the
0.95 of trained structures). This is the MIST study's result again: retrieval with the MassSpecGym MIST found 33% of
unseen answers in the first 100 of a window against 74% for our FPNet. Caveat: the decoder's corpus was not filtered
(1.1), so some of these structures may have been pretraining strings.

Our FPNet cannot simply take MIST's place: it predicts 1,464 of the 4,096 Morgan bits (those cover 85% of a truth's
set bits), and its outputs are ranking scores, not calibrated probabilities; thresholded at the best cut-off chosen
on other molecules they reach a median Tanimoto of 0.15 to the truth on A (MIST 0.30). An encoder that would feed
this decoder has to be trained for it.

## 4. What it would do to a list (step 4: NO-GO)

No engine run. Lists: the v13a-like final 25 of section 2; generator: policy L2 (for a molecule whose first guessed
formula is wrong, that formula's run); "new" = a generated structure whose key is not already in our 25.

**(i) The generator's first k new structures written to the last k places of the 25** (`s3_eval.py`):

| Honest regime lists (policy L2) | lists | answer already in our 25 | k = 1 | k = 3 | k = 5 | k = 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A no record: answers gained / pushed out | 49 | 12 | 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 |
| B record, timsTOF: gained / pushed out | 36 | 24 | 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 | 0 / 0 |
| C record, other instruments: gained / pushed out | 34 | 19 | 1 / 0 | 1 / 0 | 1 / 0 | 1 / 1 |
| **A + B + C: gained / pushed out** | 119 | 55 | 1 / 0 | 1 / 0 | 1 / 0 | 1 / 1 |
| rank at which the gained answer lands | | | 25 | 23 | 21 | 16 |
| MRR change over the 119 lists | | | +0.0003 ± 0.0003 | +0.0004 ± 0.0004 | +0.0004 ± 0.0004 | +0.0001 ± 0.0007 |
| decoys added to a list that already holds the answer (mean) | | | 0.9 | 2.7 | 4.2 | 7.2 |
| our own candidates removed per list (mean) | | | 0.9 | 2.7 | 4.2 | 7.3 |
| for comparison, D (structures in MassSpecGym, 11 lists): gained | | 5 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 |

On the 119 honest lists one answer is gained, a 388 Da molecule **with** a PubChem record that the generator puts
first and that would sit at rank 21 to 25 (reciprocal rank 0.04 to 0.05); **on the 49 lists without a record nothing
is gained at any k**, because the answer is never among the generated structures. The cost is of the same size as
the gain: k places at the tail are taken from our own candidates (one answer at rank 16 to 25 is lost at k = 10), and
every list that already holds the answer receives k decoys. The generated structures are almost never candidates we
already have (0.07 of the first 25 per list), so they bring no confirmation either.

**(ii) Handed to the main engine as extra candidates, like the PubChem ones.** What the engine does with an outside
candidate that is the truth is known from the join (`s4_exposure.py`): of 160 truths injected as PubChem candidates
under the gate, 90.6% end in the final 25, 33.1% first, 69.4% in the top five, mean reciprocal rank 0.487 ± 0.031
(0.57 when it entered at PubChem rank 1 to 5, 0.26 beyond rank 25). An injected FRIGID answer would be worth about
that. The number of such answers is what section 3 measured: 0 of 49 without a record and 1 new answer in 119
honest lists (the other generated answer is already in our list at rank 6), i.e. about 0.004 of reciprocal rank per
honest molecule with a PubChem record at the very best, and nothing for the class the generator was meant for.

**Exposure.** FRIGID returns 11 distinct structures per formula in the median and almost all are new, so the join
would add about 11 rows per formula call, 18 per molecule under L2, to **every** list under the gate, where today
100 PubChem rows are joined. Lists under the gate (`lib_max` < 0.7): 72% of the truth-removed lists, 72% of the
class-2-like lists (answer a pool row without spectra) and 17% of the class-1-like lists of the regime. The one
measured price of extra outside rows above pool answers is the leaderboard pair 100 → 200 joined rows, 0.431 → 0.406;
18 more rows are a fifth of that step in number, placed by the same ranker. Against a gain side of zero this is
exposure without a case. At the tail positions of (i) the exposure is k decoys in every list and k of our own
candidates removed.

Its versions are not the ones tested here (transformers 4.56.2, safe-mol 0.1.13); the backbone imports private BERT classes of transformers, so 5.x is a risk |
| what we would have to package ourselves | (1) a wheel set of the versions in 1.2, installed with `--target` and put on the path of the FRIGID stage only, so that the image's transformers stays untouched for the other stages; (2) `tokenizer.json` of `datamol-io/safe-gpt` in a Hugging Face cache layout (the code loads it by repository name, offline); (3) the length rule (`length_rule.json`) or a trained length model; (4) `frigid_env.py` / `frigid_local.py` (feature builder, strict loader, generation loop), since the packaged source has no inference entry point; (5) optionally a slimmed checkpoint |
| licence | weights CC BY-NC 4.0: under the unanswered forum questions |

So it could be made to run in a cut-down form (one formula, 32 samples, gated), at one to three hours of T4 time.

## 6. What failed, and what can mislead

- **My length rule was wrong in the first pass** (token counts from `SAFEConverter(slicer=None)` instead of the
  default slicer the authors' length model was built with: 41 instead of 50 tokens in the median). 264 runs were
  discarded (`gen_shortlen\`). Their picture was the same (A: 1 of 50, A + B + C: 2 of 120), but they are not used.
  The corrected rule is still my substitute for an unpublished model: a better length model would raise the share of
  formula-matched samples (16% here), and with it the number of distinct structures per call, not the fingerprint.
- **The pipeline check is small**: 18 MassSpecGym test spectra, 27.8 ± 10.6% in first place against a published 16%.
  It shows the code is not broken; it does not calibrate it.
- **The no-record set is 50 molecules** from GNPS, MSnLib, RIKEN and similar libraries on Orbitrap and QTOF
  instruments, MIST's home ground, and one of them is timsTOF. The honest timsTOF molecules are synthetic (B) or ten
  (E). All of this favours FRIGID, and it still returns nothing.
- "Not in MassSpecGym" is a key test; close analogs and twins remain (the E hit).
- 96 samples, not the 128 of the repository's scaling default; the published benchmark uses at most 100.
- The decoder may have seen our hold-out structures as unlabeled strings; that favours FRIGID too.
- `mist_canopus.pt` (the NPLIB1 encoder, whose provenance is the weaker one) was run on 9 molecules of A only: 0
  hits, fingerprint Tanimoto 0.21 in the median, 6 distinct structures per call. It is not a way out.
- The Kaggle time figures are extrapolations from another card.
- Machine: `gpushare` ran at the game duty throughout (a desktop widget is counted as a game); another agent's
  training job shared the GPU from 03:48 on, after which only one process of this study ran at a time.
- `INDEX.md` was not regenerated (no existing file may be edited): run `python tools/gen_index.py` when this review
  is committed.

## 7. Recommendation

**Do not integrate FRIGID, and do not spend a submission on it.** With the true formula it finds none of 50 answers
that have no database record, where the rule-based generator we already run reaches 22% of the same molecules for a
third of a CPU second; with the realistic formula it adds one answer to 119 honest lists (a molecule with a PubChem record, at the tail)
and none to the 49 without a record; it would add about 18
outside rows to most lists, cost one to three hours of the T4 budget in its cheapest form, needs five things packaged
that no public dataset has, and its weights are non-commercial. For the generation lever the open item of the
next-levers review stands: the rule generator's products through the join (`gen_join`, 20 parents, support order).

What would change the verdict: an encoder that predicts the 4,096-bit fingerprint well on timsTOF natural products.
The decoder half is good (7 of 9 no-record molecules from the true fingerprint), so a fingerprint model trained on
our data with a calibrated output for all 4,096 bits could make the pair useful. That is a training project (the
training plan's territory, and the user is to be told before any training), not a packaging one, and the licence
question would have to be answered first.

## 8. Reproduce

```bash
P=.venv/Scripts/python; S=scratch/frigid; D=D:/casmi-data/frigid
kaggle datasets download -d kvswim/frigidckpts-casmi26 -p $D/ckpt --unzip
kaggle datasets download -d kvswim/frigidsrc-casmi26 -p $D/src --unzip
$P -m pip install --no-deps --target $D/site transformers==4.56.2 "tokenizers>=0.22,<0.23" "huggingface-hub<1.0" \
   safetensors regex filelock lightning==2.5.1 pytorch-lightning==2.5.1 lightning-utilities torchmetrics \
   bionemo-moco==0.0.2.1 jaxtyping wadler-lindig safe-mol==0.1.13 datamol selfies loguru win32-setctime colorama \
   hydra-core==1.3.2 omegaconf==2.3.0 antlr4-python3-runtime==4.9.3 einops
$P $S/inspect_ckpt.py                      # provenance.json: keys, hyper-parameters, md5
$P $S/fit_length.py                        # length_rule.json (also fetches the SAFE tokenizer into $D/hf)
$P $S/smoke.py --n 3                       # strict load, three molecules, time and memory
$P $S/s2_lists.py ; $P $S/prep_tasks.py ; $P $S/s2_formula.py       # final lists, tasks, window formulas, policy
$P $S/make_jobs.py                         # jobs_*.json: lists of [molecule, formula]; selection with random state 0
$P $S/run_gen.py --jobs q3 --shard 0/2 & $P $S/run_gen.py --jobs q3 --shard 1/2     # true formula, resumable
$P $S/run_gen.py --jobs pol2               # remaining policy formulas
$P $S/run_gen.py --jobs diag --mist oracle # decoder with the true fingerprint
$P $S/run_gen.py --jobs can --mist canopus # NPLIB1 encoder on nine molecules of A
# pipeline check: Zenodo 15008938 msg_preprocessed.tar.gz -> $D/msg (labels, split, 60 test spectra + subformulae)
$P $S/bench_msg.py --n 30
$P $S/s3_eval.py ; $P $S/s3_eval.py --mist oracle ; $P $S/s4_exposure.py
```

`s2_formula.parquet`, `policy.json`, `s2_lists.json`, `provenance.json`. Provenance for a README entry, should the
model ever be used: Zenodo 19685145 (CC BY-NC 4.0), `coleygroup/FRIGID@d242a2c` (Apache-2.0), decoder trained on the
`datamol-io/safe-gpt` SAFE corpus, MIST encoders on NPLIB1 and MassSpecGym per the authors (not verifiable from the
files), no NIST stated anywhere.
