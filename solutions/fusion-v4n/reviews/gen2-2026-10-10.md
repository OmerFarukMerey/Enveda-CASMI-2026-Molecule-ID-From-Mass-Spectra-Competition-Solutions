## 0. Bottom line

1. **Class N of DEV is the wrong chemistry and the best case at once.** Its 254 molecules are GNPS-library compounds
   that never entered PubChem: fatty and bile-acid amides, steroid conjugates, lipids (Fsp3 0.73, a carboxylic acid in
   55%, one terminal sugar in 254 molecules, 2% timsTOF). Their nearest known structure is close, **median Tanimoto
   0.85, above 0.8 for 62%, above 0.9 for 37%**, and it is a library compound, a homologue. The other truly-absent set
   (F: 290 record-less enveda-180 timsTOF molecules, synthetic) sits at **0.63**. Where a hidden novel natural product
   sits is not known.
2. **Reach is decided by that similarity, not by the generator or the budget.** One rule step from pool parents with the
   target formula: 66% of class N, 10% of F; 90 / 72 / 70% on the three natural-product leave-out proxies, 18% on
   drug-like class C. Pooled over the six sets: **1% / 4% / 22% / 49% / 83% / 87%** of the answers for a nearest
   neighbour below 0.5 / 0.5–0.6 / 0.6–0.7 / 0.7–0.8 / 0.8–0.9 / above 0.9.
3. **It is cheap.** With parents ranked by the fold-safe FPNet's f.z inside each formula window, the single best
   parent per window reaches 57% of class N with a **median of 42 candidates** (all parents: 66%, 401; 90% point
   3,929). A second step adds 2 to 16 points for 16 to 19 times the candidates (N: 80% with 6,264). Not knowing the
   formula costs a factor of 1.3 to 2.2 in parents.
4. **What the rules cannot reach, recombination can, at a cost nobody can pay yet.** A single BRICS crossover of two
   pool structures contains 95% of class N and 88% of F (rules, any step: 80% and 17%), among 40,000 to 450,000
   formula-matching piece pairs per molecule; choosing the donors by fingerprint cosine finds none of them. Sugar
   neutral losses are seen for 76% of glycosides and 17% of non-glycosides and do not fix the glycosylation pattern.
   A backward search confirms that two steps from all parents would add at most 2 points over two steps from 10.
5. **The fingerprint network cannot rank what is generated; the parent prior can.** In the pre-declared order (parent
   rank, step, support) the answer is among the first 25 structures for 50.8% of class N (panel 67%, P 41%, F 7.6%)
   and is placed at an MRR of 0.54: **expected value 0.276 per class-N molecule**, 0.58 / 0.30 / 0.28 on the
   leave-outs, **0.012 on F**. f.z over the first 300 / 1,000 / 2,000 structures gives 0.173 / 0.138 / 0.130 on class
   N: more candidates, less score. A LightGBM on all fingerprint-level features is no better than f.z.
6. **The pipeline is not at zero on class N.** The engine's own generator already lists 21.7% of class N, and the
   ranker places those answers at 0.72: 0.156 per molecule. 137 of the 186 class-N answers scored here are new to it.
7. **Generated rows hurt database answers unless they enter low or through the ranker.** Free competition under f.z:
   −0.04 to −0.14 per database answer, 7 to 15 generated rows among the first 25. One generated row at the top of
   the production list: −0.35. Five rows from position 6: −0.003. The only gate feature that separates class N from
   pool and PubChem answers is the ranker's top score (AUC 0.85 / 0.80); "no library hit" does not (0.51 / 0.59).
8. **Net** (formula: w_N × gain_N + (1 − w_N) × harm): five rows from position 6, no gate: **+0.003 / +0.008 / +0.013**
   at an assumed hidden class-N share of 0.15 / 0.30 / 0.43 for chemistry like DEV's class N, at most +0.002 with F's
   reach. Rows placed by the ranker (assumed placement 0.36 to 0.72 of a listed answer, cost −0.012 from `genjoin`):
   −0.003 … +0.027 / +0.007 … +0.067 / +0.015 … +0.10; with F's reach −0.006 … +0.017.
9. **No public generative model is both usable under the rules and useful** (section 7).
10. **Verdict: go for one bounded build** (one step + moves from FPNet-ranked formula-window parents, at most 25 rows
   per molecule, handed to the ranker with features of their own; about 20 to 25 minutes on Kaggle), **no-go for a
   learned generator, a second step or thousands of candidates** until a scorer exists that ranks 1,000 isomers at
   MRR ≥ 0.46 (f.z: 0.19). First experiment: section 8.4. The DEV candidate sets for the joint model:
   `D:\casmi-data\gen2\gen_cands_dev.parquet` (1,023,591 rows, 904 molecules), evaluator `train/gen/gen_eval.py`.
## 1. Evaluation sets, and how realistic each is

All molecules are from the DEV half of the joint benchmark (`reviews/joint0-2026-10-09.md`; FINAL was not read) or lie
outside the benchmark (set F). The fold-safe FPNet (`fpnet_fold0.pt`) has seen none of them. Its logits were recomputed
here on CPU from the benchmark's query spectra, aggregated as the v1 engine does (0.5 × (mean single + mean merged));
my f.z reproduces the stored `fz` column of the benchmark on 40 lists (median correlation 1.000, minimum 0.991) **[M]**.

Parents are **pool rows only** (710,701 structures: every training structure and COCONUT). The PubChem tier was not used
as a parent source: its 105.9 M rows have no fingerprints on disk and a formula window holds thousands of rows; whether
tier parents add reach was not measured.

**How much easier the leave-out is than reality.** Three measured facts and one judgement:
- The answer of a leave-out molecule is a known compound, and known compounds come in families that the catalogues
  hold completely. Lpanel answers have a median of 15.5 distinct pool parents that produce them, P 5, N 3, F and C 1
  (section 3.3). A new natural product has, by definition, at least one relative fewer than a catalogued one: itself
  is how its neighbours got their neighbour.
- Removing parents above a Tanimoto of 0.7 to the answer takes Lpanel's one-step reach from 90% to 73%, P's from 70% to
  29%, N's from 66% to 27% (section 3.5). The panel barely reacts because glycosylation, acylation and the like change
  a Morgan fingerprint a lot: the "purge" removes look-alikes, not biosynthetic neighbours. It is not a realistic
  novelty simulation for the panel either.
- `docs/competition/rules-and-pitfalls.md` (pitfall 6) records that "delete the truth and regenerate it" overstates
  reach about 3×; `genjoin` found a factor of 1.2 to 1.4 at purge 0.8 and 2 to 3 at 0.7 **[C]**.
- **Judgement, not a measurement**: the panel's 90% is an upper bound that I would divide by 2 to 3 for a hidden novel
  natural product (30 to 45% one-step reach), and class N's 66% is an upper bound of a different kind: its chemistry
  (homologous lipids and amides) is the best case for methyl / H2 rules. The honest lower end is set F (10%).
  **The hidden answers' similarity to the nearest known structure is not known and decides which end applies.**

## 3. Rule-based analog enumeration, formula-constrained (`train/gen/rules.py`, `g2_enum.py`, `g7_reach.py`, `out/reach.md`) [M]

### 3.1 What was enumerated

- **Rules**: 66 one-reactant reaction SMARTS with their formula deltas: methylation and demethylation at O, acid O, N,
  aromatic C and sp3 C, CH2 insertion into and deletion from a chain (12 rules); hydroxylation at aromatic and sp3 C,
  epoxide, N-hydroxyl and the removals (7); hydrogenation of C=C and C=O, dehydrogenation, alcohol oxidation (4);
  acetylation at O and N and removal (4); glycosylation with hexose, deoxyhexose, pentose (pyranose and furanose) and
  glucuronic acid at O (alcohol, phenol, sugar hydroxyl), at an acid O, and at an aromatic C, and the removals (17);
  malonylation (2); prenylation at C and O (4); sulfation (2); dehydration and hydration (2); and, beyond the task's
  list, methoxy as one unit (2) and five common acyl groups with removals (10; coumaroyl, feruloyl, caffeoyl, galloyl,
  benzoyl). Every matching site is used (up to 64 products per rule and parent).
- **Step '1'**: one rule on every pool row whose formula is target − delta (at most 1,500 rows per formula window,
  never reached).
- **Step 'move'** (positional isomerisation): a substituent removed and re-attached on a same-formula pool row (methyl,
  hydroxyl, methoxy, each sugar, acetyl, malonyl, prenyl, sulfate, a double bond: 61 removal / attachment pairs), at
  most 500 parents, 40 s.
- **Step '2'**: any two rules in either order (net-zero pairs excluded) from pool rows whose formula is target − both
  deltas, **restricted to the 10 best parents of each formula window** by the fold-safe FPNet's f.z for this spectrum,
  60 s per molecule (cap hit for 0 to 9% of a set's molecules).
- The target formula is taken as known (cost of that assumption: 3.6). Products are stereo-free canonical SMILES of
  single-component molecules with exactly the target formula; structures that are pool rows are dropped (they are
  candidates already). A hit = the product is the answer or one of its enumerated tautomers; on the scored subsets the
  metric key confirms it (section 5).
- **Parent order, oracle-free**: inside each formula window the parents are ranked by f.z (the stored pool fingerprint
  times the logits the FPNet predicts from the query spectra; no fingerprint has to be computed). "M" below = only the
  M best parents of each window are used.

## 5. The scoring problem (`g6_score.py`, `g8_eval.py`, `out/eval.md`, `out/misc.md`) [M]

**Budgeted sets.** For every molecule the new structures were put in one pre-declared order, fixed before any scoring:
best parent rank in its formula window first (rank 1 parents of every window, then rank 2, ...), inside a rank one step
before move before two steps, then the number of distinct parents that produce the structure, then the parent's f.z.
The first 2,000 (class N) or 1,000 (other sets) were fingerprinted with the pool's 10,226-bit definition and keyed with
the metric key. Distinct metric keys per distinct SMILES: 0.950 to 0.977 (2 to 5% of a generated set are tautomer
twins). Where the tautomer canonicalisation took more than 40 ms per structure on the first eight, the plain InChIKey
first block was used for the rest of that molecule (3 to 24% of a set's molecules, the large amide- and sugar-rich ones;
one peptide-like molecule needed 0.85 s per key); an answer matched through its tautomer set carries the answer's key.

**Scorers, all of which exist today.** `order`: the pre-declared order itself (no fingerprint of the candidate needed).
`fz`: the fold-safe FPNet's f.z. `fz_norm`: f.z / √bits. `fpR`: a LightGBM lambdarank on the fingerprint-level features
only (f.z, f.z / √bits, log-likelihood, cosine, Tanimoto, bit count and their list-relative forms), fitted out of fold
by scaffold on the 1,509 DEV pool lists: the part of our ranker that exists for a generated row. On its own training
kind of list it does not beat f.z (MRR 0.528 against 0.542, row level): **the fingerprint-level features hold no more
than f.z**. `ord+fzn`: rank average of `order` and `fz_norm`, added after the first table was seen (post hoc).

### 6.3 Slot policies and the net expected effect

positions pos .. pos + k − 1; database rows from pos on move down by k. Class N gains 1 / (pos − 1 + g) when its answer
is generated row g ≤ k, **minus what the engine's own generator already gave it** (its rank in the stored `dropJ` list);
a database answer at production rank r ≥ pos moves to r + k; a gate applies the policy to passing molecules only.

- **Generated rows at the top of the list are a loss at every assumed share** (break-even w_N 0.8 without a gate, 0.5

  −0.000 … +0.008, +0.005 … +0.017**.

**Verdict: go for one bounded build, no-go for everything larger.** Build the one-step (+ move) generator with
FPNet-ranked formula-window parents as a replacement for the engine's analog-parent generator, hand at most 25 rows per
molecule to the ranker with their own features (parent rank in its window, number of producing parents, step, rule
group, parent f.z, parent Tanimoto), and decide on the benchmark. Do not build a de novo generator, do not enumerate two
steps, do not spend the 60 minutes on fingerprints of thousands of isomers: the measured scorers turn more candidates
into less score.

What the verdict rests on and what it does not: the reach and scoring numbers are local and measured; **the hidden
chemistry is not known**. DEV's class N is homologous lipids and amides whose nearest known structure has a median
Tanimoto of 0.85 and is a library compound; the one other truly-absent set (F) is at 0.63 and gains nothing. If hidden
novel natural products have a known relative above 0.8 as often as the Enveda panel's famous compounds do (64%), the
build pays; if they sit at 0.6 to 0.7, it returns a sixth to a ninth of what it returns above 0.8 (value of the first 25 rows
0.056 per molecule against 0.35 to 0.51, section 5.2). Nothing local can tell which.

### 8.2 Which generator first

1. **One step from the M ≤ 3 best parents of each formula window, plus positional moves from the M ≤ 3 best
   same-formula pool rows**; parents ranked by f.z from the stored pool fingerprints; rules of `train/gen/rules.py`
   (with the sp3 C-methyl and CH2 insertion / deletion rules that `casmi.derive` lacks); formula not fixed but taken
   from the mass window as `casmi.derive` does (cost ×1.3 to ×2.2 in parents, section 3.6; its effect on the order not
   measured). Output: the first 25 by the pre-declared order.
2. Glycosylation rules only when a sugar neutral loss is seen (section 4.3): a cost measure for the 2,600-candidate
   glycoside sets, to be measured before use.
3. Not now: two steps (only with a scorer that reaches MRR ≥ 0.46 given generated on 1,000 isomers, section 5.4);
   any download.
4. A measurement, not a build: BRICS recombination with **pieces** selected by the spectrum (a peak or a neutral
   loss at the piece's mass). It is the only route found here to chemistry without a close relative (88% of F is
   inside it), and it is 10^5 candidates per molecule until such a selection exists. The index of 6.4 M piece
   occurrences is on disk (`scratch/gen2/cache/brics`, 8 minutes on five workers to rebuild).

### 8.4 The smallest experiment that would prove a gain on the benchmark

Engine replay on DEV, three lists per molecule, our ranker refitted out of fold by scaffold with the four new
generator features (the `as_feature` machinery of `train/joint/bench.py`):
- **arms**: (a) the stored lists (the engine's own generator); (b) its generated rows replaced by the first 25 of the
  new order; (c) both.
- **sets**: class N of DEV (254; answer removed from the pool, 100 joined PubChem rows present), set F (290), and the
  database views `c2J` and `dropJ` of DEV as they are (the cost side).
- **pass**: class-N MRR@25 per molecule above arm (a)'s 0.156 by more than 2 SE (SE of a paired difference on 254
  molecules is about 0.02: so ≥ +0.04, where the formula of section 6.3 allows +0.05 to +0.25); F not below arm (a);
  `c2J` and `dropJ` not below arm (a) by more than 1 SE (`joint0`: 0.009 and 0.012). Pre-registered as in
  `scratch/joint1/PREREG.md`; FINAL once at the end.
- **cost**: 254 + 290 + about 1,600 engine runs at 2 to 3 s extra each, CPU only, about an hour on four workers; no GPU,
  no download.
- If it passes: one leaderboard probe with five generated rows from position 6 (the low-risk slot policy; the user
  decides on every submission). The probe's expected size by the formula of 6.3 is +0.003 to +0.013 for chemistry like
  DEV's class N, which one submission barely resolves; a larger effect would itself be evidence about the hidden
  chemistry.
- A cheaper first look that needs no engine run: when the joint model of phase 1 has a checkpoint,
  `train/gen/gen_eval.py --scores <its scores on gen_cands_dev.parquet>` against the thresholds of section 5.4.
## 9. The candidate file for the joint model

## 10. Not verified, limits, mistakes

- **The hidden chemistry and its similarity to known structures.** Every transfer from these sets to the leaderboard
  rests on it; nothing local measures it. The three shares of class N (0.15, 0.30, 0.43) are assumptions of the task.
- **The new rows were not run through the engine or the ranker.** The "through the ranker" lines of sections 5.3, 6.3
  and 8 use the placement of the engine's own generated answers (55 lists) and `genjoin`'s costs; they are formulas.
  The fused final list (popularity, forward models, engine 2) was not replayed; `genjoin` found the engine-2 fusion
  pushes generated rows down.
- **Formula.** Everything in sections 3 to 6 assumes the true formula. Section 3.6 measures the price in parents and
  the top-k of a spectrum-free formula prior; the order under several formulas was not enumerated.
- **Parents are pool rows only.** The PubChem tier (105.9 M rows) as a parent source was not tried; nearest-neighbour
  similarity was computed against the pool, not the tier.
- **Two steps** were enumerated forwards only from the 10 best parents of each window, with a 60 s cap (hit for up to
  9% of a set); section 4.1 bounds the rest backwards under caps of its own.
- **Hits are structure matches through the answer's enumerated tautomers** (at most 500); a product whose canonical
  tautomer equals the answer's but which is not among those is missed (an undercount). On the scored subsets the metric
  key was computed, with a fallback to the plain InChIKey for 3 to 24% of the molecules of a set.
- **Leave-out proxies are easier than reality** (section 1); the Tanimoto purge is not a novelty simulation for
  glycosides (section 3.5).
- The gate features of class N come from lists scored with the answer present and then removed; `s1` thresholds are
  in-sample medians; the pass rates and policy nets are in-sample on these 904 molecules. `ord+fzn` is post hoc.
- Class composition weights of the non-N part (0.515 / 0.333 / 0.152) are DEV's natural mix, not the hidden one; class
  L is represented by catalogue rows only (the 40 non-catalogue L molecules of DEV were not sampled).
- The acyl-removal rules also match a substituted leaving group (`decoumaroyl_O` on a feruloyl ester); such products
  have another formula and are dropped by the formula check, so no hit is created by it, but the rule table is
  looser than its names (found by the test written afterwards; the rules were not changed after the runs).
- Compound classes are SMARTS heuristics; the sugar-loss detector uses one decoy offset and a fixed 0.005 Da.
- Kaggle run time: nothing was timed on Kaggle; local timings were taken beside other jobs. The 2.55 ms per fingerprint
  is `joint0`'s figure (2.5 to 3.6 ms here). The M ≤ 3 enumeration time is scaled from candidate counts, not timed.
- Section 7 is from two local documents and from memory; cells marked [mem] were not re-verified (no network).
- `fpR` was fitted on pool lists only (no generated rows in its training lists); a ranker trained with generated rows
  might use the fingerprint features differently. Not tried.
- BRICS pair counts ignore that some formula-matching pairs give the same structure; the all-donor count is from a
  sample of molecules.

**Mistakes of mine.**
- **Process limit breached twice.** A kill command for the first enumeration run did not kill it; for about four
  minutes ten enumeration workers plus one single-process job of mine ran (CPU at 96%) before I noticed and stopped
  the old five. Later, single-process analysis jobs ran beside five workers several times (six processes, the limit),
  one of them with two BLAS threads.
- The first enumeration run had no time cap inside a parent and stalled on triglycerides; it was stopped and restarted
  with caps (60 s one step, 40 s moves, 60 s two steps). Class-N files written by the first run (no caps, none
  truncated) were kept.
- I deleted `gen/*.tmp` once while workers were running; no worker was between write and rename (the run continued),
  but it could have killed one.
- A driver script waited on a process check that matched its own command line and never started; found after six
  minutes. A first BRICS index was empty (property cache not updated on unsanitised fragments) and was rebuilt.
- The first version of the evaluation crashed on a pandas attribute (`Series.view`); no number of it was used.
