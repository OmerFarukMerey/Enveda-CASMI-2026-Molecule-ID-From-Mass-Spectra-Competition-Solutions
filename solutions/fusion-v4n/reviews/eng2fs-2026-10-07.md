## 4. Why: megayak's rows carry the answer's position

`ef_diag.py`, `ef_diag2.py`, `ef_tie.py`. Ranker alone (no blend, raw candidates), the same out-of-fold models, the same 226
molecules:

| Scored on | megayak ranker `u` | public ranker |
|---|---|---|
| megayak's own class-2 rows of the molecule | **0.722** | 0.641 |
| our class-2 record, megayak logits (coherent) | **0.594** | 0.638 (mismatched) |
| our class-2 record, public logits | 0.585 (the audit's incoherent case) | 0.635 |
| class-1: megayak's rows / our record | 0.825 / 0.826 | 0.845 / 0.842 |

The public ranker scores the two versions of a molecule alike; megayak's ranker loses 0.13 on class-2 lists when the same
molecule comes from our records, with the same FPNets. Column by column the answer rows agree except two: rank-normalised
library similarity (column 1: 0.435 in his rows, 0.759 in ours) and rank-normalised MetFrag (column 22: 0.222 against
0.313). Both are ranks over tied values. A class-2 answer has library similarity 0, like about 73% of its list, and
`pv._rank_norm` (`argsort` of the negated values) hands tied rows ranks in the order they stand in:

- answer's relative position in its group (0 = first row, 1 = last): megayak's rows **0.26 to 0.32 in every query set, both
  simulations** (median 0.20 to 0.26; first row 8%); prvsiyan's `rank_train.npz` 0.47; our panel records 0.60, mixed regime
  0.55 (in both the answer is a training-structure row, which the pool sorts behind COCONUT rows of the same mass);
- within a group of megayak's rows, among the rows with library similarity 0, position and the rank-normalised column
  correlate 0.96 (median).

So the ranker fitted on megayak's rows learns that a tied row standing early is the answer, his own rows reward it, and our
records, where the answer stands late, punish it. Test: the five rank-normalised columns (1, 6, 16, 19, 22) recomputed with
average ranks on ties, in his rows and at scoring, the ranker re-fitted per fold (`cft_fold<k>.joblib`):

| Class-2, ranker alone, n 226 | as published | position-free ties | difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| megayak ranker on megayak's own rows | 0.722 | 0.670 | **−0.051 ± 0.013 (20 / 57)** |
| megayak ranker on our records (megayak logits) | 0.594 | 0.651 | **+0.057 ± 0.012 (73 / 13)** |
| … against the public ranker on our records (0.635) | −0.041 | +0.016 ± 0.012 (44 / 36) | |
| … the same position-free ranker fed the public logits | | 0.645 | −0.006 ± 0.009 (34 / 33) against megayak logits |
| public ranker on our records, position-free inputs only | 0.635 | 0.636 | +0.002 ± 0.005 |
| megayak ranker against the public ranker, both on megayak's rows | +0.081 | +0.030 ± 0.013 (49 / 29) | |

Class-1 (the answer has a library match, few ties): position-free −0.006 ± 0.009 on his rows, −0.013 ± 0.010 on ours.

- Half of the megayak ranker's advantage on his own rows is the position. The remaining +0.030 ± 0.013 is measured on
  features of his FPNets, where the public ranker is the mismatched one.
- **The audit's reading "each ranker is better on the features of the FPNets its rows were made with" is superseded**: with
  position-free ties the megayak ranker is indifferent to the FPNet source (0.651 against 0.645). What followed the rows
  was their row order.
- Which position the answer has in the hidden test is not known here. In the notebook the candidates stand in pool order
  (mass, then COCONUT, ChEBI / LIPID MAPS, training structures); a hidden class-2 answer is a COCONUT row. Our records
  (answer late) are pessimistic for a position-reading ranker and megayak's rows (answer early) optimistic; neither is the
  hidden test's order. Why his rows have the answer early is not stated in his README or notebooks.
- Consequence for the pipeline as it is (not measured): engine 2's second ranker (weight 0.12) is fitted on megayak's
  `sim_rank_rows_nofp.npz`, same groups and order, and reads the same tied columns plus `libx`'s. Every local engine-2
  number of ours, including the audit's, carries its position reading on records where the answer stands late. The public
  pair-tail (V44) is fitted on `sim_rank_rows_fppair.npz`, all 39 columns.

## 7. Honesty and limits

- **Seen / unseen per comparison.** Section 3: both FPNet pairs unseen (megayak's by his statement plus the `f.z`
  measurement), every ranker out of fold (4 models per fold for the main rankers instead of the notebook's 8, 2 for the
  second), 226 of 250 molecules (24 not found in both row files). Section 4, rows of megayak: his FPNets unseen, rankers
  out of fold; the public ranker there reads features of FPNets it was not fitted with. Mixed regime: used only in
  section 1, both pairs have seen its molecules.
- A molecule is matched to a row group by one float (the answer's MetFrag value); a fold removes a few extra groups that
  share a value (audit, section 4).
- Candidate lists differ between his rows and our records (median 55 against 63), so "the same molecule" is the same
  spectra, answer and FPNets, not the same decoys.
- `pv._rank_norm` uses NumPy's default `argsort`, which is not a stable sort by contract; the position dependence is
  measured (correlation 0.96), not derived.
- The position-free refit and the split by starting rank were chosen after seeing the first result; nothing rests on them
  except the explanation.
- **megayak's statements are his**: the hold-out of 3,150 structures, from-scratch training, the README numbers. The
  training set of his FPNets is implied, not stated.
- **Not done**: step 3; megayak's logits in the mixed-regime lists (`ef_lists.py` arms `m`, `av` exist, not run); the
  39-column and pair-tail rankers; any Kaggle run; `EXPERIMENTS.md` / `INDEX.md`.
- **Failed or corrected on the way**: a first console print died on the Windows code page (rerun with UTF-8); a check that
  read column 31 of the `nofp` rows as a mass error was wrong (that file has no mass block) and is not used; the first
  stage table compared only `mcf` / `avcf`, and the 0.13 gap between his rows and our records for the same molecules is
  what led to section 4.
