# Audit of the fusion-v4n test harness (2026-10-02)

Question: the local harness has repeatedly mispredicted the public leaderboard. Are the tests, the metric or the code wrong?

Short answer: the metric, the submitted configs, `fusion_core.py`, `tools/refuse.py` and the popularity port are correct.
The validation sets and the calibration target are what is wrong. Four harness defects are confirmed, the validation
sets are structurally blind to the PubChem channel and dominated by compound fame, and the leaderboard differences the
harness is asked to predict are within about two standard errors of a ~132-molecule public sample.

Everything below was run, not read. Scripts are in `scratch/audit/` (git-ignored). Environment: `.venv`, RDKit
`2026.03.3`, `CASMI_DATA=D:\casmi-data`. No Kaggle submission, push or notebook run was made; the CLI was used only
for `kernels pull` and `kernels output`. The auditor made no commit and modified no existing file.

Audited state: commit `be52945`. While the audit ran, `ff09fca` added `FUSE_MIN_LIB` (default `None`) to `fusion_core.py`;
that knob is outside this audit, but the three fusion tests and the exact re-fusion of `valc1` were repeated at
`ff09fca` and still pass.

## D. PubChem-popularity port: code PASS, data CANNOT VERIFY

`python scratch/audit/extract_wph.py` extracts `CORE`, `V17_PATCH`, `RUNNER` and the engine files from
`scratch/wph/code.py`; they were diffed against `nb/embed/` and against the source that actually ran on Kaggle for v5a/v5c.

Every difference between our port and the source, with the prior on:

1. `init_worker` / `probe_one` are named `init_worker_pop` / `probe_one_pop` and swapped in at import when
   `CASMI_POP_DIR` is set and `CASMI_POP_LAM > 0`. Body of `probe_one_pop`: identical line for line.
2. Ours adds a guard: the prior is switched off, with one printed line, if the arrays are not the tier's length. The
   v5a/v5c commit logs contain no such line, `fz_top` is present and equals the plain run's top f.z for 12/12 molecules,
   and the lists differ from the plain channel (13-22 of 25 keys in common), so the prior was on.
3. `PC_N1` is read from the environment (default 5000 = source). `pc_runner.py`: `(d or {}).get('fz_top')`. No effect.
4. The environment variables are passed to the PubChem subprocess only (source sets `os.environ`). No effect.
5. Pool popularity failing to load is swallowed (prior off); the source would stop on its `assert`. Logs show it loaded
   (710,701 rows).
6. GLACIER [M+H]+ filter in `nb/07_rescore.py`: the same two statements as the source.
7. **Where the pool prior and the `|gen` tag are applied.** Source: in the base-list cell, before `ice_candidates`.
   Ours: in `fusion_core`, after ICE/GLACIER have scored candidates chosen with untagged formulas. Consequences:
   - Groups in the stage-A re-rank are the same in both (tagged). Stage C uses untagged formulas in both.
   - Ours sends a superset of candidates to ICE/GLACIER: members of a formula group that has at least two members only
     when pool and generated candidates are counted together. Measured with `python scratch/audit/d_ice_candidates.py`:
     **4 of 19,712** candidates (val1) and **9 of 20,010** (valc1). The extra scores are used only in stage C.
   - Final lists differ in 2 of 250 (val1) and 2-4 of 250 (valc1) molecules, top-1 in at most 1; local MRR
     +0.003 ± 0.003 and +0.002 ± 0.002 in our favour. Runtime cost of the superset: nil.
8. Data: v29 mounts two private datasets (the two blank `dataset_sources`); ours mounts
   `dmitriigluzdov/casmi26-pubchem-popularity-prior`, which sums counts over all CIDs sharing a stereo-free structure
   (tier) or connectivity block (pool). Whether wangpenghua's arrays use the same aggregation and scale is unknown.
9. Docker image: ours pins the v4n reference image, v29 another one. v1 reproduced 0.399 on ours.

Conclusion: the code is not why v5a scored 0.393. What remains is the data (item 8), which cannot be checked, and
noise: 0.415 − 0.393 is 2.9 reciprocal-rank units on ~132 public molecules (G1), and 0.415 itself is second-hand
("reported by a teammate from the page", `docs/landscape/public-notebooks.md`).

## F. Validation design: FAIL

Scripts: `f_validation.py`, `f_twins.py`, `f_instruments.py`, `f_topn_replay.py`, `f_shuffle.py`, `f_pc_blind.py`,
`tools/calibrate.py runs/val1 runs/valc1`.

### Confirmed code defects

**F1. The purge uses the train `inchikey14`, which is not the metric key.** `build_validation` (`nb/02_smoke.py`) drops
train rows by `inchikey14`; the metric matches on the tautomer-canonical key. 16 of 250 val1/valc1 labels have a metric
key different from the stored `inchikey14`, and **14 of 250 held-out answers keep a tautomer twin in the "purged"
library** (15 other `inchikey14`s, 490 spectra). valF (local build of `fold0_np`): **27 of 244**, 833 spectra, and 2
of its molecules repeat another molecule's metric key. On val1 the twins score 0.857 against 0.613 for the rest (n = 14, noisy).
Fix: purge every train row whose `chem.score_key(normalized_smiles)` is a held-out metric key, and draw molecules by
metric key.

**F2. valF relabels foreign spectra as timsTOF.** With `lib=None` the test spectra come from any library, and
`q['instrument_type'] = 'timsTOF'` overwrites the truth. For the `fold0_np` set 99.9% of the candidate spectra are
riken 49%, pluskal 12%, mona 11%, gnps 10%, massbank 9% (Orbitrap 34%, QTOF 22%, ...); enveda-180 contributes 0.1%.
The hidden test is 100% Bruker timsTOF, and the instrument string is passed to ICEBERG/GLACIER. Fix: restrict to
`instrument_type == 'timsTOF'` rows or keep the original instrument and treat valF as an out-of-domain set.

**F3. `regime_eval.py` result cache ignores `PC_POP_LAM` and `GL_MH_ONLY`.** The cache key is built from
`fusion_core.DEFAULTS` keys only, but `refuse.run_one` uses those two keys to choose `pc_pop.json` / `gl_mh.json`.
The v4b and v5c overrides hash to the same key (`693a1c13aca46b97`), so whichever is evaluated second returns the
other's cached result, within one call too. Shown end to end by `python scratch/audit/f_regime_cache_bug.py` on a copy
of val1 with synthetic extras: cache on, v5c = v4b exactly (0.6245, 0 lists changed); cache off, v5c 0.6210 with 162
of 250 lists changed. `pc_pop` and `gl_mh` are also missing from `STAGES`, so replacing them does
not invalidate the cache; nor does a change of the popularity arrays, `fuse.py` or `gl_fuse.py`. `calibrate.py` and
`compare_configs.py` do not use this cache. Fix: hash every key `run_one` reads and the extra stage files.

**F4. The isomer shuffle runs on the 80-deep cache and is then cut at `TOPN=60`.** The truth's same-formula group has
a median of 64 (val1) and 76 (valc1) members, so after the shuffle the truth sits beyond rank 60 in **13.9% and 16.4%**
of molecules and no stage-A term can recover it. Fix: truncate to the replay `TOPN` before shuffling.

### Design flaws, measured

**F5. `TOPN: 60` replay of a `TOPN=80` cache is not a real `TOPN=60` run.** Stages A and B are identical (base lists are
prefix-identical; popularity and re-ranks act on the first 60). Stage C is not: engine-2 candidates whose key sits at base
rank 61-80 were scored under the base SMILES string in the 80 run, and a real 60 run would score them under the engine's
string. The replay finds no score for them (z = 0): 337 candidates in 101/250 molecules (val1), 475 in 132/250 (valc1),
99 in 62/400 (vis); one of them is in the final top-25 of 38, 57 and 8 molecules. Never the truth, so the bias is bounded
but not zero. Dump validation caches at the submission `TOPN`.

**F6. The validation sets cannot see the PubChem channel or generated candidates.** In val1, valc1 and vis the answer is
a pool row flagged as a train structure in 100% of molecules; it is in a PubChem-only list in **0** of them (those lists
exclude pool keys by construction) and is never a generated candidate. The channel fills 4.9 of 25 slots in 238/250 val1
lists; deleting it changes MRR by −0.001 ± 0.007 (v1) and +0.005 ± 0.007 (v4b). So `PC_POP_LAM`, `PC_POP_UNION`, the
slot tables, `REL_TH` and `fz_top` have no measurable local effect, and a v5a/v5c replay measures `GL_MH_ONLY` only. The
part of v5 that the post-mortem blames is exactly the part the harness cannot evaluate. A class-3 validation is missing:
hold out structures and also remove them from the pool keys so the answer is reachable only through PubChem.

**F7. Fame dominates val1/valc1.** Popularity of the answers: val1 median 13.1 (99% above the pool's 90th percentile),
valF 5.3 (61%), visible test 1.39 (0%), pool 2.08. Sorting the shuffled top-60 of val1 **by pool popularity alone, with
no spectra, gives MRR@25 0.726** (valc1 0.716); the full pipeline gets 0.41-0.47 in the same sim and 0.626 plain. Any knob
that raises the popularity weight wins locally by construction; sim deltas of +0.019 to +0.044 correspond to +0.005 on
the LB.

**F8. Leakage that remains (known for the v1 ranker, wider than documented).** Restricted to train-flagged candidates
(22.6% of the val1 top-60), the truth is first in 96.4% of molecules: static artefacts (`train_structs`, pool `train_sid`,
ranker, feature families, engine-2 rank rows and FP models, PubChem FPNet A+B, ICEBERG/GLACIER) all know the held-out
structures. valA/valF swap only the v1 engine's FP bank.

**F9. Shape mismatch with the test.** Spectra per molecule: visible test median 3, mean 3.0, max 9; val1/valc1 median 4,
mean 4.7, max 16 (10% have ≥ 10); valF median 6. [M+H]+ share of spectra 79% against 48%; molecules ICE can score 92.7%
against 86.4% (79.5% valF), so the frag `gap` branch fires on about twice as many molecules locally.

**F10. The acceptance test is underpowered.** `calibrate.py` on five replayable points: val1 Spearman −0.67 (0/5 pairs),
valc1 +0.62 (3/5), val1 sim +0.36 (2/5). With five points only |ρ| = 1.0 is significant at 5%, and the LB values carry
±0.01 of noise themselves (G1). No harness can pass or fail this test meaningfully.

### Checked and fine

- Duplicate-key candidates: none. Base keys equal the metric key for all 21,627 top-25 candidates checked; no base list
  has a duplicate key; the val1 and valc1 submissions contain 0 duplicate metric keys and never the truth twice. Neither
  duplicates nor tautomers inflate or deflate local MRR inside a list (F1 is a library leak, not a list effect).
- Paired statistics in `refuse.py`, `compare_configs.py`, `sim_isomer_eval.py`, `regime_eval.py`: per-molecule paired
  differences, sd(ddof=1)/sqrt(n), seeds averaged per molecule before pairing, same shuffle for every config. Correct.
- Labels: all parse; val1 and valc1 labels are identical; local `valtest_comp` ids equal the Kaggle val1 ids.

## G. Why local estimates and the leaderboard disagree

**G1. The LB differences are small against its own noise.** The public LB is ~33% of ~400 molecules
(`docs/competition/overview.md`, `data.md`), about 132. LB × 132: v3b 51.5, v5a 51.9, v1 52.7, v2 = v4a 53.3, v5c 53.5,
v4b 54.0. The seven submissions span 2.5 reciprocal-rank units; +0.005 is one molecule moving from rank 2 to rank 1.
`python scratch/audit/g_lb_noise.py` takes the per-molecule paired differences between LB points on each validation run
and rescales their SD to n = 132: on val1, the val1 sim and valc1 the SE of a paired difference is 0.005-0.015. Every
LB gap between our points is below 2.6 of those SEs, most below 1 (v1-v2: 0.4-0.5; v4a-v4b: 0.9-1.0). Only v3b's drop
(1.5-2.6 SE) comes close to a signal. (On `vis`, where frag barely moves an exact library hit, v2-v3b is 6 SE; that
regime is not the hidden mix. The SD is borrowed from the validation sets; the hidden-test value is unknown.)
"v3b −0.014 ≈ 5-6 molecules" in `EXPERIMENTS.md` assumes 400 scored molecules; on the public part it is about 2.

**G2. The local sets answer a different question** (F6, F7, F9): answer always in the pool, always a train structure,
famous, more spectra. Local gains of +0.02 to +0.04 shrink to +0.005 or change sign.

**G3. Silent fallbacks.** Every heavy stage is wrapped in `try/except` and degrades quietly (engine-2, PubChem channel,
ICE, GLACIER, frag, both popularity loads, per-molecule re-ranks). In the hidden rerun nothing is observable but the
score. Timing is not the problem: the 400-molecule `vis` run took about 2.8 h at `TOPN=80` (engine-2 3,127 s, PubChem
3,160 s, ICE 1,762 s, GLACIER 756 s), and the popularity channel costs the same as the plain one (94 s vs 96 s for 12).

**G4. Untested paths ship.** Because the 12 smoke molecules are all library hits, code that only runs for non-library
molecules is first executed in the scoring rerun (B).

## Prioritised list

Confirmed bugs (with fix):
1. F1 tautomer twins survive the purge (14/250, 27/244): purge and sample by metric key.
2. F2 valF spectra are 99.9% non-timsTOF relabelled timsTOF: filter to timsTOF or keep the real instrument.
3. F3 `regime_eval` cache key ignores `PC_POP_LAM` / `GL_MH_ONLY` and the extras files: hash everything `run_one` reads.
4. F4 shuffle before the `TOPN` cut loses the truth in 14-16%: truncate first.
5. F5 `TOPN` replay is inexact in stage C (38-57 of 250 lists touched): dump caches at `TOPN=60`.

Suspected or structural:
6. F6 no class-3 (PubChem-only answer) validation: every PubChem-channel knob is untestable locally.
7. F7 popularity alone scores 0.726 on val1: do not tune `POP_MU` (or `v6b`'s 0.4) on these sets.
8. G1/F10 calibration against seven LB points within 2.5 RR units of each other: report the SE at n ≈ 132 and stop
   treating gaps under ~0.015 as evidence.
9. `POP_MU > 0` also enables the `|gen` split (C): add a separate flag before attributing LB changes.
10. Wangpenghua's private popularity arrays versus the public ones (D8), and the `pc_*` alignment (E): unverified.
11. G3/G4 silent fallbacks and paths never run before scoring: add one non-library molecule to the smoke set.

Verified correct: metric; RDKit pin; configs of six LB points; `fusion_core` (tests, exact re-fusion of 12 runs,
invariants); popularity port code; pool popularity alignment; no duplicate keys in lists; paired statistics.
