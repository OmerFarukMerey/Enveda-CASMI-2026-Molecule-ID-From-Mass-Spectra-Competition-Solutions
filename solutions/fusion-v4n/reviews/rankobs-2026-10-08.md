## 0. Bottom line

1. **Locally the obscure answers are not where the rankers fail.** On the 1,574 fixed held-out lists the two-way average of v33b has MRR
   0.875 on the least documented tercile of answers (median 2 PubChem substance records), 0.720 on the middle and 0.703 on the most
   documented one (median 418); without a PubChem record 0.875. The answer is the most documented isomer of its formula in 5% / 30% / 83%
   of the lists of the three terciles, so a ranker that simply preferred documented isomers would show the opposite order.
2. **Our ranker's inputs know little about fame.** No input family predicts which wrong isomer is the most documented better than AUC
   0.74 (`lib`), most of that is the tier of the row (pool, joined, generated), and inside a formula group the substance count explains at
   most 6% of the variance of any input. Both rankers do put a more documented row first when they are wrong (about e^0.9 = 2.5 times the
   records of the average wrong isomer; on the obscure tercile e^0.24 for ours, e^0.46 for the public ranker), and **no refit changed that
   number** (section 3.4).
3. **A reseed of the control moves the obscure tercile as much as any variant.** Pre-registered decision cell (two-way average, least
   documented tercile, old + hold2 lists, 1,120 lists): control B (the production recipe, other seeds) against control A
   **+0.0050 ± 0.0020**; the best variants +0.0048 ± 0.0022 (inverse-fame weights, median 2), +0.0050 ± 0.0037 (obscure third only),
   +0.0042 ± 0.0019 (weights, median 9). None is above control B. **By the pre-registered rule no variant gains.**
4. **One effect is real and small: inverse-fame weights help answers without a PubChem record and cost nothing overall.** Two-way
   average, weights with target median 2, four seeds against the four-seed control: no record +0.0085 ± 0.0039 (519 lists), at most 3
   records +0.0071 ± 0.0029, least documented tercile +0.0041 ± 0.0023, most documented −0.0019 ± 0.0023, all +0.0004 ± 0.0012. The
   no-record gain appeared in both seed pairs separately (+0.0080 ± 0.0033 against A, +0.0106 ± 0.0042 against B; the reseed controls
   are +0.0017 ± 0.0023 and +0.0025 ± 0.0034 there). Training on the obscure third only makes the same trade far steeper: no record
   +0.016 ± 0.006, most documented tercile −0.041 ± 0.005.
5. **Removing fame-carrying families does not help anywhere**: without `lib`, `cross` or `fp` nothing moves beyond the reseed noise;
   without `join` the famous tercile loses (−0.008 ± 0.003) and 21 more `c2J` lists get a joined row first; without `analog` every
   stratum loses, the obscure one too (−0.0075 ± 0.0041); without all five −0.023 to −0.039.
6. **Two constructions of the brief backfired, and say something.** Residualising the inputs against the candidate's count hands the
   trees the count (alone +0.22 on the most documented tercile, wrong-first rows e^2.0 times more documented): it is a popularity prior,
   the thing the leaderboard punished, and it shows how large that signal is locally and that the production ranker does not have it.
   Fame-masked answers taught the model to recognise a masked row (MRR 0.93 on masked test rows): an artefact, not robustness.
7. **Full replay of the weighted variant (four seeds) against the same-seed control: neutral.** Enveda panel composite −0.0021 ± 0.0020,
   mixed regime +0.0013 ± 0.0019, no type beyond about one standard error; exposure 1 against 2 (panel) and 25 against 24 (mixed).
8. **Exported, because the brief's condition (a gain on the obscure strata beyond two standard errors against the control) is met on
   the no-record and the at-most-3-records strata, and not as a recommendation**: `D:\casmi-data\kaggle_ds\casmi26-ours-ranker-obs\`
   (inverse-fame weights, median 2, four seeds), preset `configs/v48a-v33b-ranker-obs.json`. The pre-registered cell was not passed, the
   replay is flat, and the local effect is smaller than what one public-leaderboard answer is worth (0.0038, `determ`). Section 6 states
   the case and the risk.

Readings.
- **The least documented third is the easiest third for both rankers in every list group**, although its answer is almost never the
  most documented isomer. The local obscure answers are `enveda-180` synthetics with one to three records and GNPS natural products
  without a record; nitrogen-free obscure answers are few (69 lists). Fame and compound class are confounded locally.

  average is the best of the three. Whatever our ranker learned beyond the public one pays on documented answers.

Readings.
- **Most of what the inputs know about the count is the tier of the row.** Joined PubChem rows have a high fingerprint score and few
  records (ρ of `fz` with the count is −0.15), generated rows have none (`is_gen` −0.61), library structures have many (`lib_nspec`

- The public ranker leans on `lib` three times as much on obscure lists as on famous ones (−0.135 against −0.045), and on `list`

- **Direct test** (`o4b_pref.py`; lists where the answer is not first and the first row has the answer's formula): how documented is

  same-formula row: 9 to 10%). Part of this is the tier again (a ranker that prefers pool rows to generated rows has a positive value
  with no knowledge of fame); it is a property both rankers share, the public one more on obscure answers.
- **Not done**: partial-dependence curves (the permutation by stratum replaces them).

### 3.4 What the variants say about fame

- **Inverse-fame weights move a little MRR from documented to undocumented answers** and nothing else: +0.008 without a record, +0.004 on
  the least documented third, −0.002 to −0.004 on the most documented third, zero overall, exposure not up (134 `c2J` lists with a
  joined row first against 144).
- **Training on obscure answers only is the same trade made steep.** Alone, the obscure third gains +0.034 ± 0.007 on answers without a
  record and loses 0.061 ± 0.008 on the famous tercile and 0.062 on nitrogen-free molecules; `c1b` −0.086 ± 0.014, `dropJ`
  −0.039 ± 0.005 in the average (it has 398 answer-removed and 174 class-1 lists left to learn from). Inside the average the
  no-record gain is +0.016 ± 0.006, tercile 1 +0.005 ± 0.004.
- **None of this touches the preference for documented wrong rows** (2.2: control A +0.87 ± 0.05 on `ext`): `a_m2` +0.89, `a_m9` +0.87,
  obscure half +0.82, obscure third +0.94, without `lib` +0.89, without `analog` +0.81, without `join` +0.82, masked +0.85 (all ± 0.05
  to 0.06; `pref.json`). A ranker fitted only on answers with at most 6 records still puts a wrong row first that is 2.5 times more
  documented than the average wrong isomer. So that preference is not learned from famous training answers; it comes with the
  candidates (tier, and documented isomers being the typical ones of a scaffold).
- **`analog` is evidence, not fame**: without it the obscure tercile loses too.

### 3.5 Two constructions that backfired

- **`d_res` (residualised inputs) is a ranker with the popularity prior built in.** Subtracting slope × centred log count from 44 inputs
  lets the trees recover the count (two inputs of one family with different slopes give it exactly). Alone +0.221 ± 0.011 on the famous
  tercile, 0.000 ± 0.006 on the obscure one; in the average +0.072 and +0.003; its wrong-first rows are e^2.04 = 7.7 times more
  documented than the average wrong isomer. It is the local picture of v44a and v46c: a large gain where the answer is documented, none
  where it is not, and the leaderboard said the hidden answers are of the second kind. **It also shows that the production ranker does
  not have this signal: nothing among the 165 inputs gives a tenth of it.** Excluded from the choice; it would need the count at
  inference in any case.
- **`e_mask` learned the mask.** Scored on held-out lists whose answer row carries the five families' inputs of a random isomer
  (`o4c_mask.py`), every other ranker collapses (control A alone −0.43 ± 0.01, two-way −0.36; public −0.18), because the swap removes
  the answer's analog and fingerprint evidence together with anything about fame; `e_mask` scores 0.93 on those rows, above its own
  unmasked level: the inconsistency between swapped and unswapped inputs marks the answer. On real lists it is −0.003 ± 0.002 in the
  average. The masked-answer emulation is therefore not a usable fame test at ranker level, and is not reported further.

## 4. What the leaderboard hypothesis looks like after this

The brief's premise was that a ranker fitted on library compounds learns to prefer the famous isomer. Measured: (i) the fitted ranker is
best where the answer is least documented and is no worse than the public ranker there; (ii) its inputs carry little of the count;
(iii) reweighting, subsetting, removing families and masking do not change how documented its wrong winners are; (iv) the count itself,
when leaked into the inputs, changes everything on documented answers and nothing on undocumented ones. So **the fame dependence that the
two popularity submissions exposed is not in our ranker in a form a refit on these rows can remove**. Where it can still sit: the
popularity prior at 0.25 (`poplow`: no local support for lowering it), the public ranker (its wrong winners on obscure answers are
e^0.46 more documented, ours e^0.24; it cannot be refitted), engine 2, and the plain fact that the local obscure answers are library
compounds of other kinds than the hidden ones.

## 7. Honesty and limits

- **Asymmetries**: stated at the top. In addition: the training lists and the held-out lists come from the same fold of one FPNet and
  the same libraries; the fame of an answer is confounded with its compound class and its library (2.1).
- **One training set, two to four seeds per variant.** Only `a_m2` and `a_m9` have a replicate; every other variant's cells carry the
  reseed noise of 3.3 on top of the printed standard errors. The six control seeds give a spread, not a distribution.
- **Counts for joined and generated rows** come from a key lookup in the PubChem tier (connectivity block against our tautomer-canonical
  key; 4.5% of joined rows without a hit count as 0 records). The answers' counts come from the pool table and are exact as far as that
  table is.
- **The public ranker's permutation** (2.2) uses its order-free score, not the notebook's tie order.
- **Not done**: partial dependence; a second training set for the replicates; forward-model inference for the 16 to 180 replay
  candidates without scores; a replay of `a_m9`, of the family ablations or of the combinations (none passed anything); a Kaggle commit
  run; the dataset upload; `EXPERIMENTS.md` / `INDEX.md`.
- **Not verified**: the dataset cards and licences listed as open in the production README; that the hidden answers resemble any local
  stratum.
- **Failed or corrected on the way**: `a_m21` is the control (amendment 1). `d_res` leaks the count (3.5). `e_mask` learns the mask (3.5).
  The first replay of `a_m2` ran while its replicate seeds were being written and used four boosters in fold 0 and two elsewhere; those
  rows were removed from my replay file and the variant replayed with four boosters in every fold against a four-seed control (the
  first run's numbers: panel composite −0.0020 ± 0.0019, mixed +0.0023 ± 0.0021). The production recipe's stored out-of-fold scores
  (`rankbank4\oof\n0`) cover the old lists only, so section 2 is on `old`. The replay patch lost a line twice (a text replacement that
  split a statement; a here-document that turned escapes into line breaks) and failed before computing anything; no wrong number came
  of it. Two reseed pairs disagree by 0.005 on the decision cell, which is why control C and the replicates were added after the
  first evaluation (amendment 2, dated).
