# Coverage gaps (2026-10-02): adducts the forward models skip, and molecules that are never listed

Offline only. Nothing was submitted, pushed or committed, and no tracked file was edited. New files: this report,

## 0. Bottom line

**Part A (adducts).**
1. The restriction to [M+H]+ / [M+Na]+ is a property of the **checkpoints**, not of the packaging. The ms-pred code
   has a 14-adduct vocabulary (including [M+NH4]+, [M+K]+, both water losses, [M-H]-, [M+Cl]-, formate), but the
   input weights of every adduct other than [M+H]+ and [M+Na]+ still hold their initial values in ICEBERG (exactly
   zero in its intensity model). Widening the runner's `COV` tuple runs without error and scores with an untrained
   embedding. GLACIER looks trained on [M+H]+ only (its [M+Na]+ columns did not move either).
2. **[M+NH4]+ and water-loss spectra can be scored today against the [M+H]+ prediction.** Measured with the packaged
   ICEBERG checkpoint on the val1 molecules: in-group top-1 0.22 / MRR 0.37 for [M+NH4]+ (36 molecules) and 0.27 /
   0.41 for [M-H2O+H]+ (22), against chance 0.03 / 0.12, and equal to the native [M+H]+ score on the molecules that
   have both (MRR 0.34 against 0.33, n = 18). Passing the adduct "natively" is worse (0.14 / 0.27). It is a
   notebook-side change of about 15 lines in `nb/07_rescore.py`; no new model.
3. **[M+K]+ and negative mode cannot be served by the positive checkpoint.** [M+K]+ is at chance in every variant.
   Negative-only molecules are at chance with the [M+H]+ prediction shifted by 2 H (MRR 0.17, chance 0.16, n = 18).
   A negative model is needed. Good news from the library test: formate and chloride adducts fragment like [M-H]-
   (same-compound similarity 0.60 to 0.72; isomer pick 0.91 to 0.96 on timsTOF against 0.90 for an [M-H]- replicate, 0.83 to 0.93 on Orbitrap against 0.97), so **one
   [M-H]- model covers all four negative adducts of the test.**
4. Negative-mode candidates without NIST: **FIORA OS v1.0.0** (MIT, trained on MSnLib v7, [M-H]- supported,
   Orbitrap HCD only) can be tried as is; training our own is allowed and the data are there (640k negative spectra
   of 113k structures in `train.parquet`, 266k of them timsTOF), but ICEBERG training wants two 24 GB GPUs upstream.
5. Expected gain of Part A: **+0.003 / +0.010 / +0.025** (low / central / high), of which the surrogate is
   +0.001 / +0.003 / +0.006 and a working negative model +0.002 / +0.008 / +0.02.

**Part B (never listed).**
1. Today only the v1 engine proposes structures outside its pool: 1 or 2 of 46 reactions applied to the 6 strongest
   spectral analogs, at most 60 products per parent and 150 in total, first come first kept. Generated candidates
   take 15 to 31% of the final 25 slots in our caches and are never the truth there (by construction).
2. **Hide-and-regenerate with spectral parents (not an oracle): today's generator regenerates 16.7% of class-3-like
   training structures** (50 / 300); the oracle parents of the directions report gave 33%. The parent search loses
   half of the reach. With 20 parents and no first-come cap the same rules reach 30.0%; the prototype
   (`prototypes/analog_derive.py`, 112 symmetric operations, products ranked by multi-parent support) reaches
   **39.3%, and 29.7% with the truth among the first 25 generated**. With the forum's 3× overstatement that is about
   6% today and about 13% (10% within 25) for the prototype on truly novel molecules. Where the nearest training
   structure is below Tanimoto 0.6 the reach is 0 to 4%.
3. The realistic options, in order: the generator upgrade (CPU, 1.7 s per molecule), a static library of generated
   natural-product-like structures as an extra database tier (67 M structures, CC BY 4.0), and only then a
   spectrum-to-structure model (FRIGID is the best public one; audited accuracy of this model class is near 10%).
4. Expected gain of Part B: **0 / +0.004 / +0.015**, entirely dependent on the unknown class-3 share.

**Build first:** (1) the [M+NH4]+ / water-loss surrogate (half a day, validated offline, low risk); (2) the generator
upgrade (20 parents, support-ranked cap), measured with the engine's `drop_pid` before it is submitted. The FIORA
negative-mode check is third and can run in parallel because it only needs an offline in-group test first.

## 1. Part A facts: what the packaged checkpoints accept

Sources: `ice_runner.py`, `gl_runner.py`, `fuse.py`, `src/ms_pred/common/chem_utils.py` and the three checkpoints of
`ahmedberatozer/casmi26-iceberg` and `casmi26-glacier` (ms-pred commit 708148c2a8eb, MIT; ICEBERG 2.1 `msg_all` and
the GLACIER MassSpecGym checkpoint).

| Item | ICEBERG (gen.pt + inten.pt) | GLACIER (glacier.pt) |
|---|---|---|
| Coverage filter in the runner | `COV = ('[M+H]+', '[M+Na]+')` and positive mode (`is_covered`); the same tuple is hard-coded in `fuse.build_ice_input(covered_only=True)` | same `COV`; the notebook also has `GL_MH_ONLY` |
| Adduct vocabulary of the code and the checkpoint | 14 one-hot types + 8 property bits (`adduct_embedder` 14 × 22): [M+H]+, [M+Na]+, [M+K]+, [M-H2O+H]+, [M+NH4]+, [M]+, [M-2H2O+H]+, [M-NH3+H]+, [M+H-CH2O2]+, [M+CHO2]-, [M-H]-, [M+Cl]-, [M-H2O-H]-, [M-H-CO2]- | same table |
| Adducts with trained weights | **[M+H]+ and [M+Na]+ only** | **[M+H]+ only** (indirect, see below) |
| Instruments | one-hot of 4: Orbitrap and QTOF trained, IT-FT and Unknown untouched. The runner maps every non-Orbitrap instrument (timsTOF included) to `QTOF` | same |
| Collision energy | continuous sinusoidal embedding of the eV value; the runner rounds to 5 eV buckets (minimum 5), NCE spectra use the mean of the numbers in `collision_energy_orig`; no CE → prediction summed over 20 + 40 + 60 eV | same |
| Other limits | elements of `VALID_ELEMENTS`, InChI round trip; `max_nodes 100` fragments | ≤ 100 heavy atoms |

How "trained" was established (`adduct_weight_probe.py`, `adduct_weight_probe2.py`). The adduct one-hot is
concatenated to every atom's input features. A one-hot column that never saw a training example has a zero gradient
and keeps its initial values.
- ICEBERG generator (`gnn.input_project`, 512 × 136, PyTorch default init uniform ±0.0857): the columns of [M+H]+
  and [M+Na]+ have 13% and 34% of their weights outside the init bound (max 0.14 and 0.42); the other 12 adduct
  columns have 0% outside, max 0.0857, std 0.048 to 0.051 (init std 0.0495). Property bits: "positive", "alkali",
  "+H" trained; "negative", "-H", "+Cl", "-H2O", "-CO2" untouched. Intensity model: the 12 other adduct columns and
  the 5 untouched property bits are **exactly 0**.
- GLACIER (Graphormer init N(0, 0.02), so the bound test does not apply): columns that are always active together
  receive identical updates. The [M+H]+ column correlates with the bias (0.33) and with the "positive" and "+H"
  bits (0.14, 0.16), as expected for a feature that is on in every example. The [M+Na]+ column does not correlate
  with the "alkali" bit (0.01) nor with the bias (0.04): it looks as untouched as [M+K]+. This agrees with the
  public LB finding that GLACIER on [M+H]+ spectra only gains 0.006 to 0.010 (`GL_MH_ONLY`).
- MassSpecGym itself contains only [M+H]+ and [M+Na]+, positive mode, Orbitrap or QTOF (MassSpecGym paper,
  arXiv 2410.23326). The ms-pred README offers NIST-trained weights only by e-mail with a licence proof; the packaged
  ones are the open MassSpecGym weights.

**What change would "enable" other adducts, and why it does not help.** Three edits make the code path run:
add the adduct to `COV` in both runners, call `build_ice_input(..., covered_only=False)` (and relax the positive-mode
test in `is_covered` for negative adducts), and map the competition's names to ms-pred's (`[M-2H2O+H]+` →
`[M+H-2H2O]+`, `[M+CH2O2-H]-` → `[M+CHO2]-`). The prediction then uses an untrained embedding (random noise in the
generator, nothing in the intensity model) plus the adduct's mass offset on every fragment. Measured in section 2.2
as "native (untrained)": it is worse than the surrogate for [M+NH4]+ and at chance for negative-only molecules.

Side finding: ICEBERG's [M+Na]+ score is weak on natural products. On the 19 val1 molecules with both adducts the
in-group top-1 is 0.47 from [M+H]+ spectra and 0.16 from [M+Na]+ spectra; averaging both, as the runner does, is
neutral (MRR 0.567 against 0.562, +0.006 ± 0.012).

## 2. Part A experiments

### 2.1 Library test: is the [M+H]+ spectrum a proxy for another adduct? (CPU, `adduct_proxy.py`)

For compounds measured as two adducts in the same library, instrument and collision-energy setting, the query-adduct
spectrum is compared with the reference-adduct spectrum of the same compound and of up to 30 same-formula isomers,
with the runner's own cleaning and entropy similarity (0.01 Da / 20 ppm). A real reference spectrum is the best
case of a prediction, so the isomer pick rate is an upper bound for a surrogate. "Ceiling" = a replicate spectrum of
the same adduct. Transformations: `raw` none; `cut` both spectra limited to the lighter precursor + 0.5; `frag` the
lighter precursor removed too; `hyb` a query peak is moved by the adduct mass difference when that makes it match.
Up to 3,000 queries per row; top-1 over the queries that have isomers.

Reading.
- **[M+NH4]+ and [M-H2O+H]+ are good [M+H]+ proxies** wherever the labels are sound (MSnLib, np-examples, the
  smaller libraries). The three transformations differ by less than 0.02; no transformation is needed beyond
  dropping the adduct precursor.
- **Do not use enveda-180 rows to judge or train [M+NH4]+ handling.** There 40% of the [M+NH4]+-labelled spectra
  have a similarity below 0.05 to the same compound's [M+H]+ spectrum (3 to 8% in every other library), and only
  16% show the NH3-loss ion; where they show it the similarity is 0.57 (`nh4_label_check.py`). The label looks
  unreliable for this synthetic library (an ion of the same m/z from another species; not proven).
- [M+K]+ carries almost no structural signal against either reference on timsTOF.
- Negative against positive is a weak proxy (0.22 against 0.10 for isomers); negative adducts among themselves are
  near-perfect proxies of [M-H]-.

### 2.3 What the surrogate is, concretely

No runner fork is needed. Before `ice_fuse.build_ice_input(te, cands)` in `nb/07_rescore.py`, under a new flag
(for example `ICE_SURROGATE`), rewrite a copy of the test rows:
- `[M+NH4]+` → adduct `[M+H]+`, `precursor_mz` minus 17.026549, peaks at or above the new precursor + 0.5 dropped;
- `[M-H2O+H]+`, `[M-2H2O+H]+` → adduct `[M+H]+`, precursor unchanged (the "as is" row above; cutting the prediction
  at the query precursor is +0.03 MRR but needs a change inside the runner);
- nothing for `[M+K]+` and for negative spectra.
The runner then treats them as covered [M+H]+ spectra at their own CE bucket. Extra predictions arise only for
molecules or CE buckets that had none (6.4% of molecules had no covered spectrum; most extra spectra share the 20 /
40 / 60 eV buckets), an estimated +10 to 15% of the ICE jobs, inside the current budget. With `GL_MH_ONLY` the same
rewritten rows feed GLACIER. A per-adduct weight (surrogate spectra at 0.5 to 1.0 of a native one) is the only knob.

Training our own (allowed: open model trained on `train.parquet`):
- **ms-pred ICEBERG.** The code path exists (negative adducts are in the vocabulary). Steps: subformula assignment,

  in-group MRR clearly above the 0.16 chance level on negative-only molecules and no loss when added to covered ones.

### 2.5 Expected gain of Part A

## 3. Part B facts: what the pipeline does today for structures outside the pool

- **Candidate sources.** v1 engine: pool window ±10 ppm (fallback 30 ppm) of 710,701 structures (training
  structures, COCONUT and a smaller third source; `casmi26-v2-pool`). Engine-2: its own pool, needs library analogs. PubChem channel
  (`nb/embed/pc/`): every PubChem structure within ±10 ppm, pre-ranked by a partial fingerprint score (top `PC_N1`
  = 5,000), then FPNet A+B `f·z`; the top 25 whose metric key is not in the pool enter fixed slots (2, 4, …, 10 if
  its best score beats the pool's best by more than 600, else 4, 8, …, 20) and only when `lib_max < 0.9`. It covers
  class-2 answers that are in PubChem but not in the pool; it cannot produce a structure that is in no database.
- **Generation** (`casmi/engine.py::Engine.generate`, `casmi/derive.py`; `EngineCfg(generate=True)` in
  `nb/05_v1_engine.py`). Parents = the first 6 analogs with similarity ≥ 0.3 from the analog search (mass-shifted
  entropy similarity of the merged query spectrum per polarity against one representative spectrum per structure,
  polarity and library within ±200 Da; top 100 kept). For each parent, Δ = target mass − parent mass; all 1- and
  2-step combinations of 46 reaction SMARTS (hydroxylation, O/N/C-methylation, acylations, five glycosylations,
  sulfate, prenyl, redox, their removals) whose mass change equals Δ within max(0.004 Da, 10 ppm) are applied at
  every site; Δ ≈ 0 gives remove + add isomerisations. Limits: 800 reaction applications and 60 products per
  parent, **150 products in total, in order of discovery** (first parent first). Products whose key is already in
  the pool window are dropped. Each product gets a fingerprint and fragments and enters the same LightGBM ranker
  with `is_gen`, parent similarity, parent Tanimoto and, from the fe_v4 "derivation" family, support counts and
  site priors (built from COCONUT parents with a 71-reaction grammar, `derive6.py`).
- **Where generated candidates end up** (`gen_slots.py`; base lists are 64 to 80 deep, final lists from each run's
  `submission.csv`):

| Run | molecules | with ≥ 1 generated candidate in the base list | generated in base top-60 (mean) | share of base top-25 / top-5 / top-1 | best generated rank (median) | share of the **final** 25 / 5 / 1 | truth is generated |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| val1 | 250 | 61% | 13.0 | 23% / 14% / 0.4% | 5 | 15% / 8% / 1.2% | 0 |
| valc1 | 250 | 83% | 22.7 | 41% / 26% / 0.4% | 4 | 31% / 21% / 1.2% | 0 |
| vis | 400 | 79% | 9.6 | 32% / 44% / 0% | 2 | n/a (no submission file) | 0 |
| valA | 250 | 61% | 13.1 | 23% / 14% / 0.4% | 5 | 15% / 8% / 1.6% | 0 |
| valF | 244 | 53% | 11.6 | 22% / 15% / 1.6% | 4 | 16% / 13% / 2.0% | 0 |

  The truth is never a generated candidate, **by construction**: every validation truth is a pool structure, and the
  generator drops any product whose key is in the pool window. So the caches measure the cost of generation (3.8
  to 7.8 of the 25 final slots, on molecules that cannot benefit) and nothing about its return. The ranker almost
  never puts a generated candidate first (0 to 2%), which also bounds what generation can return today: a
  regenerated truth lands around rank 4 to 5 at best, reciprocal rank 0.2 to 0.25.

## 4. Part B experiment: hide-and-regenerate with spectral parents

`derive_recall.py`. A training structure is hidden: its spectra leave the analog search (`exclude_sid`), its key
leaves the pool window, structures with the same key are not parents. Its own spectra (up to 16) are the query.
Parents come from the engine's own analog search, not from an oracle. Hit = the truth's first InChIKey block is among
the products (the metric adds a tautomer canonicalisation: a few hits are missed). Two hidden sets of 300, the same
definitions as `class3_reach.py`: training structures of natural-product libraries **without a PubChem record**
(class-3-like; 10,496 exist) and PubChem-known NP-catalogue structures (the usual deletion simulation).

| Generator | Parents | class-3-like: truth regenerated | products per molecule (mean / median) | known NPs: truth regenerated |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **engine rules, engine limits (today)** | 6 spectral | **16.7%** (50 / 300) | 24 / 6 | 19.3% |
| engine rules, 300 per parent, no total cap | 20 spectral | 30.0% | 90 / 24 | 28.7% |
| prototype | 6 spectral | 24.3% | 53 / 10 | 26.7% |

| prototype | 50 spectral | 50.0% | 212 / 72 | 40.0% |

|---|---|---|---|---|

Reading.
- **The spectral parent search halves the reach** of today's generator (33% with oracle parents → 17%). The analog
  search itself is good (the best of the first 6 parents has a median Tanimoto of 0.76 to the truth, and 61% of
  targets have a parent ≥ 0.7 there), but a parent that derives the truth is often at rank 7 to 50, and the
  first-come cap fills up with the first parents' positional isomers.
- **More parents with a support-ranked cap is the cheap win**: 20 parents and ranking by the sum of
  similarity⁴ over the distinct parents that derive a product puts the truth among the first 25 generated for 29.7%
  against 18.7 to 20.3%. The larger rule table, together with dropping the per-parent cap, adds 8 to 9 points at
  equal parents (24.3% against 16.7% at 6; 39.3% against 30.0% at 20).
- **Caveats.** (1) Hide-and-regenerate overstates reach about 3× (forum, `rules-and-pitfalls.md` item 6): hidden
  training structures have close relatives in the library (median nearest Tanimoto 0.82). Divided by 3: about 6%
  today, 13% for the prototype, 10% within the first 25. The stratified table says the same: reach collapses below
  Tanimoto 0.6. (2) Being generated is not being ranked: the truth then competes with the pool candidates of its
  window for 25 slots, which this experiment does not measure. (3) The queries are library spectra from all
  instruments, up to 16 per molecule; the test has a median of 3 timsTOF spectra. (4) Runtime: 0.3 s per molecule
  for the engine rules, 1.7 s for all three prototype settings together, single core.

Risks common to all:
- **Rules.** Nothing above uses NIST. FIORA OS is MSnLib-trained (stated in the checkpoint's own params file); write
  that provenance into the README before use. CFM-ID / ISDB rest on METLIN, which is not free data: do not use
  without a host answer. Code copied from public datasets (`derive.py`, the runners) must go to
  `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`; the prototype is own code and does not copy `derive.py`.
- **Runtime.** Surrogate: about +10 to 15% ICE jobs. Generator: under 2 s per molecule on CPU, but every extra
  candidate costs fingerprints, fragments and ICE / GLACIER predictions (cap the block at about 30). FIORA:
  restrict to molecules with negative spectra and to the top-40.
- **Validation.** No local set contains a class-3 or a PubChem-only truth, and no local set orders our submissions
  like the LB (`EXPERIMENTS.md`). Items 1 and 3 can be validated in-group offline; item 2 only through `drop_pid`,
  with the 3× caveat; the LB step for each is a single paired submission with a standard error near 0.01.
