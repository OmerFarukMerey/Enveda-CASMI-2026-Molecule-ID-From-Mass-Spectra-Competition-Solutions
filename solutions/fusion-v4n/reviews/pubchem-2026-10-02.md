# PubChem channel and its merge (2026-10-02): why popularity lost, a rule without that cost, and what the channel can be worth

Offline only. Nothing was submitted, pushed or committed, and no tracked file was edited. New files:
`prototypes/pubchem_merge.py`, `prototypes/test_pubchem_merge.py`, this report, and the scripts in `scratch/pubchem/`
(git-ignored). Large inputs and outputs are in `D:\casmi-data\pubchem_study\` (about 10 GB: the public panel benchmark,
the PubChem tier, the popularity arrays, a new 498-molecule hold-out panel). Section 7 has the commands.

Terms used throughout.
- **Pool**: the main engine's candidate pool (training structures and COCONUT, 710,701 structures).
- **PubChem-only answer**: a hidden molecule whose structure is in PubChem and not in the pool. Only the PubChem
  channel (`nb/04_pubchem.py`) can list it.
- **fz list**: the channel's list as we run it today, the 25 best non-pool structures by the FPNet A+B score `f·z`.
- **Popularity list**: the same channel with `z(f·z) + 0.25·pop` and the 200 most documented window rows added
  (`PC_POP_LAM 0.25`, `PC_POP_UNION 200`), `pop = log1p(substance records) + log1p(PubMed articles)`.
- **Reference slots**: stage B of `fusion_core`. PubChem proposals take positions 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 when
  `rel = fz_top − best_pool_fz > 600`, else 4, 8, 12, 16, 20.
- Paired results are written `MRR (delta ± SE, wins/losses)` against the current rule on the same molecules.

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Why the popularity channel lost (best-supported explanation, not a full account of the size).** The prior
   helps only when the answer is documented, and the channel lists only structures outside the pool, where the
   documented ones are rare: documented structures are already pool members (81% of structures with popularity 8 to
   10, 95% above 10), and structures outside the pool have a median popularity of 1.1 (0.36% reach 5). The prior breaks
   even at an answer popularity of 1.6 to 2.4 (new hold-out panel of 498 ordinary structures, public panel). At 1.1 it
   takes away 17 to 37% of what the channel returns (hold-out strata) and 29% through our whole fusion
   (−0.055 ± 0.008 per PubChem-only molecule, 4 wins / 85 losses); at popularity 0 it takes 49 to 76%. Above about 2 it
   gains a lot (+0.24 MRR on held-out library structures), so the leaderboard loss itself says that the public
   PubChem-only answers are documented like typical PubChem structures, not like library compounds. The other
   hypotheses do not carry a loss: the popularity list does not push correct pool answers down (five regimes, all
   within ±0.002), the gate is unchanged (it reads the raw `fz_top`), there are no key mismatches, and the public
   arrays are row-aligned with the tier. Size: the mechanism gives −0.002 to −0.014 on the leaderboard depending on
   how little documented the answers are; the observed −0.007 to −0.018 overlaps only the strong end of that.
2. **The panel's promotion rule is a fame artefact.** Its 0.718 needs the answer to be famous and missing from the
   pool at the same time. 249 of the 250 panel answers are COCONUT structures, so that case does not occur. With the
   answer's popularity masked the same rule scores 0.394, below the plain fz list (0.424).
3. **Recommended rule** (`prototypes/pubchem_merge.py`, defaults): popularity never sorts the list. Proposals go to
   3, 6, 9, 12, 15; to 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 when `rel > 600`, or when the best proposal beats every same-formula base
   candidate in ICEBERG and GLACIER, or when a proposal with `pop ≥ 8` is among the first five (it then leads the
   PubChem list). Nothing is promoted to rank 1.
4. **Numbers per scenario** (config v7b, against the current rule): PubChem-only simulation 0.188 → 0.242
   (+0.054 ± 0.006, 114/0) for an obscure answer and 0.323 (+0.136 ± 0.010, 137/0) for a famous one; answer in the
   pool: +0.004 ± 0.003 (valA with honest isomer order), +0.012 ± 0.005 (valA), +0.002 ± 0.003 (valF),
   +0.001 ± 0.002 (valc1), so no measured cost; the panel's two in-pool scenarios are unchanged (0.9882, 0.9796).
5. **Leaderboard range: +0.000 / +0.004 / +0.009** (low / central / high). The driver is one number we cannot measure
   locally, the public-LB score of the PubChem list alone (the reference author's probe: 0.037). Under the same
   scaling a perfect merge is worth +0.03. **The PubChem channel cannot move 0.41 to 0.50 through its merge rule**;
   the molecules that score zero today are mostly not PubChem-only answers that we fail to promote.

## 2. Why popularity inside the PubChem channel lost on the leaderboard

Facts to explain (`research-2026-10-02.md`): with the public arrays the prior costs 0.007 to 0.018 on the fusion base
(ours 0.393 and 0.405, two other teams), and the notebook that scores 0.415 with it uses private arrays.

Reading:
- Structures with public spectra are documented (median 2.7 even outside COCONUT), and for them the prior is a large
  gain. This is the population the prior was tuned on, and no hidden PubChem-only answer belongs to it: an answer with
  public spectra is a pool structure.
- Break-even is near 1.6 here and 2.4 on the public panel. The weaker encoder leaves more room for a prior, and a
  natural-product answer has more documented near-isomers than a synthetic one (−37% against −17% at popularity 1.1).
- Three quarters of the PubChem structures outside the pool are at or below 1.6. An answer drawn from them loses with
  the prior; an answer documented like a library compound gains. The leaderboard lost.
- The hold-out also shows what the first pass costs a weaker encoder: the answer is in the 5,000-row shortlist for
  76% (enveda-180, windows of 14,552 rows), 86% and 93% of the molecules, against 98% on the public panel.

### 2.3 The gate quantity (not the cause; weak but usable)

The gate reads the raw `fz_top` in both variants (`probe_one_pop` keeps it), so the popularity list sits in the same
slots. What `rel = fz_top − best_pool_fz` can and cannot do (panel, `s3_features.py`; A = answer in the pool,
B = answer removed):

| | A | B | B, top proposal correct | B, top proposal wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `rel`, 5% / 25% / median / 75% / 95% | −412 / −160 / −26 / 94 / 356 | −288 / −34 / 123 / 370 / 767 | −269 / −63 / 79 / 260 / 789 | −288 / −9 / 146 / 395 / 752 |

| Quantity of the fz list | AUC: B with a correct top proposal against A | AUC: correct against wrong top proposal inside B |
|---|---|---|
| `rel` | 0.65 | 0.43 |
| z-score of the top proposal over the shortlist | 0.57 | 0.58 |
| score gap, proposal 1 to 2 | 0.68 | 0.72 |
| score gap, proposal 1 to 5 | 0.72 | 0.76 |
| popularity of the top proposal | 0.997 | 0.997 |

`rel > 600` fires for 1.6% of A and 9.8% of B: a sixfold likelihood ratio for "the answer is not in the pool", which
justifies the early slots. It says nothing about whether the top proposal is right (precision 33% above 600, 41%
overall). No fingerprint quantity is sharp enough for a promotion to rank 1: the best margin rule (gap 1 to 2 above
0.3 z) fires for 12.6% of B with 71% precision and for 8.5% of A. Popularity separates A from B perfectly on the panel
because the removed answers are famous; this is the artefact of section 2.1, not a signal.

### 2.4 Duplicates and tautomers (ruled out)

Among the first five PubChem proposals of the gated molecules, 70 of 1,125 (valA), 9 of 885 (valF) and 76 of 915
(valc1) are already base candidates (structures the analogue generator built). The count is identical with the
pipeline's score key and with the official metric key (70, 9, 76) and nearly so with the standard InChIKey block
(68, 9, 69). `merge()` drops them, so no slot is wasted and no answer is listed twice.

### 2.5 Verdict and size check

Best-supported explanation: **selection on documentation**. The channel only lists structures outside the pool, the
pool already holds the documented ones, so an answer that reaches the channel is poorly documented and the prior ranks
documented decoys above it. Both panels put the break-even at an answer popularity of 1.6 to 2.4, and three quarters of
the structures outside the pool are below 1.6. The leaderboard sign agrees: had the public PubChem-only answers been
documented like library compounds, the prior would have gained (about +0.009 with the hold-out numbers of the
"not in COCONUT" stratum; +0.11 MRR per molecule in the simulation with famous answers).

Size, with the scaling of section 5 (through the slots the channel returns about 0.013 on the leaderboard for
`X = 0.037` and 0.018 for `X = 0.05`): the prior removes 17 to 37% of that for answers at popularity 1.1 and 49 to 76%
at popularity 0, i.e. **−0.002 to −0.007 for answers at the non-pool median and −0.006 to −0.014 for answers with
almost no documentation**. The observed −0.007 to −0.018 has the same sign and overlaps only the strong end. Two
remarks on the gap. The observed range assumes that GLACIER on `[M+H]+` only is worth +0.007 to +0.010 on our base (a
number from another team's notebook); our own paired result for both changes together is −0.004 (v5c against v4b).
And the three teams share the same ~130 public molecules, so their losses are one observation, not three. I found no
second mechanism: displacement, the gate, duplicates and array alignment are excluded (the tier SMILES of 2,999 random
rows give the `pc_ik14` connectivity of the same row in 2,999 cases, and the most documented rows of the caffeine,
quercetin, glucose and cholesterol windows are those compounds).

About the private arrays: with them, wangpenghua's v17 (prior in the channel only) ranks next to v16 (no prior), so
his arrays show "no loss", not a gain; the 0.415 of v29 comes with the pool prior and the GLACIER filter, which score
0.408 to 0.411 without the channel prior in a public notebook. Why his arrays cost less is not testable here. A build
without summing over stereoisomers and without the fallback match would leave more rows at zero and make the prior
weaker; that is a guess.

### 3.4 Sensitivity and what was left out

- Forward-margin thresholds (v7b, `s8_rules.py --set grid`). Early slots at margin > −0.05 / 0 / 0.025: PubChem-only
  +0.066 / +0.034 / +0.021; valA-honest −0.002 ± 0.005 (3/14) / 0.000 (0/1) / 0.000 (0/1). Zero is the natural
  threshold (the proposal beats every compared candidate) and the last one without a measured cost.
- Rank 1 at margin > 0 / 0.025 / 0.05 / 0.1 / 0.15 (on top of early slots at 0): PubChem-only +0.104 / +0.081 /
  +0.060 / +0.055 / +0.051; in-pool cost −0.018 / −0.006 / −0.004 / −0.004 / −0.002 (valA-honest), −0.022 to −0.003
  (valF). At 0.05 the trade is +0.026 per PubChem-only molecule against −0.004 per in-pool molecule: it pays only if
  more than about one gated molecule in seven is PubChem-only, and both sides are measured with forward models that
  have seen the molecules. Left off (`FWD_T2 = None`).
- Requiring both forward models, or comparing against all formulas, or against the base top 5 only: same gain within
  ±0.003, no clear difference in cost. Defaults: one model is enough, same formula, top 25.
- Weight of the forward models when the PubChem list itself is re-ordered (`s12_pc_order.py`): 0 / 0.5 / 1 (current)
  / 2 / 4 give 0.505 / 0.576 / 0.590 / 0.589 / 0.595 for the list alone. Flat above 1; leave `ICE_LAM`, `GL_LAM`.
- `rel > 600` → rank 1: three losses in valA-honest and no evidence of precision (2.3). Not used.

## 4. The prototype

`prototypes/pubchem_merge.py`, function `merge_pubchem(base, base_keys, lib_max, p, cfg=None, pop=None, ice=None,
gl=None, formula=None) -> (final list, info)`. Pure, no imports beyond `math`; `p` is the PubChem dict after stage A
(`pc`, `pc_keys`, `pc_fz`, `best_pool_fz`, optional `fz_top`). Tiers and defaults:

| Tier | Condition | Slots |
|---|---|---|
| untouched | `lib_max ≥ LIB_TAU` (0.9) or no PubChem list | base list |
| 0 | nothing below | `SLOTS_GENTLE` = 3, 6, 9, 12, 15 |
| 1 | `rel > REL_TH` (600); or forward margin > `FWD_T1` (0.0); or a proposal with `pop ≥ POP_HI` (8) among the first `POP_K` (5), which then leads the PubChem list | `SLOTS_AGG` = 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 |
| 2 | forward margin > `FWD_T2` (None: off) | `SLOTS_TOP` = 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 |

Forward margin = minimum over the forward models that scored both sides of `score(best new proposal) − max score(base
top FWD_N candidates with the proposal's formula)`. The popularity step is inert unless `pop` is passed. With
`FWD_T1 = FWD_T2 = POP_HI = None` and the reference slots the function equals stage B of `fusion_core` (2,000 random
cases in `test_pubchem_merge.py`; seven tests, no data needed). `s8_rules.py --set final` runs the shipped defaults
through the fusion ("prototype defaults" row = rule D).

To wire it in (not done here):
- Stage B: `final, info = merge_pubchem(smis, keys, lib_max, p, cfg, pop=pop_by_smiles, ice=ice_scores.get(str(mid)),
  gl=gl_of(mid), formula=formula)`. Pass the same GLACIER dict the re-rank uses (`gl_mh` under `GL_MH_ONLY`).
- Stage A re-orders `p['pc']`, `p['pc_keys']` and `p['pc_fz']` and leaves every other per-proposal list in the old
  order. `p['pc_form']` is therefore misaligned after stage A and must not be used; the prototype takes a `formula`
  callable, and the popularity should be passed as `{smiles: pop}`, not as a list.
- The popularity step needs the popularity of the 25 listed proposals from the probe (`pc_lsid`, `pc_lpmid` rows of
  the window, already read by `probe_one_pop`). With `PC_POP_LAM = 0` the probe does not load the arrays today, so the
  fz probe would need a few lines that return `pop` per listed proposal. Without them the rule is C.
- Keep `PC_POP_LAM = 0`.

## 5. Leaderboard estimate

Model (`s11_lb_model.py`): `LB change = X / m · dB + sI · dI`. Molecules whose answer is in neither source contribute
nothing.
- `X` = public-LB score of the PubChem list alone = (share of PubChem-only answers) × (MRR of the fz list on them).
  The reference author's probe scored **0.037** on about 130 molecules (`pc_runner.py` docstring; 1,000-row shortlist,
  which is worth the same as 5,000 on the panel). Scaling by `X` needs neither factor separately.
- `m` = 0.523, the same list's MRR on the simulated PubChem-only molecules, the unit in which `dB` is measured.
- `dB` = change per PubChem-only molecule (3.3): slots +0.030, forward tier +0.024 more, documented answer +0.081 more.
  The slot part is confirmed on the hold-out panel: 0.070 → 0.094 through the slots (+0.023 ± 0.001, 214 wins, no
  loss), +33% of the current return there, +16% in the simulation and +22% on the public panel without the generator.
- `sI` = share of hidden molecules with the answer in the pool and `lib_max < 0.9`; `dI` = change per such molecule.

| Case | `X` | credit given to the forward tier | share of PubChem-only answers with `pop ≥ 8` | `sI` | `dI` | LB change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| low | 0.025 | 0 | 0 | 0.60 | −0.002 | **+0.000** |
| central | 0.037 | 0.5 | 0.1 | 0.40 | 0 | **+0.004** |
| high | 0.050 | 1 | 0.3 | 0.40 | +0.004 | **+0.009** |

One factor at a time from the central case: `X` 0.025 / 0.050 gives +0.002 / +0.005; forward credit 0 / 1 gives
+0.003 / +0.004; famous share 0 / 0.3 gives +0.003 / +0.005; in-pool change −0.002 / +0.004 gives +0.003 / +0.005.
**`X` drives the estimate**, and it is a single probe on about 130 molecules (about ±0.012).

Readings of the same model:
- The channel is worth about `X / m` × 0.19 = 0.013 on the leaderboard today (the slots keep 32% of what the list
  could give).
- **Ceiling of any merge rule**: if every PubChem-only molecule received its PubChem list as the final list and no
  other molecule were touched, the gain would be +0.019 / +0.029 / +0.039 for `X` = 0.025 / 0.037 / 0.050. The rule
  above takes about an eighth of that. The remaining seven eighths need a detector of "the answer is not in the pool"
  that we do not have (3.1).
- The 38 to 46% of hidden molecules that score about zero (`directions-2026-10-02.md`) are therefore not mainly
  PubChem-only answers waiting for promotion. If the fz list is as accurate on them as on the public panel,
  PubChem-only answers are about 7% of the public molecules (0.037 / 0.51); with the hold-out accuracies (0.27 to
  0.42, weaker encoder) they would be 9 to 14%. The rest of the zero bucket is answers outside PubChem or answers
  the fingerprint model does not rank. For PubChem-only answers the larger lever is the ranking inside the window
  (the answer is first for 19 to 41% and listed for 63 to 81%), not the merge.
- One public-LB molecule is 0.008, so the central estimate is half a molecule there. It should be judged on the
  private part or not at all.

## 6. What cannot be measured locally, and what a Kaggle run should dump

Not measurable here:
- The share of PubChem-only answers, how documented they are, and how well FPNet ranks them (`X` and its two factors).
- Honest forward-model margins. ICEBERG and GLACIER have very likely seen the panel and valF molecules, which makes
  the answer look better in both directions (more correct promotions in the simulation, fewer false ones for in-pool
  answers).
- What the analogue generator returns for a real PubChem-only answer (the simulation removes it; the panel replay
  keeps a generator that rebuilds famous structures from close relatives).
- The interaction with engine-2 on PubChem-only molecules (the simulation removes the answer from its list).

One validation run on Kaggle would settle the second and third points and give the gain side without simulation:
- Molecules: about 250 enveda-180 hold-out structures (timsTOF, never in MassSpecGym, popularity median 1.4) and about
  250 natural-product-library hold-out structures that are not in COCONUT (fold 0 of the fold-safe split), with the
  fold-safe checkpoint in the engine **and in the PubChem channel** (today the channel always loads FPNet A+B).
- Two passes over the same molecules: answer removed from the pool (`drop_pid`) and answer left in.
- Dump, per molecule: `base.json` at `TOPN 60`; from the PubChem stage the first 50 proposals with SMILES, key, raw
  `f·z`, `pc_lsid`, `pc_lpmid`, `pc_np`, plus the shortlist mean and standard deviation, the window size and
  `best_pool_fz`; a copy of the list and of `pc_fz` before stage A re-orders them; `ice.json` and `gl.json` covering
  the base top 60 and the first 25 PubChem proposals; `eng.json`; labels.

For the hidden test nothing can be dumped. If the team wants `X` itself, two submissions answer it: the PubChem list
alone as the whole submission (today's shortlist and stage-A re-rank), and the same list sorted by popularity. The
first gives the ceiling of section 5 directly; the sign of the difference says whether PubChem-only answers are
documented.
