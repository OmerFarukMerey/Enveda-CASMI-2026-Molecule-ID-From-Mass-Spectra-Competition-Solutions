**Reading.**
1. **Precursors of the public libraries follow the correct convention exactly** (medians within 0.012 mDa of zero in
   both polarities; the electron-ignoring convention is off by ∓0.55 mDa). Nothing in train "ignores the electron".
2. **The Enveda libraries are high in positive mode by about one electron mass relative to their own negative mode:**
   precursors +0.465 mDa (enveda-180) and +0.514 mDa (enveda-np-examples) positive minus negative; fragments +0.477 and
   +0.586 mDa. That is the 0.49 mDa of topic 743395, reproduced, and it is on the precursors as well as on the
   fragments. On top of it both polarities carry a common offset of about +0.2 mDa (precursors) that is not an
   electron (`masscal`).
3. So **for the Enveda rows, train follows the "calibrant one electron too heavy" convention in positive mode**
   (precursor +0.71 mDa and fragments +0.52 mDa against the truth; after subtracting ME: +0.17 and −0.02 mDa) **and the
   correct one in negative mode** (+0.25 and +0.05 mDa). `train.parquet` was not announced as changed.
4. **A caution on the fragment table, which weakens point 2 on the fragment side.** The negative-mode fragments of the
   *other* libraries sit at −0.44 to −0.49 mDa (in pluskal_ms2 growing with m/z, −0.39 mDa below m/z 100 to −1.40
   above 400), so **in my measurement every library group shows positive above negative by 0.36 to 0.59 mDa**; the
   fragment-level gap alone is therefore not specific to Enveda. Either those libraries have a negative-mode
   calibration term or my unique-sub-formula rule is biased by about −0.4 mDa in negative mode; I did not resolve
   which. What *is* specific to Enveda: (a) the precursors, which are measured values there (robust sd 0.9 ppm) and
   look computed in the public libraries (pluskal_ms2 sd 0.1 ppm, `masscal`), with the 0.47 to 0.51 mDa gap between
   the polarities; (b) the positive-mode fragment centre, +0.52 / +0.37 mDa against −0.04 and −0.13 mDa for the public
   libraries measured with the same rule in the same polarity, a difference of 0.5 to 0.65 mDa. If the rule is biased
   in negative mode, the Enveda negative-mode fragments are also somewhat high (by up to 0.4 mDa), which the
   announcement does not cover.

**Consequence [inference]:** if the hidden positive-mode spectra were lowered by ME, the corrected test now sits about
0.5 mDa *below* the positive-mode Enveda training spectra (the only timsTOF rows, 46% of train) and *on* the public
libraries and the theoretical masses. Before the fix the test matched the Enveda rows and was 0.5 to 0.7 mDa above
theory. Section 4 measures both directions.

### 4.6 What follows

- **What the old error cost us, i.e. what the rerun should do to our scores by itself [inference from the local replay;
  not a leaderboard estimate]: nothing measurable.** If the hidden positive-mode spectra were lowered by ME (peaks and
  precursor), v33b's final lists change in their first row for 2 to 9% of the molecules with no net direction: panel
  composite +0.0002 ± 0.0033, no type beyond 0.4 SE. One standard error is about the size of one leaderboard answer
  (0.0038), so a move of our rerun score by one or two answers either way is what this predicts; a larger move would
  have to come from the metric change or from run-to-run variation, not from the masses. The single public pair
  (0.428 → 0.423 for byte-identical code) is one such move.
- If only the fragment peaks were corrected and not `precursor_mz`: then at the base-list stage nothing happens at all (`Fm`: −0.0021 ± 0.0036 on pool answers,
  +0.0011 ± 0.0062 on joined answers), the forward models do not notice (4.4), and the precursor-side effects of 4.2 /
  4.3 do not arise. The full replay was run for the joint shift only; the fragment-only case is the smaller
  perturbation.
- **Do we need a correction now? No.** The only stage that visibly reacts is the public ranker's reading of the mass
  error, and it reacts in both directions at once (pool answers down, joined answers up); undoing the fix inside the
  notebook (adding ME back to positive-mode m/z) would return the unshifted column of 4.5, which is the same score.
  Not wired.
- **`MASS_OFFSET_BY_ADDUCT='enveda'` must not be switched on with its present table.** The table (+2.22 ppm [M+H]+,
  +0.80 ppm [M−H]−, …) was fitted on the uncorrected enveda-180 rows; on a corrected test the positive-mode offset is
  about +0.5 ppm (4.1), so the flag would shift the PubChem window by 1.7 ppm in the wrong direction. It is off in
  v33b and in every preset since v21a. Should a mass window narrower than ±10 ppm ever be revisited
  (`reviews/masswin-2026-10-05.md` closed it), the offsets to use after the fix are about +0.5 ppm in positive and
  +0.7 ppm in negative mode, i.e. practically one number.
- Engine 2's second ranker carries `abs(ppm + 1.5)`, centred on the old offset; it has blend weight 0.12 and was not
  re-run on shifted input: unmeasured, expected small.
