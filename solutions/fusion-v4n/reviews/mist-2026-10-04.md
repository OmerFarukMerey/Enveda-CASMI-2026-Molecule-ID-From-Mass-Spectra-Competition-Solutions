# MIST as a second fingerprint model for the PubChem channel (2026-10-04)

Offline only. Nothing was submitted, pushed or committed, no Kaggle notebook was run, no existing file was edited, no
model was trained. GPU: inference only, through `train/gpushare.py`, 1.6 GB per process. Scripts and log:
`scratch/mist/` (git-ignored; `LOG.md`). Data and outputs: `D:\casmi-data\mist\` (0.2 GB). "±" is one standard error;
differences are paired per molecule against FPNet alone, with (gains / losses) in brackets. A local retrieval gain is
not a leaderboard gain.

## 0. The answer

**MIST is usable under the rules and runs offline in a few minutes, but it does not help.** On structures it cannot
have seen it puts the answer in the first 100 of the PubChem window for 33% of the molecules against 74% for our
fold-safe FPNet, and no combination of the two gives a clear gain at 25, 50 or 100. Where MIST looks strong it is
because it was trained on the structure, and such structures are pool rows in our pipeline, never PubChem-only
answers. No prototype was written (step 3 of the task was not reached). No other packaged model is worth the same test.

Error overlap at 100 on the same 172: both have the answer 51, FPNet only 76, **MIST only 6**, neither 39. Of the 45
molecules FPNet misses, MIST finds 6; of the 115 MIST misses, FPNet finds 76. The best combination at 50 (70.3%) stays
below FPNet at 100 (73.8%): −3.5 ± 2.0, so it does not buy "the hit rate of 100 with 50 candidates" either.

**The formula.** We know a 10 ppm mass window, not the formula. A window holds 52 distinct candidate formulae on
average (median 38, 90% point 120, maximum 249). One prediction per (candidate formula, spectrum) and each row scored
with the prediction for its own formula costs 144 forward items per molecule. This is what was measured. Giving MIST
the true formula (not possible on the test) lifts it from 33% to 53% in the first 100 on the honest set and still
leaves it 21 points below FPNet, so the missing formula is not the main reason for the result. A formula predictor
(msbuddy, packaged in `franciscoangulo/casmi26-mist-msbuddy-assets`) would remove the per-formula loop, but its
models' training data are not documented there: unknown, unusable.

**Speed** (250 regime molecules, one below-normal CPU thread per process, RTX 4070 Ti SUPER shared with the desktop):
subformula assignment and features 2.4 s per molecule (median 1.0 s, 95% point 9 s, maximum 48 s), forward pass
0.22 s per molecule (about 640 items per second), 1.6 GB of GPU memory at batch 64 (0.1 GB for the weights), model
load 1.4 s. For comparison the channel's own full f.z on the 5,000 shortlist took 9.8 s per molecule here. On a T4
(three to four times slower) with four CPU workers: about 1 to 2 s per molecule, 10 to 30 minutes for 1,000 molecules.
Runtime is not an obstacle.

## 2. Does it retrieve?

### 2.1 Set-up (`scratch/mist/run_window.py`, `analyse.py`)
- **Molecules.** Mixed regime, truth-removed, PubChem record: 325; with at least one `[M+H]+` / `[M+Na]+` spectrum:
  261; 11 of them have an empty tier window; **250 analysed**. MIST gets at most six of the supported spectra;
  FPNet's logits are the stored fold-safe ones from all spectra of the molecule (`D:\casmi-data\pcjoin\pc\z.npz`).
- **Candidates: the whole ±10 ppm window** (9,044 rows on average), not a prefix. FPNet = the notebook's two passes
  (ECFP4 block on every row, full f.z on the best 5,000). MIST = every row. Combinations are formed inside the 5,000
  shortlist.
- **Universe and rank.** Rows that parse, are not pool rows and are unique by the tier's InChIKey first block; rank of
  the truth = 1 + structures with a higher score. This replaces the metric key (which costs 15 to 50 ms per row); for
  FPNet it agrees with the exact stored lists of the pcjoin study: first 50 69.2% against 69.6%, first 100 74.0%
  against 74.4%, same side of 100 for 99.6% of the molecules.
- **Arms** fixed before the full numbers were read: MIST alone (window, shortlist, true formula), z-sum with weight
  0.5 / 1 / 2, reciprocal-rank fusion (60), alternating union, and MIST or the z-sum re-ranking FPNet's first 200 or
  1,000. Thirteen arms and several subsets: read a single cell with one extra standard error.
- **Honest subsets.** MassSpecGym also contains MSnLib (`pluskal_ms2` in train), so "outside GNPS / MassBank / MoNA"
  is not enough. Membership was tested directly against the 28,923 MassSpecGym structures
  (`n10705013/casmi26-msgym-pool`, InChIKey first block, tautomer twins of train included). *Honest* = the structure
  is not in MassSpecGym (any fold; 104 of 250). *Honest-strict* = honest and no spectrum of the structure from GNPS,
  MassBank, MoNA or MSnLib in train (40, of which 35 are enveda-180 timsTOF). "In MassSpecGym" (146) overstates
  "seen", because it includes MassSpecGym's validation and test folds.
- **Confirmation set.** The regime's natural-product honest cell (49 molecules) showed a gain near two standard
  errors for the 0.5 z-sum. It was re-tested on fresh molecules: the 74 hold-out molecules of the training-plan study
  (`D:\casmi-data\trainplan`) that are natural-product catalogue structures outside MassSpecGym with a record and a
  supported spectrum (68 with a non-empty window). They share no metric key with the regime.

### 2.4 Is it the instrument or the structure? (`prep_np.py`; 212 enveda-np-examples molecules)
The timsTOF row above is drug-like enveda-180 chemistry, so a second timsTOF set was scored: the enveda-np-examples
structures (natural products on the test's instrument; the `np` fold, unseen by the fold-safe FPNet), with their
timsTOF spectra only. 205 of the 212 structures are in MassSpecGym, measured there on other instruments.

| enveda-np-examples, truth removed from the pool, 212 | first 25 | first 50 | first 100 | first place | MRR@25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FPNet (fold-safe) | 67.9 | 74.5 | 80.2 | 16.5 | 0.281 |

So MIST reads timsTOF spectra well **when it knows the structure**, and the failure in 2.2 is about unseen
structures, not about the instrument. This table is not evidence for the pipeline: only 7 of these molecules are
outside MassSpecGym (no reading possible), and the other 205 are training structures with spectra, which the pipeline
answers from the pool and the library (class 1), not from the PubChem channel.

**Why the memorised structures do not help the channel.** A PubChem-only answer is by definition outside our pool
(train ∪ COCONUT). Of the 28,923 MassSpecGym structures 27,278 are pool keys; at most 1,645 could ever be a
PubChem-only answer that MIST has seen. For every other hidden PubChem-only answer the honest tables apply.

### 2.5 What can still mislead
- The honest set is small (172 pooled; 40 strict) and its natural-product part comes from GNPS, MSnLib, RIKEN, MS-DIAL
  and MoNA spectra on Orbitrap and QTOF instruments, MIST's home ground; only 35 + 7 honest molecules are timsTOF.
- "Structure not in MassSpecGym" does not exclude close analogues in MassSpecGym; that favours MIST, and it still
  loses.
- The fold-safe FPNet is weaker than the notebook's A + B pair, which favours the combination here.
- The InChIKey-block universe is an approximation (2.1), the same for every arm.
- The confirmation set carries a wrong instrument label in `analysis_confirm.parquet` (the table has no instrument
  column; no number above uses it).

## 3. Consequences

**Not for the pipeline.** No honest subset shows a clear gain in "answer in the first 100", and 50 candidates of the
best combination do not reach FPNet's 100. A weight below 0.5 would move even fewer molecules; a weight of 1 already
costs 8 points in the first 25. This matches the earlier results for the same step: snapshot ensemble +0.010 ±
0.009, trained fingerprint re-scorer −0.009 ± 0.010, natural-product prior not supported end to end.

Had it helped, the entry point would have been `probe_one` in `nb/embed/pc/probe_core2.py` after pass 2 (the walk
order `np.argsort(-fz)` becomes z(f.z) + λ z(MIST) over the shortlist; `pc_runner.py` would load MIST next to the
FPNet pair and pass the positive spectra with the task), handing the selected rows over in f.z order. The selection
rule itself already exists as `prototypes/pc_select.select(fz, prior, n, lam)` with a test; MIST's cosine would be
the `prior`. Kaggle needs: the 97 MB dataset, no wheels, 10 to 30 minutes for 1,000 molecules.

**Other available models: none is worth the same test.** The remaining MIST files are of unknown provenance or
non-commercial; DreaMS cannot score a structure without a spectrum; FIORA is a forward model for the joined list,
not a retrieval model; the joint-embedding models are not packaged and share MIST's training data, so they share its
blind spot (structures outside MassSpecGym).

**Fine-tuning (described, not done).** MIST would need the training structures FPNet has (182,941 enveda-180
structures on the timsTOF and the other train libraries): subformula assignment for the 1.5 M `[M+H]+` / `[M+Na]+` spectra of train
(4 to 5 CPU-hours), then training from the MassSpecGym checkpoint, upstream settings 600 epochs at batch 128 on
231k spectra, i.e. tens of GPU-hours on the shared card. The result would be a second model trained on the same data
as FPNet in another architecture. The evidence here (FPNet 84% against MIST 15% in the first 100 on unseen drug-like
structures; MIST first for 65% of structures it was trained on) says the training data decide, not the architecture;
and candidate (b) of the training plan found no information left in a fingerprint prediction beyond f.z. Not
recommended.

## 4. Reproduce

```bash
P=.venv/Scripts/python; S=scratch/mist; D=D:/casmi-data/mist
kaggle datasets download -d ahmedberatozer/casmi26-mist-msg-v1 -p $D/ds/mist --unzip
kaggle datasets download -d n10705013/casmi26-msgym-pool -p $D/ds/msgym --unzip
$P $S/smoke.py --n 8                                              # strict load, timings
$P $S/run_window.py --shard 0/2 & $P $S/run_window.py --shard 1/2  # regime, 33 min, resumable
$P $S/analyse.py                                                   # tables of 2.2 and 2.3 -> $D/analysis.txt
$P $S/prep_np.py                                                   # enveda-np-examples set
MIST_M=$D/np MIST_OUT=$D/win_np MIST_Z=$D/np/z.npz MIST_EXTRA=$D/np/extra.json $P $S/run_window.py --shard 0/1
$P $S/analyse.py --win $D/win_np --mols $D/np/mols.parquet --out $D/analysis_np.parquet
MIST_M=D:/casmi-data/trainplan MIST_OUT=$D/win_confirm MIST_Z=D:/casmi-data/trainplan/pc/z.npz \
  MIST_EXTRA=D:/casmi-data/trainplan/pc/extra.json MIST_ONLY=$D/confirm_ids.txt $P $S/run_window.py --shard 0/1
$P $S/analyse.py --win $D/win_confirm --mols D:/casmi-data/trainplan/mols.parquet --out $D/analysis_confirm.parquet
```

`scratch/mist/mist_local.py` runs the dataset's own adapter (`scorer/mist_adapter.py`) with its Kaggle-only guard
switched off; no code of the package was copied into the repository. Provenance for a README entry, should the model
ever be used: Zenodo 11580401 (CC BY 4.0), `Janne98/mist@16b4438` (MIT), trained on MassSpecGym (MoNA, MassBank, GNPS,
MSnLib), no NIST. `INDEX.md` was not regenerated (no existing file may be edited): run `python tools/gen_index.py`
when this review is committed.
