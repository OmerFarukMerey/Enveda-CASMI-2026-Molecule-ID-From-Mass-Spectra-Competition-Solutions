## 0. Bottom line

1. **The suspected mechanism does not exist.** No [M+NH4]+, water-loss, [M+K]+ or negative spectrum reaches ICEBERG or
   GLACIER, with any label: `fuse.build_ice_input` drops them before the runner input is written (`fuse.py` line 54,
   called without arguments in `nb/07_rescore.py` line 59), and both runners drop them again (`ice_runner.py` lines 390
   to 393 and 576; `gl_runner.py` lines 427 and 708). Checked in the data: of the 649 spectra in the runner items of the
   panel, 0 carry another adduct. A candidate's score is the unweighted mean of the entropy similarity over the
   molecule's covered spectra (`ice_runner.py` line 616, `gl_runner.py` line 748). Recomputing every covered panel
   molecule from its [M+H]+ / [M+Na]+ spectra only is therefore the computation the notebook already does; done
   (31,702 candidates, both models), it gives v24a back: no reciprocal rank changes inside cs in either arm.
2. **The cs effect is real in the replay and is explained by weak forward evidence entering at full strength, not by
   a choice of spectra.** cs molecules have fewer covered spectra (2.0 against 3.2), weaker ones (median best base peak
   296k against 862k), and twice as many same-formula candidates in the list (median 46 against 23; 30 of the 38 have
   no nitrogen). A single [M+H]+ spectrum of a cs molecule ranks the answer in its group with MRR 0.53 (GLACIER) and
   0.48 (ICEBERG) against 0.71 and 0.65 for the other covered molecules; an [M+Na]+ spectrum of a cs molecule is at
   chance (0.18 and 0.26, chance 0.11). The stage then z-scores the similarities inside the group, which gives this
   weaker evidence the same pull as strong evidence (`fuse.py` lines 109 to 121 and 156; `gl_fuse.py` lines 60 and
   131 to 132). Once nitrogen, base-peak intensity, mass and group size are accounted for, the cs flag itself adds
   nothing (coefficient −0.03 ± 0.05 on GLACIER's per-spectrum reciprocal rank).
3. **No choice of spectra or aggregate repairs it** (section 5): [M+H]+ spectra only, the best-matching spectrum, a
   peak-count-weighted mean, single-energy spectra only, unfragmented spectra dropped, the precursor region removed
   from the similarity. None is positive beyond two standard errors anywhere, and each loses a type beyond one in at
   least one arm. Inside cs: [M+H]+ only `c2` +0.015 ± 0.023 (`ctrl`), +0.004 ± 0.019 (`pub`), against the −0.123 the
   forward stage costs there.
4. **Mirror questions** (section 4). Only [M+Na]+ covered (6 panel, 13 mixed molecules): the forward stage costs `c2`
   0.04 to 0.11 in all three sets, never beyond one standard error; 2 to 3% of the molecules. Few against many spectra:

   with three or more; it gains 0.03 to 0.18 on `dA` / `dB` in both groups, so a lower weight for few spectra is the

5. **Config bagging does nothing** (reciprocal-rank fusion of the lists at forward weights 1.0, 0.75, 0.5 against 0.75

**Mechanism, as far as the data carry it.** In cs the forward similarities separate the candidates less (in-group
standard deviation 0.08 to 0.09 against 0.12 to 0.15) and rank the answer lower, in a group twice as large. The fusion
z-scores each column inside the group (`fuse._z`, `gl_fuse._z`), so the column has unit spread whether the raw
similarities differ by 0.05 or by 0.20: a weak column gets the pull of a strong one. With 46 rivals instead of 23,
one of them beats a first-placed answer more often. This is a property of the rule (z-scoring discards the strength
of the evidence), measured in section 6; it is not an error in which spectra are scored. **Whether the forward models
are worse on these molecules for a chemical reason (no good protonation site) or because their spectra are weak
cannot be separated here; the regression says intensity, mass and group size are enough.**

## 6. A forward term that keeps the strength of the evidence

**The rule** (stated with its constants before it was replayed). Inside a same-formula group, the forward column of
model k is multiplied by sd_k / ref_k, where sd_k is the standard deviation of the raw similarities in the group and
ref = 0.10 (ICEBERG), 0.12 (GLACIER), round numbers between the panel's and the mixed regime's median in-group
spreads. That is z(ranker) + λ (x − group mean) / ref: the forward term on a fixed scale instead of a per-group one.

cut. A capped variant, min(1, sd / ref), was stated with it. The MetFrag gap term is left as it is.

## 9. Honesty and limits

- **The panel is leaked for most public models** (`reviews/panel2-2026-10-05.md`): the public ranker was trained with
  these molecules, ICEBERG / GLACIER have very probably seen 245 of the 250 structures, the answers are famous. A
  leaked ranker has the pool answer first, so any forward term can only cost there and every rule that weakens the
  forward term where it is noisy looks good on `c1` / `c2`. This applies in full to the cs loss, to the spread rule's
  `c2` gain on the panel, and to the stage-A replay of the fresh timsTOF set. It is the pattern that made forward
  weights 0.5 look better than 0.75 locally while the leaderboard preferred 0.75. What speaks against "only the
  leak" for the spread rule: no answer-removed cost in three sets, and a gain outside cs in the mixed regime.
- **Found and confirmed on the same molecules.** cs: panel and mixed regime (anatomy study). The explanation of
  section 3 (weak evidence, large groups): the same molecules; the spread-reliability relation was then seen again
  on the two fresh sets. The spread rule: constants fixed beforehand, but the uncapped variant is one of two, the
  cs-only restriction and the GLACIER-only split were added after the first panel result, and 40-odd levers were
  replayed in all; a best-of-many correction was not computed. The fresh sets were used for nothing but section 7.
- **The fresh sets are not what was asked for**: no cs natural products exist outside the panel; the fresh replay is
  stage A on pool answers only, without the join, on lists of the ranker study (the fold-safe FPNet, public ranker).
  The answer-removed side of every lever is untested on fresh molecules.
- **Small segments**: 38 cs molecules on the panel, 26 in the mixed regime, 6 and 13 with only [M+Na]+ covered. One
  molecule is 0.026 of a cs mean. No in-segment number here has a standard error under 0.015.
- **Per-spectrum numbers** use the `c2` lists of the `ctrl` arm only, and the pool candidates of the answer's formula
  in the first 60 rows; the regression's t values ignore that spectra of one molecule are dependent.
- **Run-to-run differences of the forward scores** (section 1) are small but not zero; they move 2 to 3% of the lists
  below the answer. Local forward scores are not bit-identical to Kaggle's either (`reviews/fwd-replay-2026-10-05.md`).
- **Local stand-ins as in the panel2 and anatomy reviews**: RDKit 2026.03.3 in the runners (`--any-rdkit`), no time
  budgets, the `pub` arm's FPNet A instead of the production `fpnet_full1`, the arm's own PubChem selection.
- **The hidden test's adduct mix is not documented** (`docs/competition/data.md` gives the ten adduct names and
  nothing on shares); every share above is the panel's.
- **Not done**: a full-replay fresh regime; GLACIER's precursor-free similarity on the panel, and ICEBERG's per-spectrum similarities in the mixed regime (its aggregate rows are GLACIER only): both were queued behind the fresh sets and stopped for time; a negative-mode
  forward model; any Kaggle-side run; `EXPERIMENTS.md` / `INDEX.md` entries (other agents work in the tree; run
  `python tools/gen_index.py` when this file is committed).
- **Failed or corrected on the way**: the first replay of the spread rule also rescaled the MetFrag gap term of the
  uncovered molecules (the patch caught every `rerank_multi` call); fixed, and every number here is from the rerun.
  Starting a second and a third runner to save time backfired: each `gpushare` instance read the others' memory as a
  game and dropped to duty 0.25; the two extra runners were stopped after about seven minutes (their partial results
  were kept; my runners held at most about 3.9 GB together). A queue shell script was edited while it ran and was
  replaced by `fm_queue.py`. One shell here-document failed on quoting. The fresh sets took 1.7 hours of GPU wall time
  instead of one. Several waits hit the ten-minute foreground limit, and the session was interrupted twice by an API
  error; the jobs ran on and nothing was restarted twice. No runner failed a prediction (fail 0 in every chunk).
