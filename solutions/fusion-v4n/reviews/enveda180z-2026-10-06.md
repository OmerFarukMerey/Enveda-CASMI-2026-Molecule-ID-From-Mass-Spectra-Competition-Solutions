# The Enveda-180 Zenodo release: what it holds beyond `train.parquet` (2026-10-06)

**The release adds nothing useful to the pipeline as it stands.** Its structures beyond train are Enamine screening
compounds, structurally far from the Enveda natural products, and neither engine would take a better analog from them.
No library was extended, no engine re-run, no replay, no dataset folder, no flag, no preset.

Offline only. Nothing was submitted, pushed, committed or uploaded; no `kaggle` command was run; no model was trained;
the visible `test.parquet` was not read; no NIST data. Web: the Zenodo API record and one file of it (366 MB). GPU: not
used. CPU: at most four workers or numba threads of mine at below-normal priority. Scripts: `scratch/zen180/`
(git-ignored, `s0` to `s8`). Download: `D:\casmi-data\input\zenodo\enveda-180\` (0.78 GB with the decompressed copy).
Outputs: `D:\casmi-data\zen180\` (0.47 GB, all new; other caches were read, never written; numba caches went to
`zen180\numba_cache`). **No file of the solution tree was changed except this review** (`INDEX.md` not regenerated,
`EXPERIMENTS.md` has no entry).

**Every number below was computed in this study** unless a file is named. Keys: `casmi.chem.score_key` (the metric
key, RDKit 2026.03.3 asserted in every worker) on the release's SMILES; train keys from `train_structs.parquet` of
`casmi26-v2-pool`. **No leaderboard estimate is given.**

## 0. Bottom line

1. **Compliance: usable.** CC BY 4.0, published by Enveda (the competition hosts are among the authors), measured
   in-house on a Bruker timsTOF Pro 2 from Enamine compounds; nothing in the record refers to NIST. Host: "fine to
   use" (section 1).
2. **Beyond train, by structure**: 3,717 metric keys are not in train. 817 of them have a standard-adduct spectrum
   (3,194 spectra, 408 with [M+H]+; the forum counted 816 / 3,193 / 408), 2,900 appear only as in-source-fragment
   adducts. All 3,717 carry an Enamine catalogue id; none is a pool row, none is in engine 2's COCONUT / ChEBI /
   LIPID MAPS sets, 3,594 are PubChem-tier rows.
3. **Beyond train, by spectrum**: 1,073,683 of the 1,175,202 rows are acquisitions train already has (same
   structure, adduct, collision energy and base-peak count), here with a noise-filtered peak list (median 14 peaks
   against 138 in train). 101,519 rows are not in train: 14,341 of the new structures and 87,178 of train
   structures, 77,722 of the latter in adducts train does not have for that structure, almost all in-source
   fragments.
4. **Analog similarity (the test asked for in step 3)**: with the Zenodo-only structures added under each engine's
   own representative rule and similarity kernel, the best analog of a panel molecule comes from them for **0 of 250
   (v1 engine) and 2 of 250 (engine 2)**; the best similarity rises by 0.02 or more for **0 of 250 in both**
   (largest rise 0.017). Mixed regime, 380 molecules: 0 and 2 to 5 molecules; rise of 0.02 or more for 0 and 2. The
   threshold for step 4 was 10% of the panel. **Steps 4 and 5 were not run.**
5. **Why**: nearest-neighbour Tanimoto of the panel's natural products to the rest of train is 0.78 in the median;
   to the 3,717 new structures it is 0.22 (maximum 0.58), and for 0 of 250 molecules a new structure is closer than
   the nearest train structure. The new structures are ordinary Enveda-180 chemistry (nearest train neighbour 0.66
   in the median, the same as an Enveda-180 train structure has to the rest of train).
6. **One thing in the release is new information, and it is for training, not for the libraries**: timsTOF spectra
   of the test's water-loss adducts. Train has 24 timsTOF [M-H2O+H]+ spectra, 8 [M-2H2O+H]+ and 1 [M-H2O-H]-; the
   release has 11,600, 1,486 and 903 (3,775 / 498 / 368 structures), all drug-like. Not used here (section 5).
7. A side observation, not a recommendation: treating the whole filtered file as a second library raises the best
   similarity (the same Enveda-180 structures score higher with noise-free peaks) and makes the analog lists of the
   natural products structurally worse (section 4.3).

## 4. Can it help the libraries (step 3)

### 4.1 Method

`s6_analog.py`, `s7_report.py` → `analog_{panel,mixed}_{v1,e2}_{A,B,C}.parquet`, `s7_report.json`. This is the
engines' analog search re-implemented from their code with their own kernels, not a run of the engines:

- **v1 engine** (`casmi/engine.py`, `Engine._reps` and `analogs`): one representative per structure, polarity and
  library (most cleaned peaks), from the spectrum cache `casmi.build` writes (`D:\casmi-data\ranker_study\work`,
  2,539,608 spectra, 445,686 representatives); the query spectra merged per polarity; `casmi.spectra.search_shift`
  (hybrid entropy similarity) with `SearchCfg` defaults, ±200 Da, same polarity.
- **Engine 2** (`nb/embed/eng/casmi_engine.py`, `_reps` and `analog_search`, set "all"): one representative per
  structure (timsTOF first, then most raw peaks; 275,810), every query spectrum on its own, the maximum of direct
  and shifted entropy similarity (`pv.search_shift_pre`), ±200 Da. Peaks were taken from the v1 cache and passed
  through `pv._clean`, which differs from the engine only by the cache's cut above precursor + 2 Da.
- Zenodo spectra: cleaned with `casmi.build.clean_block` (the settings of the train cache), structure mass from the
  SMILES.
- Queries: the 250 panel molecules of `reviews/panel2-2026-10-05.md` (1,184 timsTOF spectra) and the 380 molecules
  of the mixed regime (1,423 spectra); the target mass is the answer's mass. "The Enveda natural products of train
  in general" are the same 250 structures (the panel is all of `enveda-np-examples`), so there is no larger
  natural-product set to report.
- Leak rule: every train representative with the answer's metric key is removed (the `c2` / `dA` / `dB` convention)
  and so is every Zenodo row with that key. That removes nothing for the panel (0 of 250 keys are in the release)
  and the own spectra of 40 mixed-regime molecules (the Enveda-180 hold-outs).

Variants of what is added:

- **A**: the 817 new structures, representatives chosen among their standard-adduct spectra (894 representatives).
- **B**: all 3,717 new structures, any adduct (4,120 representatives).
- **C** (hypothetical, see 4.3): the whole filtered file as a separate library, one representative per structure
  and polarity (249,340).

A and B are what appending the release to the libraries does for analogs. Appending the rows of structures train
already has changes almost nothing under the engines' own rules, because the train row has more peaks and stays
the representative: a Zenodo row would become the representative for 4,442 of 245,311 structure-polarity groups in
the v1 engine (1,462 of them a polarity the structure does not have in train) and for 955 of 180,371 structures in
engine 2 (`s8_repwin.json`).

### 4.4 The other two indirect routes

- **(c) Extra spectra of train structures for molecules that are in train.** This can only matter for a hidden
  molecule that is an Enveda-180 compound: 0 of the 250 Enveda natural products are, and 50 of the release's
  184,088 structures are COCONUT rows. The extra rows are in-source fragments of drug-like compounds.
- **(b) timsTOF training signal.** Not measured (no model was trained). What is there: 101,519 timsTOF spectra
  train does not have, 26,580 of them in the test's ten adducts. The part that is new in kind:

  | Adduct | timsTOF spectra (structures) in train | Zenodo rows not in train (structures) |
  |---|---|---|
  | [M-H2O+H]+ | 24 (22) | 11,600 (3,775) |
  | [M-2H2O+H]+ | 8 (7) | 1,486 (498) |
  | [M-H2O-H]- | 1 (1) | 903 (368) |
  | [M+NH4]+ | 4,800 (1,605) | 1,242 (383) |
  | [M+H]+ | 589,076 (167,406) | 8,064 (2,506) |
  | [M-H]- | 185,023 (65,996) | 2,213 (857) |

  Train's water-loss spectra are almost all from other instruments (21,658 [M-H2O+H]+ rows, 24 of them timsTOF).
  `reviews/anatomy-2026-10-06.md` (item 3) found that molecules with an [M+NH4]+ or water-loss spectrum are where the
  forward models cost pool answers; a water-loss model or surrogate trained on timsTOF data would have its first
  sizeable training set here. Limits: drug-like chemistry only, and the labels are the release's own in-source
  annotation (parent structure with a fragment adduct), which I did not audit. The filtered peak lists and the
  per-spectrum `explained_intensity` could also serve as a noise label for train's 1.07 million matching rows. None
  of this was tried.

## 5. Steps 4 and 5

Not run: step 3 is below its threshold. For the record, from reading the code only (nothing was built):

- **Engine 2** reads `train.parquet` in `load_library` and builds its index at run time; rows in train's schema
  could be appended to the file it is given. Structures enter its candidate set only through that file as well.
- **v1 engine**: the spectrum cache is also built at run time (`casmi.build.build_spec_cache`, called in
  `nb/05_v1_engine.py`), but every spectrum is tied to a row of the packaged `train_structs.parquet` by InChIKey14;
  a spectrum of an unknown structure gets `sid` −1 and would index the wrong mass in `Library`. New structures need
  appended rows in `train_structs.parquet` and `train_fp_sel.npy` (the library sorts by mass itself, so appending
  is enough there). Making them candidates is a different matter: the pool is mass-sorted with `pool_fp.npy` and the
  fragment arrays aligned by row, so 817 structures mean rewriting those arrays, and whatever is keyed to pool row
  ids. Not timed.

## 6. Not done, not verified

- The unfiltered files of the record (1.7 to 2.6 GB each) were not downloaded. Whether train's `enveda-180` equals
  the unfiltered release, and whether that release holds standard-adduct spectra for the 2,900 fragment-only
  structures, is unknown.
- The analog result comes from a re-implementation with the engines' kernels and representative rules, not from
  engine runs: the answer's exact mass stands in for the engines' target mass, the query cleaning is
  `casmi.spectra.clean_peaks` with the cache's settings (I did not check that `v1engine` cleans queries the same
  way), engine 2's representatives were read from the v1 cache, and only engine 2's "all" set was computed, not its
  per-polarity lists.
- No engine, forward model or replay was run on extended libraries; exposure was not measured. The direct-library
  channel (`lib_max`) with appended rows was not computed: on the panel no query structure is in the release.
- "Same acquisition" rests on four fields and two hand checks; the 8,739 rows with a different base-peak count
  were not resolved.
- Class of the new structures: Enamine id, COCONUT membership and five descriptors; no natural-product-likeness
  score was computed.
- The licence was read from the record's metadata through the Zenodo API; the repository, the paper and the forum
  thread were not opened. The host quote is the one in our landscape note.
- The fine-tuning value of the water-loss spectra is a count, not a result.
