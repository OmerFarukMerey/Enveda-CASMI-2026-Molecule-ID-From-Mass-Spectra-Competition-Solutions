# A1: the direct-hit overlay on our labelled lists (2026-11-13)

Plan: `docs/plan/research-2026-11-13.md` section 5, A1. Scripts (not in git): `scratch/a1/a1_ext.py`, `a1_cos.py`,
`a1_eval.py`, `a1_split.py`; log `scratch/a1/a1_eval.log`. CPU only, about 10 minutes.

## Setup
- Lists: the clean instrument's 600 held-out molecules, variant `s1` (`casmi-work/clean/replay/rows.parquet`; recomputed
  MRR 0.6029, identical to the file). 250 are the Enveda NP panel, 350 are enveda-180.
- Queries: the molecules' own timsTOF spectra (`dump_a|b/s1val/val_test.parquet`).
- "External library": the 188,429 non-Enveda train spectra inside the queries' precursor windows. The overlay's exact rule:
  same ion mode, 10 ppm + 0.002 Da, sqrt-intensity cosine, >= 4 matched peaks, best structure promoted to rank 1,
  lists with `library_max` >= LIB_TAU untouched.
- Arm P: the answer's references are in the external library. Arm A: they are removed (the answer has no public
  spectrum). Arm A can only lose; the question is how much against arm P's gain.

## Reading
- In arm A the pipeline's leader was correct in 52 of the 70 fired lists and the overlay replaced it with a wrong isomer.
  Almost all of this is the NP panel (63 fires of 250); enveda-180 fires 7 of 350.
- The band `library_max` 0.7 to 0.9 carries four fifths of the loss and almost none of the gain: when the pipeline's own
  library already holds a fair match, the pipeline has used that evidence, and a second library vote adds isomer errors.
- Thresholds and margins on the external cosine do not separate the arms (same finding as the research note, section 4).
  The list's own `library_max` does.
- Candidate: the same cell with `LIB_TAU = 0.70`. It keeps 92% of arm P's gain and removes 81% of arm A's loss.

## Caveats
- The external library here is train minus Enveda, so in arm A the overlay's wrong match and the list's `library_max`
  come from the same spectra. The real external datasets are GNPS + MassBank + MoNA merges, which train also contains,
  so the overlap is real, but references that exist only outside train would not raise `library_max`.
- Lists are the s1 line (0.451 era), not the 0.487 chain.
- The public effect is unknown: if the one public molecule behind 0.481 -> 0.487 has `library_max` in 0.7 to 0.9, the
  variant scores 0.481 on the public part while being safer on the private part.

## Review by the research lead (same day): the LIB_TAU 0.70 candidate is withdrawn
Details: `docs/plan/research-2026-11-13.md` section 9; scenario split in `scratch/research1113/a1_eval_rl.py`.
- 20 of arm A's 59 losses are class-`lib` molecules whose references the pipeline saw and the overlay did not. That
  cannot occur on the test, and 18 of them sit in the 0.7 to 0.9 band, so the table above overstates the benefit.
- All of arm P's gain comes from molecules whose references the pipeline never saw (class `all`). Where it had seen
  them the overlay never helped (48 fires, 6 losses).
- `library_max` separates the arms only because the same train spectra produce both numbers. For a reference that
  exists only outside train, LIB_TAU protects nothing; 8 of the 23 real wins sit in the band and would be lost.
- Board evidence: the public gain comes from the MassBank file alone (extlib only 0.481, MassBank only 0.487), and a
  train-like 421k-spectrum library given priority scored 0.467. The replay's losses are real for train-like
  references; ax13's libraries do not behave like one on the public part.
- Not submitted. Next test needs the two real libraries locally: tag each reference as train structure or not, and
  judge "promote only structures absent from train" on fires split by that tag.
