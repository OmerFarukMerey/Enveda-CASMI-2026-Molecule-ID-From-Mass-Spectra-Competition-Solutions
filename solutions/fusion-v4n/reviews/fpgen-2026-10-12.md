## 2. The noise model and its calibration (phase 0)

Conditioning on a *predicted* fingerprint only helps if the training fingerprints look like predictions. The calibration set is 19,937
held-out structures (folds `hold` / `np`, none of them a benchmark molecule or in set F) whose aggregated fold-safe logits were computed
with our own code path; against the stored DEV logits of `gen2` the path agrees to a median correlation of 1.000 (minimum 0.988 over 60
molecules, sign agreement 99.6%) **[M]**.

Per bit j: the two class-conditional means of the raw logit (`mu1`, `mu0`) and their pooled standard deviation `sd`; the conditioning
value is the Gaussian posterior `p_j = sigmoid(w_j z_j + c_j)`, and a training fingerprint is corrupted by drawing
`z_j ~ N(mu_{b_j}, scale * sd_j)` and applying the same map. Numbers on the half of the calibration set that was not used for the fit **[M]**:

| quantity | predicted (real network) | simulated (noise model) | truth |
|---|---|---|---|
| sum of the conditioning vector | 966.6 | 952.5 | 955.6 on-bits |
| bits above 0.5 | 357.4 | 348.9 | |
| Tanimoto (above 0.5) against the true bits | 0.220 | 0.218 | |
| Brier against the true bits | 0.0626 | 0.0624 | |

Reliability (bin of the predicted probability, share of bits, true rate, rate under the simulation): 0.05 / 71.4% / 0.020 / 0.020;
0.15 / 12.2% / 0.149 / 0.152; 0.25 / 7.1% / 0.241 / 0.244; 0.45 / 2.0% / 0.427 / 0.432; 0.75 / 0.6% / 0.711 / 0.719;
0.95 / 0.3% / 0.913 / 0.929 **[M]**. The calibration is close to the diagonal and the simulated vectors match the real ones in sum,
Tanimoto and Brier to three decimals, which is what the method needs.

**What the table also says, and it decides the outcome below:** the conditioning signal is weak per bit. The median separation of the
two class-conditional means is **0.32 standard deviations**, and the thresholded prediction shares only **22%** Tanimoto with the true
fingerprint. The network is good at *ranking* a true structure against decoys (hold-out top-1 0.68 **[C ownfp]**) but a single predicted
bit says little, and a generator has nothing but those bits.

## 3. What was built (phase 0)

| part | decision | reason |
|---|---|---|
| representation | SMILES, stereo-free canonical, 8 stored randomised SMILES per structure, one drawn per sample | `selfies` is not installed and installs are forbidden; the formula constraint is enforced in the decoder and by an exact-formula filter, so the validity guarantee of SELFIES buys little |
| vocabulary | 55 atom-level tokens (tokens seen fewer than 20 times dropped; 677,630 of 677,745 structures encode) **[M]** | |
| conditioning | the pipeline's own 10,226 selected bits as calibrated probabilities, four prefix tokens from a two-layer map | the bit set the fold-safe network predicts; the engine's own fingerprint |
| formula | one prefix token (9 heavy-element counts and hydrogens, counts and log1p); while decoding, an atom token whose element is exhausted is masked and EOS is masked until every heavy atom is used; a sample is kept only when its exact formula matches | hydrogens cannot be counted from a SMILES string |
| model | transformer decoder, d 512, 8 layers, 8 heads, 31.94 M parameters **[M]** | the 10 to 30 M band of the task |
| trainer | teacher forcing, AdamW, cosine schedule, mixed precision, GPU sharing (duty 0.8 / game 0.25, yield above 9 GB, 8 GB cap), resume, ledger | `train/README.md` rules |

**Throughput on the RTX 4070 Ti SUPER at duty 0.8** **[M]**: batch 128 / 192 / 256 give 6.3 / 5.2 / 4.1 steps/s at 4.5 / 7.9 / 12.5 GB
reserved; the chosen batch **160 runs at 7.3 steps/s (1,170 structures/s) in 7.7 GB** inside the 8 GB cap. One pass over the 677,745
training structures takes 9.7 min. Sampling: 2,237 sequences/s at batch 2,048 (4.4 GB) with a preallocated key-value cache, against 51/s
with a cache that is concatenated per step — the preallocation is what makes 10,000 samples per molecule affordable **[M]**.

**The 20-minute smoke run found a real bug.** The first run (6,000 steps, loss 1.03) sampled 15,000 sequences of which 0.6% parsed:
the training targets were shifted by one position (the slice of the logits started at the last prefix token, so the model learned to
predict a token from its predecessor's predecessor). After the fix a 2,000-step run already gives 28.4% parsable and 13.4% exactly
formula-matching samples **[M]**, and the loop runs end to end: train -> sample -> validate -> rank -> metrics.

## 7. Gate verdict, the limiting factor, what is not verified

**The gate fails.** It required, with predicted fingerprints and the pipeline's formulas, at least 10% of molecules with
the answer in the top 25 **and** at least double the rule-based generator on F and on the scaffold hold-out:

| criterion | required | measured | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| top-25 at least 10% on the fold-clean novel sets | N, F, SH all ≥ 10% | 10.6% / 0.0% / 4.0% | **fails on F and SH** (passes on N alone) |
| double the rule baseline on F | ≥ 9.0% | 0.0% | **fails** |
| double the rule baseline on SH | ≥ 86.0% (production) or ≥ 32.7% (scaffold banned) | 4.0% | **fails** |

**The limiting factor is the conditioning fingerprint, not the decoder and not the ranking.** Three measurements point
the same way. (1) The decoder works: 53% of its samples are valid structures with exactly the right formula, it writes
roughly 500 distinct isomers per molecule, and the training loss is still falling. (2) The ranking works: a generated
answer lands at median rank 3.5. (3) The information is not there: the fold-safe network's prediction shares 22% Tanimoto
with the true fingerprint and its median per-bit separation is 0.32 standard deviations (section 2); the network is built
to *rank* a true structure against decoys, not to *describe* it. Giving the generator the true formula changes nothing
(+1.2 points on N, +0.7 on F), which rules the formula out as the limit. The true-fingerprint arm, which would put a
number on how much of the gap is the fingerprint, **was not reached** (section 5) — this is the one piece of the
diagnosis that rests on the calibration numbers rather than on an end-to-end measurement.

**Could more training close the gap?** Not plausibly at this scale. The gap is a factor of 5 on N (10.6% against 53.1%)
and total on F (0.0% against 4.5%): the rule-based generator wins because it starts from a known pool structure that is
already 0.85 Tanimoto from the answer, while the learned generator starts from a fingerprint that locates the answer only
to within 0.3 to 0.5 Tanimoto. Doubling the compute moves a language model's likelihood, not the mutual information
between its conditioning vector and the target. What would have to change first is the conditioning: a network trained to
*predict* a fingerprint well (bit-level recall, not candidate ranking), or conditioning on the spectrum itself instead of
on a fingerprint bottleneck, or a generator that starts from retrieved neighbours (the rule-based channel) and learns to
edit them. None of that is a phase-2 of this build.

**Recommendation: do not go to phase 2.** The analog channel of `gen3` remains the better instrument for the
no-database block, and it is already measured, bounded and cheap on Kaggle. No integration, no upload, no submission was
prepared here, as the task required.

**Not verified / not measured.** The true-fingerprint upper bound and the whole ablation stage (section 5); the
10,000-sample budget (only 1,000 was run); whether a longer schedule would raise the 53% formula-exact rate; the behaviour
of the generator on the hidden test's chemistry (the three sets disagree with each other by a factor of three, and which
one the hidden novel molecules resemble is unknown); anything about the leaderboard — no estimate is given. The SH
baseline is reported in two variants because the production rule generator may use the answer's scaffold mates as parents
while the learned generator never saw the scaffold; the gate was judged against the production variant, as pre-registered.
