2. **What limits it: two things stacked, and not the ranking.** (a) The predicted fingerprint: with the answer's true bits
   the same decoder reaches 32.7% on N and 15.3% on SH, three to four times as much. (b) The decoder: even with the true
   bits it misses two thirds of N, 85% of SH and all of F, so **a perfect fingerprint would not pass the gate either**. The

   number of steps, sets the level (section 5.2).
3. **It adds almost nothing to the channel we already have.** Of its 27 top-25 answers on N, 23 are already among the
   rule-based generator's 25 rows (union 54.7% against 53.1%); on SH 11 of 12; on F it has none.
4. **The remaining budget was not spent on training** (4.95 of 14 GPU hours used). A run five times as long could not

## 2. The noise model and its calibration (phase 0)

Conditioning on a *predicted* fingerprint only helps if the training fingerprints look like predictions. The calibration set is 19,937
held-out structures (folds `hold` / `np`, none of them a benchmark molecule or in set F) whose aggregated fold-safe logits were computed
with our own code path; against the stored DEV logits of `gen2` the path agrees to a median correlation of 1.000 (minimum 0.988 over 60
molecules, sign agreement 99.6%) **[M]**.

Per bit j: the two class-conditional means of the raw logit (`mu1`, `mu0`) and their pooled standard deviation `sd`; the conditioning
value is the Gaussian posterior `p_j = sigmoid(w_j z_j + c_j)`, and a training fingerprint is corrupted by drawing
`z_j ~ N(mu_{b_j}, scale * sd_j)` and applying the same map. Numbers on the half of the calibration set that was not used for the fit **[M]**:

| quantity | predicted (real network) | simulated (noise model) | truth |
|---|---|---|---|
| sum of the conditioning vector | 966.6 | 952.5 | 955.6 on-bits |
| bits above 0.5 | 357.4 | 348.9 | |
| Tanimoto (above 0.5) against the true bits | 0.220 | 0.218 | |
| Brier against the true bits | 0.0626 | 0.0624 | |

Reliability (bin of the predicted probability, share of bits, true rate, rate under the simulation): 0.05 / 71.4% / 0.020 / 0.020;
0.15 / 12.2% / 0.149 / 0.152; 0.25 / 7.1% / 0.241 / 0.244; 0.45 / 2.0% / 0.427 / 0.432; 0.75 / 0.6% / 0.711 / 0.719;
0.95 / 0.3% / 0.913 / 0.929 **[M]**. The calibration is close to the diagonal and the simulated vectors match the real ones in sum,
Tanimoto and Brier to three decimals, which is what the method needs.

**What the table also says, and it is one of the two things that decide the outcome below:** the conditioning signal is weak per bit. The median separation of the
two class-conditional means is **0.32 standard deviations**, and the thresholded prediction shares only **22%** Tanimoto with the true
fingerprint. The network is good at *ranking* a true structure against decoys (hold-out top-1 0.68 **[C ownfp]**) but a single predicted
bit says little, and a generator has nothing but those bits (measured end to end in section 5.2).

## 3. What was built (phase 0)

| part | decision | reason |
|---|---|---|
| representation | SMILES, stereo-free canonical, 8 stored randomised SMILES per structure, one drawn per sample | `selfies` is not installed and installs are forbidden; the formula constraint is enforced in the decoder and by an exact-formula filter, so the validity guarantee of SELFIES buys little |
| vocabulary | 55 atom-level tokens (tokens seen fewer than 20 times dropped; 677,630 of 677,745 structures encode) **[M]** | |
| conditioning | the pipeline's own 10,226 selected bits as calibrated probabilities, four prefix tokens from a two-layer map | the bit set the fold-safe network predicts; the engine's own fingerprint |
| formula | one prefix token (9 heavy-element counts and hydrogens, counts and log1p); while decoding, an atom token whose element is exhausted is masked and EOS is masked until every heavy atom is used; a sample is kept only when its exact formula matches | hydrogens cannot be counted from a SMILES string |
| model | transformer decoder, d 512, 8 layers, 8 heads, 31.94 M parameters **[M]** | the 10 to 30 M band of the task |
| trainer | teacher forcing, AdamW, cosine schedule, mixed precision, GPU sharing (duty 0.8 / game 0.25, yield above 9 GB, 8 GB cap), resume, ledger | `train/README.md` rules |

**Throughput on the RTX 4070 Ti SUPER at duty 0.8** **[M]**: batch 128 / 192 / 256 give 6.3 / 5.2 / 4.1 steps/s at 4.5 / 7.9 / 12.5 GB
reserved; the chosen batch **160 runs at 7.3 steps/s (1,170 structures/s) in 7.7 GB** inside the 8 GB cap. One pass over the 677,745
training structures takes 9.7 min. Sampling: 2,237 sequences/s at batch 2,048 (4.4 GB) with a preallocated key-value cache, against 51/s
with a cache that is concatenated per step — the preallocation is what makes 10,000 samples per molecule affordable **[M]**.

**The 20-minute smoke run found a real bug.** The first run (6,000 steps, loss 1.03) sampled 15,000 sequences of which 0.6% parsed:
the training targets were shifted by one position (the slice of the logits started at the last prefix token, so the model learned to
predict a token from its predecessor's predecessor). After the fix a 2,000-step run already gives 28.4% parsable and 13.4% exactly
formula-matching samples **[M]**, and the loop runs end to end: train -> sample -> validate -> rank -> metrics.

### 5.2 Diagnosis: what limits it

Five measurements, all on `ref0` (scripts `scratch/fpgen/diag_*.py`, `cpu_eval.py`; the CPU ones were run while the user's
game held the GPU).

**(a) True against predicted fingerprint.** The table above: with the answer's true bits the decoder triples its top-25
rate on N (10.6 → 32.7%) and nearly quadruples it on SH (4.0 → 15.3%), and does nothing on F (0.0 → 0.3%). The same
contrast on the 100-molecule sub-samples at K 200, on the CPU: N 22% against 5% (+17 ± 4 points), F 0% against 0%, SH 1%
against 1% **[M]**. Caveat: `ref0` was trained on noise-model vectors only, and clean 0/1 bits are slightly off its training
distribution (on training structures its loss is 0.537 per token with true bits against 0.487 with noise-model vectors
**[M]**), so the true-fingerprint row is the upper bound of *this* decoder, not of a decoder trained for true bits; that
arm (`--noise 0`) was not run.

**(b) What the decoder writes when it misses** (gate arm, the molecules whose answer was not generated; at most the 500
most frequent distinct structures per molecule; `diag_miss.py`) **[M]**:

| | N (226 misses) | F (289) | SH (288) |
|---|---|---|---|
| valid, formula-exact share of the 1,000 samples (all molecules of the set) | 42.8% | 67.7% | 47.8% |
| distinct structures per molecule (median, capped at 500) | 391 | 500 | 479 |
| the answer's exact Murcko scaffold is among the samples (molecules with a scaffold) | 30.9% | 8.7% | 6.6% |
| ... its generic scaffold | 45.0% | 28.4% | 13.2% |
| share of all valid samples that carry the generic scaffold | 3.7% | 0.5% | 0.1% |
| best Tanimoto of a sample to the answer, engine fingerprint (median) | 0.55 | 0.48 | 0.47 |
| `f.z` of the true structure / of the best sample / of the 25th sample (medians) | 0.48 / 0.41 / 0.16 | −0.11 / −0.15 / −0.44 | 0.15 / 0.21 / −0.03 |
| **the true structure would be rank 1 among the samples** | **55%** | **53%** | **32%** |
| **... would be inside the top 25** | **91%** | **94%** | **79%** |
| a sample beats the truth in soft Tanimoto to the conditioning vector | 92% | 99% | 88% |

By the score that ranks (`f.z`), the true structure agrees with the predicted fingerprint **better** than whatever the
decoder wrote for most molecules: had the answer been written it would have been ranked, so the predicted fingerprint is
good enough to *recognise* the answer among a few hundred attempts and the failure is in *writing* it. (The soft Tanimoto
to the calibrated vector says the opposite, but it mostly rewards a matching number of on-bits and it is not the ranking
score; both are reported.) The right scaffold is rarely written, and when it is, it is a fraction of a percent of the
samples on F and SH. Hits are small molecules: on SH 6 of the 12 are among the 8 molecules with at most 15 heavy atoms,
and none of the 154 molecules with 26 to 40 heavy atoms was generated **[M]**.

**(c) How much probability the answer gets** (`diag_nll.py`: teacher-forced log-likelihood of the answer's canonical and 8
randomised SMILES; `diag_nll_train.py`: the same for 400 random *training* structures) **[M]**:

| | N | F | SH | training structures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NLL of one SMILES string of the answer, predicted (training: noise-model) fingerprint, median, nats | 22.0 | 26.2 | 36.3 | 22.8 |
| ... per token | 0.555 | 0.658 | 0.728 | 0.487 |
| ... with the true fingerprint, per token | 0.451 | 0.669 | 0.563 | 0.537 |
| ... with another molecule's fingerprint (same set; training: a random structure), per token | 0.725 | 0.783 | 0.865 | 0.843 |
| lower bound of P(answer among 1,000 / 10,000 samples) from the 9 strings | 4.6 / 13.1% | 0.0 / 0.1% | 0.5 / 1.3% | 4.3 / 7.5% |

A structure has many SMILES spellings (collision entropy of 2,000 RDKit random SMILES: about 7.5 to 8.8 nats, 20 molecules
per set **[M]**), so the answer's probability per sample is roughly e^-14 (N), e^-18 (F), e^-27 (SH) at the median, where
1,000 samples need about e^-7. Two things follow. First, on N the held-out answers are written **about as well as the
structures the model was trained on** (0.555 against 0.487 nats per token; 9-string bound 4.6% against 4.3%): on N-like
chemistry the decoder is not under-trained relative to its own fit, it is at the level this recipe gives, and that level
is low because a noise-model fingerprint leaves on the order of 15 nats of structure undetermined even for a training
structure. Second, on F and SH there is a generalisation gap on top (+0.17 and +0.24 nats per token, 3 to 14 nats per
string): F is synthetic chemistry that COCONUT and the train structures do not cover, SH is an unseen scaffold.

**(d) Where samples die under the hard formula constraint** (`diag_die.py`, 11,867 sequences of 120 molecules, CPU)
**[M]**: accepted 52.7%; **SMILES syntax 23.4%**; hydrogen count 12.8% (off by exactly two in 91%; too many hydrogens,
that is one ring or double bond too few, in 73%); valence 6.1%; kekulisation 4.7%; no sequence ran out of length and none
had the wrong heavy atoms. Per set the syntax share is 10% (F), 30% (N), 30% (SH). The syntax failures are an artefact of
the constraint: once the last allowed heavy atom is spent only bonds, brackets, ring digits and the end token remain, and
the string ends with an open branch (44% of 461 failures in 1,800 further samples), an unclosed ring (32%) or, in the
remaining third, a dangling bond symbol (`...C(O)=`) — the decoder does not plan to its atom budget **[M]**. A
grammar-aware mask would raise the valid rate, but these are strings that wanted more atoms than the formula has; they are
not lost answers, and the valid samples already give 400 to 500 distinct structures per molecule. With the true formula
56% are accepted against 48% under a wrong pipeline formula. The valid rate is not what limits the result.

**(e) Learning curve.** `train.py` overwrites its resume file, so **no intermediate checkpoint of `ref0` exists** and the
curve the task asked for (checkpoints every 2,500 steps) could not be drawn. What exists: the training loss (0.841 at 2,000
steps, 0.602 at 10,400, 0.534 at 20,000, 0.511 at 26,000, 0.495 at 35,000; the last 9,000 steps were at a learning rate
below 3e-5, so the flattening is partly the schedule) **[M]**; the 2,000-step model `smoke2` (loss 0.847, its own short
schedule) on the gate arm at K 1,000: 11.0% valid samples and top-25 0.0 / 0.0 / 0.3% against 53.1% and 10.6 / 0.0 / 4.0%
for `ref0` (+10.6 ± 1.9 points on N, +3.7 ± 1.1 on SH) **[M]** — generation rises with training between 2,000 and 35,000
steps, and two points cannot say whether it still rises at 35,000; and measurement (c), which says more than a curve
would: on N the answers already sit at the likelihood of the training structures.

### 5.3 What was not run, and why

The remaining budget was **deliberately not spent on training**. A longer run of the reference, the reseed pair and the
ablations of `queue2.txt` (noise 0 / 1.5, train-only, PubChem arm, smaller model) cannot change the verdict: with predicted
fingerprints the gate is missed by a factor of 8 (SH, scaffold banned) to 21 (SH, production) and completely on F, and it
is missed on every line even with the **true** fingerprint (section 7). Since no ablation is reported, no reseed control
was needed. Also not run: temperature 0.7 and the soft constraint (inference variants of `queue2.txt`), the arm "true
fingerprint, true formula", and the 100-sample budget (the sample curve exists at 200 on the CPU sub-sample, at 1,000 and
at 10,000). The queue that was run is `scratch/fpgen/queue3.txt`; its training lines were cut.

## 7. Gate verdict, the limiting factor, what is not verified

**The gate fails.** It required, with predicted fingerprints and the pipeline's formulas, at least 10% of molecules with
the answer in the top 25 **and** at least double the rule-based generator on F and on the scaffold hold-out:

| criterion | required | measured (predicted fingerprint) | with the **true** fingerprint | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| top-25 at least 10% on the fold-clean novel sets | N, F, SH all ≥ 10% | 10.6% / 0.0% / 4.0% | 32.7% / 0.3% / 15.3% | **fails on F and SH** (passes on N alone) |
| double the rule baseline on F | ≥ 9.0% | 0.0% | 0.3% | **fails** |
| double the rule baseline on SH | ≥ 86.0% (production) or ≥ 32.7% (scaffold banned) | 4.0% | 15.3% | **fails** |

How far it got: on N a tenth of the answers, a fifth of what the rule-based generator reaches (10.6% against 53.1%); on
the scaffold hold-out 4.0% against 43.0% (16.3% with the scaffold banned for the rules as well); on F nothing against
4.5%. Even a perfect fingerprint would not pass the F line or the SH line.

**The limiting factor: two limits stacked, neither of them the ranking.**

1. **The predicted fingerprint costs two thirds to three quarters of what this decoder can do.** True bits instead of
   predicted ones: 10.6 → 32.7% on N, 4.0 → 15.3% on SH (section 5.2 a). The fold-safe network's thresholded prediction
   shares 22% Tanimoto with the truth (section 2); it is built to rank a structure against decoys, and it does that well
   here too — the true structure would be in the top 25 of the decoder's own samples for 79 to 94% of the missed molecules
   (5.2 b). It *recognises* an answer; it does not carry enough to *steer* a decoder to it.
2. **The decoder itself stops at a third (N), a seventh (SH) and nothing (F) even when it is told the truth.** It gives
   the answer about e^-14 to e^-27 of its probability per sample; on N that is the same as for its own training
   structures, so more steps of the same recipe do not help there; on F and SH there is a generalisation gap to chemistry
   and scaffolds that 677,745 natural-product structures do not teach (5.2 c).
3. Not limiting: the ranking (median rank 3.5 of a generated answer), the formula (the true formula changes the top-25
   rate by +1.2 / +0.7 / −0.7 points), the 53% valid rate (5.2 d), the number of samples (ten times as many add 8 ± 4 points on N and 1 point on F and SH).

The draft of this section named the fingerprint alone; the measurements correct that: the fingerprint is the larger of
the two factors on N and SH, and the decoder with its training data is the whole story on F.

**Could anything short of a much larger effort close the gap?** No. The remaining 10 GPU hours could buy a run about five
times as long as `ref0`. On N that moves a model whose held-out answers are already as likely as its training structures;
on SH the line to reach is 86% (or 32.7% in the matched comparison) from 4.0%, and the perfect-fingerprint ceiling of this
decoder is 15.3%; on F the ceiling is 0.3% against a line of 10%. Closing it would need all of: a fingerprint predictor of
a different quality class (bit-level accuracy, where ours has Tanimoto 0.22), training data that covers the hidden
chemistry at the scale MSNovelist used (millions of structures, days of training), and a decoder that plans to the formula
— each a project of its own, none of them inside the competition's remaining time. And the honest reading of 5.2 (c) is
that the predicted fingerprint, as the noise model reproduces it, leaves on the order of 15 nats (millions of comparably
likely isomers) undetermined even for a structure the decoder has seen: **it does not pin down a novel structure**, it
only ranks candidates that something else proposes. That something else exists already: the rule-based analog channel
starts from a retrieved neighbour that is 0.85 Tanimoto from the answer **[C gen2]** and is ranked by the same fingerprint.

**Recommendation: close this line. No phase 2.** The generator is below the rule-based channel on every set, adds 4
molecules of 254 on N and 1 of 300 on SH to what that channel already finds, and nothing on F. The analog channel of
`gen3` stays the instrument for the no-database block. Nothing was integrated, uploaded or submitted; the weights stay on
the local disk.

**Not verified / not measured.**
- A decoder trained on true bits (`--noise 0`): the clean upper bound of the decoder. The true-fingerprint row is `ref0`
  reading vectors it was not trained on, so the real ceiling may be somewhat higher than 32.7 / 0.3 / 15.3%.
- A learning curve from intermediate checkpoints (none were kept) and a reseed control (no run was repeated): the run-to-run
  spread of every number above is unknown; the "±" are sampling errors over molecules only.
- All ablations of the pre-registration (noise level, training set, PubChem arm, model size, temperature, soft constraint)
  and the arm "true fingerprint, true formula".
- The 15-nat and e^-14 to e^-27 figures are order-of-magnitude estimates (9 SMILES strings per molecule, a collision
  estimate of the spelling multiplicity on 20 molecules per set, likelihoods without the nucleus truncation). The "other
  molecule's fingerprint" control on the evaluation sets uses the neighbouring molecule of the same set, which shares
  chemistry, so it understates what the fingerprint contributes.
- The CPU arms (K 200, 100 molecules per set) have standard errors of 3 to 4 points; on SH they showed 1% against 1% where
  the full set at K 1,000 shows 16.0% against 4.0%.
- The behaviour on the hidden test's chemistry: the three sets disagree by more than a factor of ten, and which one the
  hidden novel molecules resemble is unknown. **No leaderboard estimate is given.**
- The SH baseline is reported in two variants because the production rule generator may use the answer's scaffold mates as
  parents while the learned generator never saw the scaffold; the gate was judged against the production variant, as
  pre-registered, and fails against either.
