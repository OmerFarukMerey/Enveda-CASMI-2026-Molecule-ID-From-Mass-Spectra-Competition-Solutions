# Ordering same-formula isomers (2026-10-02): the z-sum is at a flat optimum

Offline only. Cached stage outputs were re-scored and re-fused, ICEBERG was run locally to get per-spectrum scores,
nothing was submitted, pushed or committed, and no existing file was edited (the modified tracked files in
`git status` belong to other sessions). New files: `prototypes/isomer_rank.py`, `prototypes/test_isomer_rank.py`, this
report. Scripts: `scratch/isomer/` (git-ignored), outputs `scratch/isomer/out/`. Reproduce: section 9.

## 0. Bottom line

1. **No rule orders the isomers substantially better than `z(ranker) + z(ICEBERG) + z(GLACIER)`.** With a prior that
   never saw the molecules, every alternative tested (confidence gates, rank and robust combinations, fitted weights
   with interactions, per-spectrum aggregations, other similarities, size or spectra-dependent weights, top-k
   restriction, structure smoothing) moves the truth's in-group reciprocal rank by less than ±0.01 at a standard error
   of 0.005 to 0.01. A cross-fitted conditional logit lands on weights of (1.0 to 1.7, 0.8, 0.7 to 1.1): the rule in use.
2. **Everything that "wins" on the plain caches is the ranker leak.** Under the leaked ranker, the gain of any gate is
   the gain of trusting the prior more, and a uniform lower weight does it at least as well (weight 0.25: +0.03 to +0.04
   in-group; the best gate +0.01 to +0.04). Under an honest prior the hard gate ("keep the prior order unless ICEBERG
   and GLACIER agree") **loses 0.02 to 0.05 in-group and 0.02 to 0.06 in the whole pipeline**.
3. **Why gating fails although the confidence signal is real.** Agreement is right in 81 to 92% of groups and
   disagreement in 30 to 43%, as reported. But where the two models disagree the honest prior is right in only 25 to
   27%, and the z-sum of all three reaches 32 to 43%: that is where the sum adds most, not least.
4. **Per-spectrum evidence does not help either.** Local ICEBERG predictions (r = 0.99 with the Kaggle scores) show
   that the plain mean over spectra is as good as any other aggregation, that the collision energy asked from the
   model is the right one, and that merged stepped-CE predictions, precursor masking and cosine change nothing
   beyond noise. [M+Na]+ scores are weak (GLACIER close to chance), yet dropping them gains at most +0.008 in-group
   for GLACIER and nothing for ICEBERG.
5. **Prototype.** `rerank_conf` (same contract as `gl_fuse.rerank_multi`, identical by default) adds two confidence
   knobs. Recommended setting `w_disagree 0.5, agree_bonus 1.0`: whole pipeline against v4b **+0.003 to +0.006 on the
   class-2-like sets and +0.030 on the class-1-like set under an honest prior**, +0.009 to +0.019 on the plain caches,
   0 on the visible test. It is not distinguishable from existing knobs (`ICE_LAM = GL_LAM = 0.75`, the soft library
   gate). Expected leaderboard effect **+0.002 to +0.010**, below what one submission can resolve.
6. **Where the remaining loss is.** Of the in-group errors on natural products, 46 to 48% are in groups where ICEBERG
   and GLACIER disagree (33 to 35% of groups), 26 to 33% in groups without a forward-model score, 21 to 26% in groups
   where both models agree on a wrong isomer. Only new evidence reaches these: a third forward model as tie-breaker,
   adduct coverage, a better prior. Re-combining what is cached does not.

### 3.7 (g) Literature

- MetFrag (Ruttkies 2016) combines its terms as a weighted sum of scores normalised by the list maximum; the CASMI
  tool comparison (Blaženović 2017) built consensus rankings of MetFrag and CFM-ID from fixed weights or vote counts. Tested as "sum / product of
  max-normalised scores" and as Borda / RRF in 3.3: not better than z.
- COSMIC (Hoffmann 2022) derives a confidence from the score gap to the next candidates and uses it to decide
  whether to *accept* the top hit; it does not re-rank. Our margin and agreement features reproduce its premise (they
  predict correctness) and its limit (they do not improve the order).
- The ICEBERG application paper ("Neural spectral prediction for structure elucidation with tandem mass spectrometry",
  2025) ranks by entropy
  similarity averaged over collision energies, merges predictions for stepped-CE spectra and masks the precursor peak.
  The runner already averages; it predicts stepped spectra at their mean energy and keeps the precursor. Both
  differences were tested with local predictions (section 4): no gain.
- Consensus over structural neighbours (kernel smoothing of the forward score over Morgan-Tanimoto neighbours in the
  group): −0.086 to +0.002 under N, −0.052 to +0.003 under H. The models separate close isomers better than a
  smoothed score does.

## 5. What does not work, in one list

Hard gates on agreement or margin; switching between prior and forward order; rank, Borda, RRF, min-rank; clipped or
MAD z; products or sums of max-normalised scores; min or max of the two models; fitted weights with or without
interactions; per-spectrum z, ranks, median, max, min; merged stepped-CE predictions; a different collision energy;
precursor masking; explained-intensity scores; top-k restriction; structural smoothing; size or spectra-dependent
weights; [M+H]+ only for ICEBERG; adding frag for covered molecules (−0.009 ± 0.005 on H:val1, +0.007 ± 0.008 on H:valF,
+0.034 ± 0.008 on N:vis: chemistry-dependent, and −0.014 on the leaderboard).

## 7. Recommendation and expected leaderboard effect

1. **Keep the z-sum.** Do not spend submissions on combination rules, gates, aggregations or similarity variants.
2. If one submission is free for this stage, the choice is between three equivalent hedges against a prior that is
   stronger than the caches let us see: `rerank_conf(**RECOMMENDED)`, `ICE_LAM = GL_LAM = 0.75`, or the soft library gate
   (v4d). The second needs no new code. I would pick it or the soft gate over the prototype for that reason, and wire the
   prototype in only if a later, leak-free validation separates them.
3. Keep `GL_MH_ONLY`; do not add an ICEBERG equivalent.

|---|---|---|---|
| class 1, at or above the 0.9 gate | 4 to 9% (26% of class 1 on valc1) | 0 (weights are 0) | 0 |

One public-leaderboard molecule is 0.0025 to 0.008 and a paired difference has an SE near 0.01, so this cannot be
confirmed by a single submission. I put the chance that it is negative at one in four (the class-2 term is +0.003 to
+0.006 with an SE of 0.011). For comparison, the same arithmetic gives +0.001 to +0.007 for `ICE_LAM = GL_LAM = 0.75`.

The goal of 0.50 is not reachable through isomer order with today's evidence. A perfect in-group pick is worth +0.37
to +0.41 on molecules whose truth is listed; what this stage can still deliver realistically is in the error budget of
section 3.2: a third, independent forward model applied as a tie-breaker in the third of groups where ICEBERG and
GLACIER disagree (48% of the errors; adding the second model to the first was worth +0.02 in-group), forward-model
coverage for the 14 to 24% of groups without any score (26 to 33% of the errors, the coverage study), and a better
prior for the quarter of errors where both models agree on the wrong isomer.

## 8. What could not be tested, and what a Kaggle re-run would have to dump

Not tested:
- GLACIER per spectrum (collision energy, stepped spectra, aggregation): too slow locally today. Only its adduct split
  is known.
- How strong the real ranker is inside a formula group on hidden molecules. Every weight or gate question reduces to
  it. FPNet A (0.47) and the leaked ranker (0.80 to 0.94) bracket it.
- ICEBERG / GLACIER on natural products they have not seen (the val sets are optimistic for them). vis is honest but
  drug-like, and its ICEBERG-covered groups are easy (0.81 top-1).
- Truths that are generated candidates or PubChem-only: no cache contains one, so nothing here says how the knobs act
  on them (a generated truth sits in the `|gen` group, where the same rule runs).
- Negative mode and [M+NH4]+ (no forward score at all; the coverage study).
- The stage-C effect at a real `TOPN 60` dump (the caches are 80-deep replays, audit F5).

A re-run that would settle the rest should dump:
1. per-spectrum similarities for both models, `{mid: {smiles: [sim per covered spectrum]}}`, plus per molecule the list
   of (adduct, CE bucket, stepped flag) in the same order: a change of `score_molecule` in `ice_runner.py` and
   `gl_runner.py` from `mean(sims)` to the list (and `rerank_conf` already accepts it);
2. the stage caches at `TOPN 60`;
3. a base list whose ranker score is fold-safe (the local-engine prototype), so that the L prior can be replaced by
   the real thing instead of an FPNet stand-in;
4. engine-2's own library score, to gate stage C on evidence rather than on agreement;
5. forward-model scores for a set of natural products outside MassSpecGym (the 1,309 hold-out NP structures without
   GNPS / MoNA / MassBank spectra).
