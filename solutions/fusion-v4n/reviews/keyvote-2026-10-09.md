## 0. Bottom line

1. **The fusion's join key is our tautomer-canonical first block computed without the stereo step**
   (`casmi.chem.score_key` for engine 1's rows, `casmi_engine.canon_key` for engine 2's: the same three RDKit calls),
   not the SMILES string and not the full InChIKey. Different SMILES writings and different tautomers of one molecule
   therefore get one combined vote today. What does not merge is a pair the stereo step makes equal: a string with
   stereo marks and one without can land on two canonical tautomers.
2. **Such pairs exist only across the two engines, are rare, and never involve the answer.** Inside any single list
   (base 60, PubChem 25, engine 2's 40, both fusion inputs) there are 0 metric-duplicate rows in every cell, except
   one engine-2 list of the mixed regime (`dB`). Across the engines 0 to 9 lists per cell (of 150 to 380) hold a metric key
   under two fusion keys: 90 pairs in all, every one "engine 2's catalogue row with stereo marks against a pool row
   without". **The answer is the split key in 0 of 4,360 lists.** On the stored full Kaggle run: 0 of 400.
3. **Flexonafft's "one vote per metric key"** computes the stereo-free key from the SMILES for both lists, lets a key
   vote once per engine and counts ranks over distinct keys. We had the per-list part (our lists are de-duplicated by
   key before the fusion) and the cross-engine part for everything but the stereo pairs. Implemented as
   `FUSE_KEY_VOTE` (`'best'` / `'compact'`, off by default): **3 to 27 of 1,000 to 1,180 lists change, the first row
   in 0 to 2, no answer gains, 0 to 2 answers per cell lose a place** (the merged row is never the answer and moves
   up). `'best'` and `'compact'` give the same lists here. No preset.

the positive rows and the negative rows. `_reps` sorts the rows by structure, then timsTOF first, then most raw peaks,
then row number, and **keeps the first row per structure** (`drop_duplicates("p")`, line 193): 275,810 representatives
of 2,539,608 spectra in the "all" set (Kaggle log of `runs/diag-n100`). `analog_search` (line 220) scores every
representative within ±200 Da of the target (`pv.py:18`) against each query spectrum with the shifted kernel and
takes the maximum over the query spectra; `top_analogs` (line 231) takes the maximum per structure and keeps the best
`N_ANALOG` = 200 structures (`ENG_N_ANALOG`; the constant 80 in `pv.py` is overridden by `eng_runner.py:45`). The
analogs enter the rankers through `pv.rank_features` (candidate-to-analog Tanimoto weighted by similarity to the
power 3, the best analog's similarity, …) for the "all" set and through the `anapol` block for the two polarity
sets. There is no random choice: `ENG_SEEDS` are the seeds of the gradient-boosting rankers, not of the
representatives. The "first six" analogs are the v1 engine's generator parents, not an engine-2 quantity.

### 5.2 The flag

`ENG_REPS` (default 1). `eng_runner.reps_k(E, k)` is `_reps` with the first k rows per structure in the same order;
`eng_runner` replaces `E._reps` only when the value is above 1, so `casmi_engine.py` stays verbatim and the default
path is untouched. `top_analogs` already takes the maximum per structure, so an analog's similarity becomes the best
over its k representatives; the number of analogs stays 200. k 2 = the union of the reference set and a second set
(the next spectrum of every structure). Representatives: 275,545 → 534,057 (k 2) → 985,188 (k 4) of 2,482,335 rows in
the panel's purged library. `tests/test_eng_reps.py`: k 1 selects the reference's rows on a synthetic library, k 2
keeps them and adds the next by the same order.

### 5.5 Run time on Kaggle

Measured in one process on 39 mixed-regime molecules (every ninth of the 380, the first dropped because it carries the
compile time; `kb_kernel.py --limit 40`, run next to one engine process, so the ratios are the measurement, not the
seconds): the three analog searches are **99% of the CPU time of the channel stage** (2.39 of 2.41 s per molecule; the
FPNet forward pass is not in this number) and cost **1.90 times as much with k 2 and 3.21 times with k 4**
(534,511 and 986,111 representatives against 275,768).

On Kaggle (`runs/diag-n100` log, 400 molecules): the channel stage took 1,696 s (418 s → 2,114 s), building the
three representative sets 18 s, engine 2 as a whole 2,782 s. **k 2: about +1,500 s (25 minutes), so about 4 h 45 min
instead of 4 h 20 min of the 9 h; k 4: about +3,700 s (62 minutes).** These are upper estimates in so far as Kaggle's
channel stage also contains the FPNet forward pass, which does not grow. Memory on Kaggle was not measured; the
representatives' cleaned peaks are stored once more per added set.

**Verdict for B: nothing survives.** The better analogs are real and reproducible from train; with the rankers as
they are fitted they do not move the answer, they reshuffle the list.
