## 0. Bottom line

1. **No calibration of the regime predicts held-out submissions better than "no change".** Five type weights fitted to
   the 13 replayable submissions: fit error 0.0094, leave-one-out error 0.0120, "no change" 0.0122 (skill +0.04). The
   weights are not identified: within one unit of leaderboard noise `c1` can be 0 to 0.50 and `c2` 0 to 0.25. The one
   quantity the data fix is the weight of PubChem-only answers: **0.065 (0.045 to 0.085)**, i.e. a local gain on `dBr`
   reaches the public leaderboard at about one fifteenth. That single weight has leave-one-out error 0.0108 (skill
   +0.22): it says "the join is worth about +0.011" and nothing else.
2. **Neither large drop is reproduced by any hypothesis.** The regime expects +0.001 for N 100 → 200 (leaderboard
   −0.025) and +0.001 for the gate at 0.8 (−0.015). Counting the regime's own per-molecule spread and the draw of 130
   public molecules, the two results are about 3 to 5.6 standard errors away from every plausible weight vector. It is not an
   unlucky draw *of the population the regime describes*; a mechanism is missing.
3. Per hypothesis (one knob each; fitted step for N 200 / gate 0.8, wanted −0.025 / −0.015):
   (a) engine-2 weaker or absent on pool answers: 0.000 to +0.002 / −0.001 to +0.001. **Not preferred.**
   (b) forward weights at zero against real ICEBERG: +0.001 / +0.001 in both; but that test is void for depth, because
   locally no joined row beyond PubChem rank 25 has a forward score. With synthetic forward models that score every
   joined row, a weak model (truth 1 sd above decoys) gives `c2` −0.012 ± 0.005 for N 100 → 200 after the fusion and
   −0.003 fitted: **the right sign, a tenth of the size.**
   (c) less confident half of the pool answers: +0.001 to +0.002 / +0.001. **Opposite of what was assumed**: it is the
   *confident* half that loses at N 200 (−0.012 ± 0.007, engine-2 off −0.018 ± 0.008).
   (d) natural-product structures, large or small windows, many same-formula rows: 0.000 to +0.001 / down to −0.009 only
   together with engine-2 off and a `c1` weight of 0.45.
   (e, added) class-1 lists have a lower `lib_max` on the hidden test, so the gate lets them through, and engine-2 does
   not repair them: 0.000 / **−0.006**; held out, it puts the gate-0.8 submission at +0.006 against v4b (observed
   +0.007; the pure mixture +0.014). This is the only hypothesis that moves one of the two drops, it needs two
   assumptions, and it says nothing about N 200.
4. **What would have to be true for N 200**: a loss of about 0.03 per molecule averaged over all hidden molecules from
   rows 101 to 200. The largest loss any local sub-population shows for that step is −0.025 (pool answers that were
   first, engine-2 off) and that population cannot also produce the +0.011 and +0.011 of the first two steps. Three
   causes the regime cannot contain remain (section 4); one label-free check is done: the forward-model job count does
   not grow from N 100 to N 200 (57.4 → 57.7 per molecule), so a time-budget cut in ICEBERG / GLACIER is not it.
5. **List assembly** (N 100 and 200, gate 0.7; judged on exposure counts, since there is no calibration). Best rule:
   **joined rows only below the pool's first candidate when the unjoined list is confident (top-score margin > 1.25
   sd), free otherwise**. It keeps 92 to 94% of the `dBr` gain (−0.015 ± 0.004 at N 100, 0 / 15) and removes about two
   thirds of the pool answers that lose the first place on the list before the engine-2 fusion (17 → 6 of 248 at N 100,
   24 → 9 at N 200; `c2` +0.018 ± 0.005, 11 / 0, and +0.024 ± 0.006, 15 / 0). After the local engine-2 fusion it changes
   nothing at N 100 (0 / 0) and one list at N 200. "At most k joined rows above" for k ≥ 1 and "rank no worse than 2 or
   3" keep 82 to 94% and save none of the lost first places; alternation keeps half and adds losses; "never first"
   removes three quarters and keeps half.

## 5. The calibration as a module

`prototypes/lb_calibration.py` (numpy only): `type_stats`, `fit_weights` (w ≥ 0, Σ w ≤ 1, projected gradient),
`weight_set` (all grid vectors within a tolerance of the best fit), `leave_one_out`, `calibrate` → `Calibration`
(weights, weight set, fitted, leave-one-out table, `skill` = 1 − leave-one-out SSE / "no change" SSE), `predict(cal,
rr_cfg, rr_base)` → difference to the base with an interval, `public_z`.

```python
cal = LC.calibrate(D, y, names, types)            # D: submissions x types (mean paired difference to v4b), y: LB - 0.409
p = LC.predict(cal, {'c2': {mid: rr}, 'dBr': {...}}, {'c2': {...}, 'dBr': {...}})
p['delta'], p['lo'], p['hi'], p['reliable']       # interval never narrower than the leave-one-out RMSE
```

The interval combines the local error, the public draw and the spread over the equally fitting weight vectors, and
is never narrower than the calibration's leave-one-out RMSE (±0.012 today); `reliable` is False while the skill is
below 0.25. The fitted design matrix is stored in `D:\casmi-data\lbcal\calibration_M5.json`. On today's 13 submissions
(table of section 2) `reliable` is False for both the five-weight and the one-weight calibration. Use it as: "a change
that acts on PubChem-only answers is worth about 0.065 × its local `dBr` difference, ± 0.012; for anything that
changes how many outside rows compete with pool answers, the module has no skill."

## 6. List-assembly rules after the join

`t4_rules.py`, `t7_exposure_tau.py --part B`, `t8_best.py`. The rule re-orders the joined base list before the fusion
(`join_protect.protect`): public ranker, gate 0.7, 263 joined `c2` / `dB` lists and 21 `c1` lists. In the joined list a
joined row is on top of 13% (N 100) and 16% (N 200) of the `c2` lists and 60 to 65% of the `dB` lists; the unjoined
top candidate is the first pool row of the joined list in 81 to 87% of the pool lists.

- `cap k`: at most k joined rows above the first pool row (k = 0: a joined row is never first).
- `pin r`: the unjoined list's top candidate keeps a rank no worse than r.
- `alt m`: the first m pool rows and the first m joined rows alternate, pool row first.
- `conf τ`: `cap 0` when the pool rows' top-score margin exceeds τ sd, free otherwise; margin of the non-joined rows
  of the joined list ("joined"), or of the unjoined list ("unjoined", needs a second engine pass).

Reading.
- **"At most k above" with k ≥ 1 and "rank no worse than 2 or 3" do not protect the first place**, by construction:
  they turn a fall to rank 3 or 4 into a fall to rank 2 (11 to 19% of the lost reciprocal rank) and still cost 6 to
  18% of the `dBr` gain. A rule that should guard a first place has to decide who is first.
- **Alternation is the worst**: it forces joined rows into positions 2, 4, 6 of every list, which costs the pool
  answers at ranks 2 to 5 after the fusion (lost first places 4 → 12) and halves the `dBr` gain.
- **"Never first" (`cap 0`)** removes 72 to 76% of the exposure and keeps half of the gain: the 60 to 65% of `dB`
  lists whose joined row was rightly first are demoted with it.
- **The confidence gate separates the two**: in a `dB` list the pool rows have no clear leader (median margin 0.5
  sd), in a `c2` list they do (1.2 sd). The margin of the unjoined list is the better feature, because the joined
  rows change the list-relative features of the pool rows: at equal cost (8 to 15 `dB` lists) it saves 11 to 15 `c2`
  first places instead of 6. The threshold is flat from 1.0 to 1.75; 1.25 was read off these 263 lists, on 18 to 25
  exposed lists, so the exact value carries no weight.
- **After the local engine-2 fusion the rule does almost nothing** (0 / 0 at N 100): its whole case rests on the
  premise that the hidden test does not repair what the local engine-2 repairs. Under any mixture and the local
  fusion its net is −0.001 to −0.003; on the pre-fusion list with `c2` 0.36 and `dBr` 0.065 it is +0.005.
- It does not touch the gate: class-1 lists above 0.7 stay unjoined. For the gate at 0.8 the same rule would apply to
  the newly joined lists; not evaluated beyond the counts of section 4 (3 class-1 first places lost at 0.8, 8 at 0.9).
