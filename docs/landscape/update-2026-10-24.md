## 3. What the gains share [I]

1. **Repeated winners are evidence or candidate coverage for molecules that had none.** N1A / N1 challenger (GLACIER evidence
   for molecules without an [M+H]+ spectrum, and joint use where only ICEBERG decided before), E006's ICEBERG fallback where
   GLACIER is missing, PubChemLite candidates for engine 2, the PubChem-only channel itself. Each was positive on 3 or more
   bases. The challenger's +0.008 appears six times in both directions. It is probably the same public molecule each time, so
   it shows the gain is stable across bases, not that it is large.
2. **One fitted threshold also won repeatedly**: rel 150, sharp on both sides (100: −0.002, 160 to 200: −0.005 to −0.006).
3. **Repeated losers change how existing evidence is weighed or ordered.**
   - Simulator weights and filters, engine-2 capacity (depth, seeds, LambdaRank), fusion α / KRR, popularity μ: 0 or negative,
     and the sign flips across bases.
   - Swapping in other fingerprint networks: −0.005 to −0.012 every time.
   - Hedges, shields and extra gates: 0.000.
   - Generating more candidates or promoting generated ones: −0.008 to 0.000.
4. **So the 0.465 base is where re-tuning is exhausted on the public part.** Its own +0.001 is a correctness fix of the gate
   (gate on the candidate that actually leads). The next gain should come from a new evidence source for a subset that
   currently lacks one. A new weighting will not do it.
