## 2. What the library is

From GNPS's own pages:

- GNPS documentation, library list (`ccms-ucsd.github.io/GNPSDocumentation/gnpslibraries/`): "GNPS Matches to
  NIST14: 5,763 High confidence matches to NIST14 MS/MS library spectra."
- GNPS2 library page (`external.gnps2.org/gnpslibrary`): `GNPS-NIST14-MATCHES`, library type "GNPS", downloads in
  MGF / MSP / JSON; all libraries last fully exported 2026-03-29.
- Licence, GNPS documentation (the same paragraph on the batch-upload, curation and download pages): "All GNPS
  Reference spectra contributed directly to GNPS by default will have the CC0 license. Third party libraries
  imported may not conform to the CC BY license and should be verified by users." **No licence is stated for this
  library specifically** on the pages read. The library is typed "GNPS" (not an imported third-party library) and
  its records were submitted by a GNPS account, so the CC0 default is the natural reading, but that is my reading,
  not a statement by GNPS. The file itself has no licence field.

From the file (5,763 records):

- Every record has `SUBMITUSER=mwang87`, `LIBRARYQUALITY=3` (bronze) and a `FILENAME` inside a public MassIVE
  dataset (238 different `MSV…` datasets; the largest contributes 223 records). `NAME` is "Spectral Match to
  <compound> from NIST14 <adduct>" for all but 25 records: 17 say "from METLIN", 3 "Incorrect annotation", 5 a
  plain name.
- So the spectra are **measurements by GNPS users in public datasets, not NIST's spectra**; what comes from NIST is
  the label (compound identity and adduct), assigned because the spectrum matched a NIST14 reference. This confirms
  the description in the question the host answered. Not verified: how the matching was done, or its error rate.
- `SOURCE_INSTRUMENT` values (ESI-qTof 2,849; ESI-QQQ 1,226; ESI-Ion Trap 950; …) may describe the matched
  reference record instead of the user's instrument; the file does not say which.

## 4. What it would add

Usable subset = has a key, not flagged "Incorrect annotation", one of the ten test adducts with matching polarity,
neutral CHNOPS + halogen structure, precursor within 20 ppm of the annotated structure and adduct:
**4,484 spectra, 1,375 structures.**

- **New structures: 10 in the file, 8 in the usable subset** (28 spectra): N-acetylneuraminic acid, biliverdin,
  bilirubin (the METLIN-labelled one), androstenedione, testosterone, boldenone, lovastatin hydroxy acid, auramine
  O. Dropped: vitamin B12 (doubly charged, 1,272 Da) and ferrioxamine (iron complex; the file's only new COCONUT
  row). Of the 8: **0 in our candidate pool, 0 COCONUT rows, 8 in the PubChem tier**, 1 also in engine 2's
  ChEBI / LIPID MAPS set; all 8 are in the hidden set's range (150 to 1,250 Da, CHNOPS + halogens). They are
  textbook primary metabolites, steroids and a dye, not low-observation natural products. Why train lacks them
  (testosterone, for one) was not investigated.
- Polarity: 5,279 positive, 484 negative. Adducts: [M+H]+ 3,500, [M-H2O+H]+ 659, [M-H]- 403, [M+Na]+ 262, [M+NH4]+
  72, others; 788 records carry an adduct outside the test's ten (M+2H, 2M+H, M+H-NH3, in-source fragments).
- Instruments as labelled: QTOF 3,010, QqQ 1,377, ion trap 1,228, HCD / FT 144. **No timsTOF.** No collision
  energy field.
- Peaks: 57 raw peaks in the median (10% of spectra have 16 or fewer, 10% have 267 or more); 42 after the v1
  cache's cleaning; 29 spectra keep fewer than 5.
- Mass convention: the precursor is given to 3 decimals in 4,742 records, 2 in 874, 1 in 147. Error against the
  annotated structure and adduct (electron handled correctly), 4,781 records: positive median −0.47 ppm (5% to 95%:
  −10.9 to +16.4), negative +0.41 ppm (−18.4 to +8.1); 3,513 within 5 ppm, 604 at 5 to 10, 393 at 10 to 20, 236 at
  20 to 50, 35 beyond (33 of them off by more than 0.5 Da, i.e. a wrong adduct or structure). Looser than train's
  other public libraries (`reviews/extlib-2026-10-09.md`: ±2 ppm), no systematic offset.

## 5. Does it help where we need it? No.

Reading: no molecule gains a library hit; no new structure is the best analog of any molecule. **Every rise in
analog similarity comes from a row that is in `train.parquet` already.** That is the effect the extlib study
described in its section 4.3 (engine 2 keeps one representative spectrum per structure, and a second spectrum of
the same structure sometimes matches better); it needs no external file.

**Engine-level test (the e006u replay with the library added): not run.** There is nothing to carry: 8 new
structures outside the candidate pool and 139 extra spectra of structures train has. For the record, where a switch
would go: the baseline reads `train.parquet` from the competition folder (the stage has a folder override,
`OURS_E006_COMP`, described in `nb/embed/e006_stage.py`), and engine 2 builds library and index from that file in
`casmi_engine.load_library(train_path)`; an environment variable naming an extra parquet in train's schema,
concatenated there, default unset, would be the smallest change. Not written.

**The real question, bounded.** Could a hidden answer be in this library? For 1,498 of its 1,508 structures the
answer is "then it is in train too", and for 4,924 of 5,077 spectra the very row is. The remainder that looks like
the hidden population is 8 structures, none a natural product in our pool. Local sets cannot measure hidden-set
coverage; the bound is that 8 structures is nothing against a pool of 436,515 COCONUT rows.
