# Enveda CASMI 2026: research strategy

**Status:** research and data inventory, 29 September 2026. No model has been trained or submitted. This is a decision plan, not a claim of measured model performance. Detailed EDA is the next piece of work.

## 1. Objective and constraints

Predict the 2D structure of each unknown molecule from all its LC-MS/MS spectra. The output is **one ranked list of up to 25 SMILES per `molecule_id`**, not one prediction per spectrum. The competition scores **mean reciprocal rank at 25 (MRR@25)** across molecules: a first correct hit at rank `r ≤ 25` scores `1/r`; no hit scores zero. The [official metric](https://www.kaggle.com/code/metric/casmi-mean-reciprocal-rank) canonicalizes tautomers with RDKit 2026.03.3, then compares the first 14 characters of the InChIKey. Stereochemical and tautomer differences therefore do not necessarily constitute different answers. See the [competition overview](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/overview).

| Operational requirement | Consequence for the research plan |
| --- | --- |
| `submission.csv` has exactly `molecule_id,smiles`; every ID appears once; 1–25 semicolon-delimited guesses per row, without nulls | Build a molecule-level aggregation, connectivity-level deduplication and output validation step. The sample's repeated `CCO` is a format example, **not** a prediction baseline. |
| Submission must be generated by a Kaggle Notebook, with internet disabled and CPU/GPU run time ≤9 hours | Anything needed at inference must be available offline in the Notebook environment. Measure full inference time and artifact size before pursuing large candidate databases or ensembles. |
| At most five submissions per day; two may be selected as final | Use local held-out validation to choose experiments; do not tune repeatedly to the public leaderboard. |
| Entry and team-merger deadline: 7 December 2026; final submission: 14 December 2026, 23:59 UTC | Have a reproducible, valid Notebook submission before the final phase; deadlines may be updated by the organizers. |
| Competition data: CC BY-NC 4.0, non-commercial competition/research use; team size ≤5 | Keep raw competition data out of git and inaccessible to non-participants. Check external dataset/model availability and licenses before depending on them. |

The [rules](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/rules) additionally restrict private sharing of competition code outside the team and require winning solutions to supply reproducible training/inference code and an appropriate open-source license. The repository's MIT license does **not** relicense the competition data.

## 2. What we know about the data

The table distinguishes **measured facts** from the organizer's description. All local figures refer to files downloaded on 29 September 2026, not to the hidden scoring set. The [dataset page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/data) describes the provenance, acquisition process and novelty classes.

| File | Local observation | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `train.parquet` | 3,033,286,496 bytes; **2,539,608 spectra**, **275,810 distinct non-null `inchikey14`**; 18 top-level columns; 21 row groups | Labeled spectra from overlapping libraries. One structure can have many spectra, and a structure can occur in multiple libraries. |
| `test.parquet` | 4,848,729 bytes; **1,213 spectra**, **400 `molecule_id` values**, 1–9 spectra per molecule; 12 columns; all `instrument_type = timsTOF` | **Placeholder only.** The organizers say these rows are examples from the training set; a hidden test set replaces them at Notebook rerun. Do not infer the true class mix, chemistry or achievable score from this file. |
| `sample_submission.csv` | 43,619 bytes; 400 unique IDs, exactly matching the downloaded placeholder test IDs | Use for format/ID checks only; its 25 copies of `CCO` per row carry no chemical information. |

**Schema correction:** despite the dataset page's shorthand that training has the test columns plus labels, `train.parquet` has **no `molecule_id` or `spectrum_id`**. Ten columns are shared; training supplies `normalized_smiles`, `inchikey14`, `ingest_lib`, `molecular_formula`, `precursor_error_ppm` and other label/provenance columns. Group training structures by `inchikey14`; do **not** assume its rows can be grouped into the same genuine multi-spectrum acquisition that the test's `molecule_id` identifies. Avoid manufacturing a query by pooling every spectrum of a structure across unrelated instruments and libraries.

**Acquisition shift:** the real test is described as approximately 400 molecules and 1,500 timsTOF spectra, with roughly 1–16 measurements per molecule and ten possible adducts. Its hidden molecules span a wider mass range than the downloaded placeholder. The placeholder has 959 `[M+H]+`, 193 `[M-H]-`, 61 other spectra; its aligned peak arrays have 4–3,259 peaks (median row: 230) and no nulls in the 12 columns. These facts check parsing; **they are not a target distribution**. Test spectra have light noise filtering; training sources have heterogeneous instrument names, collision-energy conventions and missing raw base-peak intensity. Never treat a missing `base_peak_intensity` as a low-intensity measurement. The supplied `collision_energy_ev` is the organizer's approximate common-energy representation, not a guarantee of matched fragmentation across instruments. [Dataset page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra/data)

**Relevant training sources (local counts):** `enveda-180` 1,153,785 (same instrument family, largely synthetic chemistry); `pluskal_ms2` 527,581; `riken` 347,171; `gnps` 220,849; `enveda-np-examples` **1,184** (natural products measured on the closest published acquisition pipeline). The dataset page lists 1,151 spectra for `enveda-np-examples`; use the actual downloaded file for calculations. RIKEN/GNPS are more natural-product-focused, but their acquisition conditions differ. Counts alone do not establish reference coverage of the hidden set.

**Three hidden novelty classes** as defined by the organizers:

1. **Public reference spectra exist:** spectral-library retrieval can directly recover the structure.
2. **Known structure, no public reference spectra:** PubChem/COCONUT-style structure retrieval is possible, but reference-spectrum lookup alone cannot solve it.
3. **Novel structure absent from PubChem:** a closed PubChem candidate set cannot contain the answer; de novo or structure-generating approaches are needed for genuine coverage.

Their proportions and molecule-level labels are withheld. Any offline class split is a **proxy**, not a verified reconstruction of the private leaderboard.

## 3. Validation first: answer the right question

**Primary question:** for each novelty regime, does a candidate generator place the correct connectivity within 25 guesses, and can a ranker move it towards rank one? A single MRR from a random spectrum split answers neither question.

1. **Fix the scoring contract.** Match the official RDKit tautomer normalization and InChIKey14 comparison; compute MRR@25 per molecule, first correct rank, candidate recall@25, top-1 accuracy, and MRR conditional on a correct candidate being present. Deduplicate candidates by normalized connectivity before ranking. Report all metrics per regime and by acquisition/library where the proxy supports it. Keep molecule IDs and fold assignments fixed between experiments.
2. **Create a class-1 proxy: independent reference measurement.** Reserve query spectra from one source and retain reference spectra of the same connectivity from another source. Prefer a held-out timsTOF/natural-product slice when enough overlapping structures exist. Block exact and near-duplicate measurements across the boundary; disjoint source labels alone do not prove independence. Since train lacks acquisition-group IDs, first evaluate single-spectrum queries; aggregate only spectra whose common acquisition can be established, and label any constructed multi-spectrum proxy as such.
3. **Create a class-2 proxy: unseen spectrum, reachable structure.** Hold out **all spectra of each query InChIKey14 across every training and external reference library** from spectral training and indexing. Retain a structure in a fixed, licensed candidate catalog only if its catalog membership is independently verified. Otherwise report the result as an unseen-structure retrieval proxy, not an authentic class-2 estimate.
4. **Create a class-3 stress test: answer unavailable to catalog lookup.** Hold out all query structures from spectral training, supervised generative-model training **and** the candidate index used by the experiment. Audit pretrained model provenance where feasible; an unknown pretraining corpus limits novelty claims. This stress test measures whether a method can propose withheld structures; it does not prove they are novel to PubChem. Verify database membership against a frozen snapshot before calling anything genuinely novel.
5. **Protect the boundary.** Split by InChIKey14 before supervised feature fitting, model selection and any out-of-fold stacking. Record source overlaps, structure overlaps, possible isomer/tautomer collisions, precursor/adduct distributions and candidate-catalog membership. Freeze folds, database versions, cleaning rules and metric code before comparing experiments; do not use the visible placeholder test to choose thresholds. Report per-class proxy results rather than weighting them into an assumed hidden-class mix. For small slices, show molecule counts and uncertainty alongside scores.

**Exit criterion for validation:** reproducible fold and catalog manifests; no unintended held-out structure in a class-2/3 spectral reference index or supervised model training set; a class-1 query independent of its retained reference; exact official metric semantics; per-proxy scores with sample counts. If a clean class-1 multi-spectrum grouping cannot be derived, keep the single-spectrum proxy and state the limitation instead of synthesizing favorable groups.

## 4. Experiment sequence and decision gates

Experiments are ordered by **what can improve MRR@25** while respecting the offline Notebook limit. Record the same validation slices, per-molecule predictions, candidate recall and runtime for each experiment. Do not replace a working baseline on the strength of a public score alone.

| Stage | Question and experiment | Move forward only when... |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A. Data audit and metric** | Profile source overlap, formula/mass range, adduct, polarity, instrument, collision energy, peak count, missing values and `precursor_error_ppm` by source. Check candidate identity and training labels under official connectivity normalization. Establish the validation proxies above. | The split and scoring checks pass, plus the source slices used for validation have documented coverage. No model comparison is meaningful before this. |
| **B. Reference retrieval** | Gate by the **observed adduct** and mass tolerance; compare peak filtering/intensity transforms, standard versus modified spectral cosine, and collision-energy/source-aware matching. Aggregate evidence only for actual spectra sharing one `molecule_id` at inference. Rank distinct connectivity keys, not individual reference spectra. | Class-1 candidate recall@25 and MRR improve over the simplest mass-gated spectral search on fixed folds without violating inference time. Retain the simple method if complexity adds no reproducible gain. |
| **C. Structure candidates** | Audit an openly accessible, license-compatible frozen PubChem/COCONUT catalog. Generate neutral-mass/formula-compatible candidates for supported adducts and assess natural-product priors and fragment/neutral-loss evidence. Evaluate catalog coverage **before** reranking. | Class-2 candidate recall@25 increases on independently verified catalog members within storage/runtime limits. If the correct answer is absent, tune retrieval rather than the ranker. |
| **D. Reranking and fusion** | Rank the union of reference and catalog candidates using mass error, spectral evidence, precursor/adduct/formula compatibility and multi-spectrum consistency. Compare with simple rank/score fusion first; use learned models with out-of-fold features and structure-disjoint validation only if needed. | MRR@25 improves on the same folds without reducing recall or breaking Notebook limits. Inspect per-class regressions; do not rely on an unknown class mix. |
| **E. Novel-structure research** | Evaluate formula/fragment-guided generation or suitably licensed public spectrum-to-structure models on the class-3 stress test. Track valid, distinct generated connectivities and correct-structure recall@25, not only SMILES validity. | Generation adds withheld answers that catalog lookup cannot supply, and can be integrated into the 25-slot ranking under the runtime/licensing constraints. Otherwise retain the tested retrieval pipeline. |
| **F. Submission rehearsal** | Package required indexes/models as permitted offline inputs and run the entire Kaggle Notebook with internet disabled on the provided placeholder. Validate schema, every ID, 1–25 parseable/unique-connectivity guesses and elapsed run time. | A committed Notebook produces `submission.csv` within nine hours in Kaggle. The placeholder run proves **format and execution**, not hidden-test accuracy. |

**Diagnostic decision rule:** low candidate recall calls for broader/better generation; high recall with low MRR calls for ranking work. A model with neither a fixed validation win nor a feasible offline path is not a priority. External dataset snapshots and large pretrained models require an explicit accessibility, license and resource check before integration.

## 5. Execution and reproducibility

- **Data location:** `data/raw/` is ignored by git. Competition data must not be added to this repository or shared with non-participants. A participant with accepted Kaggle rules can retrieve the three files with `uvx --from kaggle kaggle competitions download enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra -f <filename> -p data/raw`, substituting `train.parquet`, `test.parquet` and `sample_submission.csv`. Preserve source/version information for any additional dataset without committing its raw contents.
- **Artifacts to retain when implementation starts:** data/schema checks, immutable fold definitions and catalog snapshot IDs; metric version; source-specific preprocessing settings; candidate lists with connectivity keys and provenance; out-of-fold results; resource/runtime logs; Notebook dependency versions and instructions to rebuild its offline inputs. Store derived artifacts only where the competition rules permit.
- **Team workstreams:** validation/data audit; spectral retrieval; chemistry/candidate catalogs; ranking and Kaggle packaging. Workstreams share the **same folds and metric**. Owners and compute allocation should be agreed by the team; this document assigns neither people nor unmeasured performance targets.
- **Schedule:** establish valid validation and a simple working offline Notebook first; use subsequent cycles for candidate coverage and ranking; leave time before 7 December for team/entry administration and before 14 December for a fully reproducible final Notebook run. Recheck the live competition timeline before submitting.
